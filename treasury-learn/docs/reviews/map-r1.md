## Critical errors (must fix)

**1. FX hedge bands contradict the canonical policy bands — and produce a wrong hedge decision.**
- `content/pages/map/fx.mdx`, exposure table: "Policy band | 60–90% | 30–60% | 30–60% | 30–60%" and "Q4 is below the band: Daniel needs to sell about EUR 6m forward for Q4 this month"; Hedge ratios section: "Helvetic's policy — 60–90% of next-quarter net EUR inflows, 30–60% for quarters 2–4". Repeated in `content/glossary/map.yaml` (`layered-hedging` example: "60–90% of the next quarter's net EUR inflows and 30–60% of quarters 2–4").
- Why wrong: AUTHORING canonical facts and the gold-standard workflows specify graded bands **Q+1 60–90%, Q+2 40–70%, Q+3 25–55%, Q+4 0–40%** (`content/workflows/fx-hedging.yaml` line 177, `fx-exposure-management.yaml` lines 198–200, `content/compare/wf-risk.yaml` line 8). Under the canonical Q+4 band of 0–40%, the table's Q4 ratio of 14% is **inside** the band, so "Daniel needs to sell about EUR 6m forward for Q4" is an incorrect action — a founder reading this learns the wrong decision rule.
- Fix direction: use the canonical four bands in the table and both texts; recompute the narrative (Q1 74% fine; Q2 50% fine; Q3 33% fine at 25–55%; Q4 14% fine at 0–40% — or make Q4 genuinely below band with a different number), and align `layered-hedging` in the glossary.

## Missing concepts

- **Intercompany netting.** GlobalChem's canonical structure includes "monthly multilateral netting", and the map pages reference `netting` four times (cash-management, fx natural hedging, intercompany funding), but no page explains what netting is, who runs it, what the settlement cycle is, or why it cuts FX volume. For a founder this is one of the most distinctive large-company practices. A short block (or at least 2–3 sentences with the ~30-currency example) in cash-management or fx would close the gap.
- **Commodity price risk.** `risk.mdx` enumerates "the six risks" and stops there. GlobalChem is a specialty-chemicals group whose P&L is squeezed between feedstock costs and prices; a founder interviewing a chemicals treasurer will hear about commodity hedging and wonder why the manual never mentions it. One sentence ("outside this map's scope, but real at GlobalChem…") would inoculate against that.
- **Hedge accounting for beginners.** `fx.mdx` treats hedge accounting as one line plus one confusion callout. The distinction that actually bites mid-market groups — cash-flow hedge of forecast sales vs economic/balance-sheet hedge, and that Swiss GAAP FER and IFRS 9 differ in burden — deserves its own short callout, especially given issue 5 above.
- **What happens when a bank relationship breaks** (declined RCF participation, a bank leaving the syndicate). `banking.mdx` hints at it ("likely to leave at the 2027 refinancing") but the failure mode — re-allocating commitments, replacing cash management services — is only implicit.
- Minor: **in-house bank** is used as a load-bearing concept (cash-management, payments) but only exists as a glossary tooltip; a 2-sentence gloss in cash-management would help.

## Unrealistic workflow descriptions

Very few — this is the module's strength. Almost every section answers "what does the person actually do" (Lea proposes a EUR 3.8m transfer, Daniel approves before the 14:00 cut-off; Petra removes two disputed invoices and uploads pain.001 via EBICS; the trigger lists, cut-offs, call-backs and holiday-cover failures are exactly what practitioners describe). Two spots where reality slips:

- `payments.mdx`, "A CHF 4m payment request at Helvetic": "Thomas adds the second signature on his phone" — realistic, but the exemplar workflow shows that for single payments above CHF 2m the **CEO** is also required and the payment waited for a morning signature; the map version makes the control look lighter than the manual's own standard (see critical error 4).
- `cash-management.mdx` interview prompt: "Which account is always the problem — late statement, missing data, **a bank in another time zone?**" — for a Swiss group the canonical clarification is that Asian statements arrive *early* and a missing Chinese statement is a connectivity/bank problem, not a time-zone one. The question trains the reader's intuition the wrong way; ask instead "which statement never arrives automatically, and what is the real reason?"

## Verdict: REVISE

The pedagogy, workflow realism and sourcing discipline are genuinely strong — the "plain / professional / example / ask" architecture, the confusion callouts and the named-actor workflows would survive an interview with a practising treasurer, and the currentness of the payment/rate/regulatory layer (VoP Oct 2025, SIC IP, Swift EOC, EBICS 3.0 Nov 2026, safe-harbour 2026, SNB Sept 2026) is exemplary. What blocks PASS is a dense cluster of internal-consistency errors against the canonical facts and gold-standard workflows: wrong FX hedge bands (with a wrong resulting decision), two different Helvetic balance sheets, a wrong RCF maturity/split/margin, a wrong signing threshold, IFRS 9 where FER applies, and a moved 13-week low point. These are exactly the details an interviewing founder would quote back at a treasurer. Fix the seven critical items (most are mechanical substitution against `content/workflows/*.yaml` and the AUTHORING canonical block), re-check the two UNVERIFIED statistics, and this module is close to gold-standard.
