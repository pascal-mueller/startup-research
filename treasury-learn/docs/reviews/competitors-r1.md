## Missing concepts

- **Commercial model / pricing.** For a startup-lens section there is nothing on how these products are sold and priced (subscription vs module vs transaction, implementation fees, bank-resold/OEM deals, contract length, who signs). Not fabricating prices is correct — but the method page should give a *research recipe* ("ask interviewees what they pay, when it renews, who owns the budget; ask vendors for list-price structure") and the interview scripts should include a cost/contract question. Today none of the nine interview callouts asks about money.
- **Vendor scale.** The map sizes the *customer* but never the *vendor*. A founder cannot tell a 100-person Embat from a 5,000-person FIS. At least employee/revenue ranges (sourced or explicitly "not researched") per entry.
- **Investment portals are under-covered relative to the workflow material.** `content/workflows/surplus-cash-investment.yaml` names ICD as the example portal; the `investments` category has exactly one entry (TreasurySpring). Add ICD (and note banks' own portals), or say why not.
- **Supply-chain finance incumbents.** Working capital lists Taulia and C2FO only; PrimeRevenue (and bank programmes generally) is the other name a treasurer will mention. Absent and not in the omissions list.
- **Implementers/partners.** Treasury consultants, SIs and bank resellers shape every selection ("often with consultants" appears once). The method page warns about analyst lists and bank white-labelling but the landscape has no entry/row for this channel.
- **The brief's "Predica" and "HedgeGuard" are absent (probably correctly).** My searches found **HedgeGuard** as buy-side trading/portfolio software for hedge funds and crypto asset managers (Paris/Dublin — sourceforge/goodfirms/Chartis listings), and **Predica** as an IT consultancy (Predica Inc., Microsoft-focused; appears in SoftwareOne's 2025 accounts) or Crédit Agricole's insurance arm — neither as a corporate treasury product. **UNVERIFIED (R1)** as treasury competitors; if the omission is deliberate, one line in "Missing from this landscape" saying "names often raised but out of scope: buy-side systems (e.g. HedgeGuard), consultancies/developers" would save the reader a dead end.
- **Market sizing.** There are no market-size or share claims anywhere (so nothing mis-cited — good), but a founder will want at least a sourced sense of segment sizes/install bases or an explicit "this section does not size the market; here is how to".

## Unrealistic workflow descriptions

Overall the step-mapping is one of the section's strengths (the forecast-step table on `/competitors/cash-forecasting`, the FX chain on `/competitors/fx-hedging`, "two layers" on connectivity, and "who owns this inside the customer" on working capital all match `content/workflows/*.yaml`). Weak spots:

- **No day-level product reality.** Everything is at the level of "what the product type covers". Nothing shows a person's morning *inside* a product: e.g. Helvetic's 08:00 position built in Excel+EBICS today vs the same task in an API-first platform vs in Kyriba — what is clicked, what is downloaded, what still fails. For "what these products actually do in a treasury's week", the section teaches the map but not the terrain.
- `tms.mdx` "Implementation: Months… vs Usually shorter (vendor claim)" is fine, but the section never says *what* takes the months (bank mandates and testing, static data, ERP interfaces are mentioned in the connectivity page but not tied to the buying story).
- The interview questions, by contrast, are excellent and behaviourally realistic ("What do you still do by hand between 08:00 and 10:00?", "How many of your banks were live on day one?").

## Verdict: REVISE

The bones are good: the four-system-types model, the evidence-badging discipline, the method page's honesty about its own limits, and the interview-question craft are all above bar, and the section correctly refuses to fabricate pricing, features or customer counts. But a competitive-research section is only as good as its ownership facts, and three of those are wrong in ways a five-minute search catches (Cashforce→TIS not Nomentia; Kantox→100% BNP Paribas; Agicap's round is 2024 not 2022), one statistic is cited against a source that expressly disowned it (AFP "46%"), and the AI page's "nothing could be confirmed" is false for Palm and Nilus — the two most decision-relevant names for a founder. Fix the critical errors, re-run the verification pass the stream notes already plan (the badges make that cheap), and add the commercial-model lens; then this section earns the trust it is otherwise careful to ask for.
