## Critical errors (must fix)

1. **Glossary example teaches a wrong statement model.** `wf-pay.yaml`, `camt-054` example: *"Alpine's camt.053 shows one debit of CHF 1.9m for the Tuesday run; the camt.054 lists the 83 executed payments…"*. The Tuesday run pays from **three accounts in three currencies** (payment-processing worked example: CHF 1,016,400 at the cantonal bank, EUR 741,900 at the cantonal bank, USD 236,500 at the large Swiss bank). A camt.053 is **per account, in the account's currency** — there is no "one debit of CHF 1.9m"; there are three debits (CHF 1.016m, EUR 741.9k, USD 236.5k), and CHF 1.9m is only the treasury-style CHF-equivalent sum. As written, the example silently teaches that a multi-account run produces one consolidated bank debit. Fix: "the camt.053 of the CHF account shows one debit of CHF 1,016,400 for the Tuesday run (the EUR and USD accounts show their own debits); the camt.054 of each account lists the executed payments…". The batch-debit logic itself (one camt.053 line, camt.054 breaks it down) is correct and worth keeping.

2. **Cross-file contradiction on payment-file structure.** `pain-001` example: *"Alpine's Tuesday run creates **one pain.001 with three payment-information blocks** … containing 84 transactions"* vs `payment-processing` example: *"Business Central generates **three pain.001.001.09 files**"* (consistent with the workflow's step rule "one pain.001 file per bank (or per debtor account)" and with two paying banks). Both can't be true. For a beginner this is exactly the thing that must be unambiguous: a file per bank/account is the common ERP output, and one file cannot span two banks. Align the glossary example with the workflow ("three pain.001 files — one per paying account — with one payment-information block each").

3. **Canonical-facts violation: Helvetic places a CHF deposit.** `large-payment-approval` example: *"A CHF 2m one-week deposit matures on Thursday."* AUTHORING.md's clarified canonical facts: *"CHF rates ≈ 0%: a company with a drawn RCF uses surplus CHF to repay/reduce drawings at the next rollover rather than placing CHF deposits; CHF deposits earn ~nothing"* — and Helvetic is precisely the case (RCF drawn CHF 35m + EUR 27m, **interest periods roll on the 15th**). A parent sitting on CHF 13.6m against a CHF 10m buffer does not place a one-week CHF deposit; it holds the cash for the next rollover. Fix: replace with "a CHF 2m customer receipt is due on Thursday" or "the CHF 2m surplus is held for the 15th RCF rollover". (Kleio's "CHF 4–8m … for deposits" in `large-payment-approval` companies is *not* hit by this rule — no debt to repay — but could mention USD/EUR deposits or a money-market fund, per "EUR/USD surplus can earn €STR/SOFR-level rates".)

## Missing concepts

- **Direct debits (LSV+/BDD, SEPA Direct Debit) and eBill.** The module is credit-transfer-centric. Direct debits appear only in an account-closure list and one reconciliation anecdote (the CHF 7,900 terminated-contract debit — an excellent story, by the way). A Swiss founder interviewing AP/AR people will meet LSV+, BDD, SDD, eBill and the QR-bill immediately; the glossary has `qr-bill` but no direct-debit term, and the fact that **LSV+/BDD is being discontinued (end-Sept 2028, per the SIX factsheet already cited in `six-address-factsheet`)** is current, concrete and relevant.
- **Value dating vs booking date in payment execution** (why "requested execution date" ≠ value date; how value dating is a hidden fee). `value-date` is linked in `large-payment-approval` but never explained in the payment-run context.
- **Virtual accounts** (collections/liquidity structure increasingly used instead of physical accounts) — at least a one-line mention in `bank-account-management` by_size.multinational, where account-count reduction is the theme.
- **Recovery after a fraudulent payment** is correctly delegated to `fraud-investigation`, but `large-payment-approval` could name the practical first hours (bank recall/return request, police report, the 24–48h reality of Swift recall) with one sentence.
- Minor: **standing orders (Daueraufträge)** — the SIX materials repeatedly warn that they must be cleaned before Nov 2026; real AP/AR people get asked about this.

## Minor issues

- `wf-pay.yaml`, `positive-pay` example: "Alpine Inc." → "Alpine Robotics Inc." (canonical name).
- `wf-pay.yaml`, `charge-bearer`: "SEPA credit transfers always use SLEV (shared, no deductions)" — SLEV literally means "following service level"; the parenthetical is the effect, not the code's meaning. Fine for practitioners, slightly off for a glossary definition.
- `bank-fee-analysis` companies.kleio: "0.5–1% from mid, **CHF 15–30k a year**" on USD 250k/month — 0.5–1% of USD 3m is USD 15–30k ≈ **CHF 12–24k**; the USD figures were relabelled as CHF. - `sct-inst` example has Kleio paying a EUR invoice by SCT Inst on a Saturday from a Swiss bank — plausible, but Swiss banks' SCT Inst *sending* is voluntary and patchy (the professional entry says so about the EU, not CH); one hedge word would prevent an interviewee correcting the reader.
- `payment-processing` step 9 wording "returns arrive days later as credits" — under SEPA, returns are often same-day/next-day; "days later" fits correspondent chains. Consider "same or next day for SEPA; days later cross-border".
- `payment-processing` data note: "Structured addresses become mandatory in Swiss payment files from November 2026" — true domestically (verified), but as of 30 Sep 2026 the **cross-border CBPR+ equivalent is deferred** (Swift, 27 Aug 2026; EPC's 15 Nov 2026 SEPA date "stands until further notice" as of 28 Aug 2026, decision pending). A payments module in autumn 2026 should state this split explicitly — right now only the vague "cross-border CBPR+ timelines were extended" in the glossary carries it.
- `bank-reconciliation` example's 212-transaction day: "191 match or post automatically … 21 go to the queue" — a ~90% automatic rate for HM AG is consistent with the "German AR team auto-applies ~78%" line and the honest software section; no change needed, just noting it reads high-but-plausible.
