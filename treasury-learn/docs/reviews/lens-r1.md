## Critical errors (must fix)

**C1. The dw-liquidity model mis-models the one input it calls the weakest — the Chinese balance — and commits the exact trap its own template warns about.**
- `content/pages/lens/dw-liquidity-recommendation.mdx`: "At about 08:00 Lea opens the multibank connectivity tool, checks that all twelve statements arrived"; worked morning: "HM Suzhou, CNY | *stale — yesterday's file failed*"; "Chinese bank file failed … Ticket drafted to the connectivity provider"; `content/data/lens.yaml` inputs: "Prior-day statements … for 12 accounts, source: Multibank connectivity tool … the Chinese bank file fails 1–2 times a week (connectivity)".
- The reference workflow (`content/workflows/daily-cash-positioning.yaml`, the gold standard, and per `content/pages/lens/matrix.mdx` "the reference for how the work is done"): "The twelfth is HM Suzhou's account at a Chinese domestic bank, **which has no EBICS or Swift reporting to the group**: as every day, **Wei has emailed a portal export** from Suzhou, and Lea types the CNY balance into the workbook." There is no file to fail and no connectivity provider to ticket — the balance arrives in an email from a person, every day.
- Why this matters: this is precisely the trap `content/pages/lens/digital-worker.mdx` names for the Inputs section — "Assuming the ERP holds what actually arrives by email". A founder who learns the position model from this page will ask mid-market treasurers about "failed Chinese statement files" and sound naive; the real problem is a human emailing a portal export.
- Fix direction: make the dw model match the workflow (input = "Wei's emailed portal export, whenever Wei sends it; the weakest input"), and use the AUTHORING time-zone canonical for a *connected* Asian bank instead ("the bank delivers early; a missing Asian statement is a connectivity problem, not a time-zone one"). Alternatively change the failure mode to "Wei's export has not arrived by 08:30 — chase Wei, not the connectivity provider".

**C2. dw-bank-fee-review contradicts the bank-fee-analysis workflow on whether the work exists, how often, and what the material finding is.**
- `content/data/lens.yaml` (`dw-bank-fee-review`): "performed_today_by: **Nobody, regularly** — Lea did it once after the 2024 account rationalisation"; `dw-bank-fee-review.mdx` models "A worked quarter (Q3)" and identifies "roughly CHF 3.5k equivalent" in overcharges, with fee table totals of about CHF 49k equivalent per quarter.
- The reference workflow: "In January **Lea spends about four days on the review**"; explicit fees 2025 "about **CHF 410k**"; `companies.helvetic`: "an **annual** Excel review of five banks by Lea, feeding Daniel's wallet review"; `by_size.midmarket`: "An annual Excel review by the treasury analyst … a few disputes a year". The dw's own "Quarterly" cadence and "nobody does it" framing undercut both the workflow and the founder's willingness-to-pay question (a skipped task vs an existing 4-day annual routine are different businesses).
- Worse, the worked quarter **omits the workflow's named largest cost and failure mode**: "Only explicit fees analysed → The largest costs (FX spreads, deductions on receipts) are missed" — the workflow's biggest finding is "CZK conversions executed ~0.35% from mid versus ~0.10% on the FX platform … ~40,000 on ~CHF 16m converted — the item that did not appear on any fee statement". The dw model reproduces exactly the failure its reference workflow warns about.
- Also numeric drift: "Two accounts closed in 2024 still charged account maintenance" at "Swiss bank A" (dw) vs "three accounts closed in the 2024 rationalisation" at "Swiss bank B" (workflow); annualised dw fees ≈ CHF 195k vs the workflow's CHF 410k explicit fees.
- Fix direction: align cadence and "who does it today" with the workflow (or explicitly mark the divergence as the lens's own assumption), add the FX-spread/receipt-deduction line to the worked quarter (it is also the honest bridge to "wallet share"), and reconcile the account count and fee magnitudes.

**C3. dw-exposure-collection's timeline and staffing conflict with the fx-exposure-management reference, and import GlobalChem's control design into Helvetic.**
- Assignment (`content/data/lens.yaml`): "**By working day 4**, give me a clean group exposure report"; "performed_today_by: Lea … **1–2 days a month**"; `dw-exposure-collection.mdx`: "By working day 4 the system has reconciled balance-sheet exposures for seven of eight subsidiaries"; execution: "trades are executed on the bank platform by **authorised dealers** and confirmed by **back office**".
- The reference workflow: "WD2 (Fri 2 Oct) — Lea sends the exposure template to the eight subsidiaries … **deadline WD5 noon**"; "WD5–WD6 — Submissions arrive"; "WD7 — Daniel reviews"; `companies.helvetic`: "Lea spends roughly **WD2–WD6** of each month on it". A clean group report by WD4 cannot include forecast exposures whose submission deadline is WD5. And at Helvetic "back office" does not exist: `fx-hedging.yaml` says "**Lea matches confirmations and keeps the hedge register**"; back office is Marta's Kraków function at GlobalChem.
- Fix direction: re-phase the assignment (e.g. "balance-sheet reconciliation by WD3, full report after the WD5 submissions, for Daniel's WD7 review"), state Lea's real effort (several days at WD2–WD6), and replace "authorised dealers / back office" with Daniel dealing within his mandate and Lea matching confirmations.

## Missing concepts

1. **The vendor-security and procurement gate.** Giving a startup's agent read access to every bank statement, the treasury mailbox and ERP exports is a bank- and audit-facing decision: EBICS/subscriber rules on third-party access, data residency and retention by the vendor, the auditor's view of outsourced processing, insurance. None of the `must_be_true` lists or DW tool tables mentions who must approve that access — yet the method itself says "the binding constraints are internal: audit, ICS…". For a founder this is likely the first wall after the pilot.
2. **System-availability and format-drift failure modes.** Every DW failure-mode list covers hallucination, stale data, automation bias and injection, but none covers "the system is down at 08:00" or "the bank changed its file". The latter is imminent and concrete: under the Swiss Payment Standards the 2009 camt.05x versions may no longer be delivered after Nov 2026 (see `sources/core.yaml`, `six-sps-cash-mgmt`) — every parser and mapping rule in the positioning model breaks at once.
3. **Liability and commercial model.** If Daniel approves a wrong prepared proposal, who bears the loss — the vendor, the company, the insurance? Nothing on outcome-based pricing or guarantees (HighRadius launched outcome-based pricing in Feb 2026) even though "who eats the error" decides whether "maker, never checker" is sellable.
4. **A willingness-to-pay bound.** The lens quantifies time ("30–90 minutes of analyst time a day") but never converts it (≈150–370 h/year ≈ one to two months of a mid-market analyst fully loaded) against the claim that mid-market buyers have "small software budgets". One sentence per candidate class would let the founder sanity-check price points.
5. **Evaluation-set ownership.** Corrections captured "in the system" are simultaneously the learning loop and the switching cost; the defensibility criterion implies it but the DW design rules should say who owns the labelled data on exit.
6. **When the human cannot verify.** The verification sections assume Daniel can judge each explanation; a line on sampling/spot-audit of otherwise-unverifiable output would complete the control story.

## Verdict: REVISE

The method is the most sound and falsifiable-minded part of the section: explicit criteria with scales, one-line reasons per score, "the class is not a weighted sum", stated limitations (ordinal scores, size dependence, vendor claims as inputs, evidence gaps) and interview questions that would move the ratings. The tone is properly sceptical — vendor material is labelled as claims throughout, "no independent evidence of results" is said out loud, and the section repeatedly warns against selling autonomy where controls reserve acts to named humans. Automation claims checked against real product documentation and practitioner reporting hold up almost verbatim.

The revision is about **internal consistency with the manual's own reference workflows**, not about the framework: three of the four digital-worker models contradict the workflow pages they are "based on" (how the Chinese balance arrives — the very "input comes by email" trap the DW template names; whether bank-fee review exists at all and what its material finding is; the exposure-cycle calendar and who matches confirmations at a two-person treasury), plus one mis-cited AFP source and a handful of numeric drifts. Fix C1–C3 and the mis-citation, and this section comfortably passes at round 2.
