## Critical errors (must fix) — quote passage, file, why wrong, evidence/URL, fix direction

1. **Deloitte 2024 survey used for a claim the project's own source registry records as unverifiable — three times.**
   - Quotes:
     - architecture.mdx L110: *"In Deloitte's 2024 survey, cash forecasting at many companies was still primarily spreadsheet-based[](cite:deloitte-gts-2024)."*
     - spreadsheets.mdx L13: *"In Deloitte's 2024 global treasury survey, cash forecasting at many respondents was still primarily spreadsheet-based, and fewer than a quarter rated their forecasting maturity above average[](cite:deloitte-gts-2024)."*
     - tms.mdx L67: *"Surveys consistently find spreadsheets remain in use alongside treasury systems, especially for forecasting[](cite:deloitte-gts-2024)."*
   - Why wrong: `content/sources/core.yaml`, `deloitte-gts-2024`, states explicitly: *"The earlier quoted phrase about forecasting being 'primarily supported by spreadsheets' could not be verified verbatim and is no longer quoted."* The registered note supports only *"213 respondents worldwide… 22% say the maturity of their cash-positioning capability requires development"* — note: cash **positioning**, and "requires development", not "forecasting maturity above average". The second sentence in spreadsheets.mdx ("fewer than a quarter rated their forecasting maturity above average") is a metric nobody has sourced. I re-searched Deloitte's own pages (deloitte.com/us/en/services/consulting/articles/global-corporate-treasury-survey.html and country mirrors; ACT write-up at treasurers.org) and found no spreadsheet-forecasting finding attributable to the 2024 survey from search-result text of the original publisher.
   - Fix direction: delete the spreadsheet claim or replace it with figures that survive verification — the same paragraph already has one: the Strategic Treasurer/TIS 2025 figure (39%→53% calling forecasting "difficult") is correctly cited and matches its source note. If you want a Deloitte data point, use "22% say cash-positioning maturity requires development" as registered, or mark the spreadsheet statement as field observation with no cite.

2. **Swift CSP claim is backwards — a service bureau or Alliance Lite2 does not remove the security attestation.**
   - Quote: bank-connectivity.mdx L48: *"annual security attestation under Swift's Customer Security Programme applies to Swift users (a reason many choose a service bureau or Lite2)"* (echoed in glossary `service-bureau` example: *"would have needed dedicated IT staff and an annual security attestation of its own infrastructure"*).
   - Why wrong: Swift's own corporate guidance says the opposite: *"In the case of corporates using a service bureau, each corporate will still have direct responsibility for its own self-attestation, so the requirements on Alliance Lite2 customers and those using a service bureau are alike."* — Swift, *Swift Customer Security Programme for corporates*, https://www.swift.com/sites/default/files/documents/swift-customer-security-programme-for-corporates.pdf (see also swift.com/myswift/customer-security-programme: attestation is mandatory for every Swift user with their own BIC). What a bureau/Lite2 removes is the *operation and hardening of your own Swift infrastructure* — not the annual CSP attestation.
   - Fix direction: "…applies to every Swift customer regardless of access mode — bureau and Lite2 users must still self-attest annually; what they avoid is running and securing their own Swift interface." Keep the bureau rationale in the glossary but replace "annual security attestation of its own infrastructure" with infrastructure-operating burden.

3. **"MT940/942 not part of the Nov 2025 cut-over" misstates the scope of the coexistence end.**
   - Quotes: index.mdx L43: *"MT940/MT942 statements | Still supported; not part of the Nov 2025 cut-over…"*; bank-connectivity.mdx L44: *"The November 2025 end of MT/ISO 20022 coexistence applied to cross-border payment messages between banks (CBPR+)."*
   - Why wrong: the registered Swift source itself (`swift-iso-mt9xx`, core.yaml) says coexistence ended 22 Nov 2025 for *"cross-border payments and **reporting** (CBPR+)"*, and that *"Other in-scope MT types, including reporting/statement messages, were not withdrawn from FIN immediately: they are deprecated (no longer maintained), with disincentives to be introduced later."* So MT940/942 **were in scope**; what happened in Nov 2025 is that they were deprecated rather than withdrawn. Direction of travel now documented by banks quoting Swift: J.P. Morgan's ISO 20022 FAQ says *"As per current Swift communication the end-of-coexistence period for statements and reporting message is November 2028"* (https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/payments/fx-cross-border/iso-20022-faqs); Kyriba's migration FAQ says Swift removed the Nov 2025 deadline for reporting messages with no new date before end-2026. A founder who says "MT940 wasn't part of the ISO migration" will be corrected.
   - Fix direction: "MT940/MT942 are in CBPR+ scope but were deprecated (not withdrawn) in Nov 2025; interbank reporting migration now targets ~2028 per current Swift communication; corporate-to-bank MT940 delivery follows each bank's own timeline (Swiss banks quote no end date — zkb-iso20022-factsheet)."

## Missing concepts

- **Swift gpi / payment tracking (UETR).** The connectivity story stops at "payment out, statement in". The actual 2025–2026 reality corporates hear about is end-to-end tracking of cross-border payments (UETR on each payment, gpi Tracker status updates, charge/FX transparency, gpi Stop & Recall). Nothing in the module mentions it — not "What has to flow", not the Swift section, not the formats table (a tracked payment is still a pain.001/MT101, but status now flows back). The project already registers a source (`ubs-gpi-corporates`, wf-pay.yaml) and a term (`swift-gpi`, glossary/wf-pay.yaml) — cross-link rather than duplicate.
- **Instant payments in the "Standards timeline 2025–2026" table.** The EU Instant Payments Regulation (receive SCT Inst Jan 2025, send Oct 2025, Verification of Payee from Oct 2025) and Swiss SIC Instant Payments (live Aug 2024, mandatory reception for all remaining Swiss banks by end-2026) are exactly the dated milestones this table exists for, and they change treasury mechanics (24/7 settlement, no overnight window for fraud checks or positioning, cut-offs less meaningful). Sources already registered in wf-pay.yaml (Instant Payments Regulation, SIC IP) and terms exist in glossary/map.yaml (`instant-payments`) and glossary/wf-pay.yaml. The table covers only ISO 20022/Swiss versions/EBICS/addresses.
- **Name bLink in the Swiss API paragraph.** bank-connectivity.mdx L58 says Swiss API access came "through market initiatives and bank-by-bank offerings rather than regulation" — the market initiative is SIX bLink (registered source `six-blink` in core.yaml), and it is precisely Kleio's stack: accounting software connecting to the banks via bLink with approval in e-banking. The `api-banking` glossary term mentions bLink; the page a founder actually reads on connectivity does not.
- **Aggregator APIs.** Cloud cash platforms often connect via account-information aggregators (Tink/Plaid/Codat class) rather than direct bank APIs — one sentence in "Bank APIs" with the data-depth/liability caveat would match the Agicap profile's "aggregator/API connections".
- **SAP FSCM / payment approval inside SAP.** The SAP profile names F110 payment runs (good, and rare in generic material) but not Bank Communication Management / FSCM payment approval — the thing that decides who signs SAP-generated files before they reach MBC. One clause.
- **What happens to pain.002 rejections in practice.** Formats table says "Rejections arrive here; someone must read them" — good instinct, but this is a workflow page: name the routine (payment-factory day-end exception list, ops chases, re-issue before the bank cut-off). This is one of the most common "what actually goes wrong" stories in payment interviews.

## Verdict: REVISE

Workflow realism, pedagogy and concreteness are at or near the bar (9/9/8) and the currentness machinery (dated timeline table, checked/not-re-verified markers, vendor-claims warning) is the strongest in the manual. But the module fails its own core promise in three places: it repeats a survey claim its own source register records as unverifiable (three files), it states the Swift CSP rationale backwards, and it mis-scopes what the November 2025 milestone covered. Fix the three critical errors, add gpi/instant payments/bLink to the connectivity story, and tighten the five minor consistency points, and this passes comfortably in round 2.
