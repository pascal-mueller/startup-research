## The assignment today

At about 08:00 Lea opens the multibank connectivity tool, checks that all twelve statements arrived, copies balances
into the positioning workbook, compares yesterday's movements with what was expected, reads the overnight emails from
Giulia (Italy), Jens (Germany) and Wei (China), adds today's known flows, and proposes transfers. Daniel reviews at about
09:30 and approves before the EUR cut-off. It takes her 60–90 minutes on a normal day and most of the morning when a
statement is missing or something odd shows up. Leading vendors now market agents for exactly this step[](cite:sap-cash-agent,kyriba-tai-2025)
— claims, but a sign of where the incumbents are going.

## A worked morning

<Example title="Tuesday: what the system hands Daniel at 09:10" company="helvetic" open>

**Position (extract, CHF equivalent at EUR/CHF 0.935, illustrative):**

| Account | Opening | Known flows today | Projected close | Minimum | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HM AG, CHF (Swiss bank A) | CHF 14.2m | −0.6m (Swiss supplier run) | CHF 13.6m | CHF 3.0m | Surplus |
| HM AG, EUR (Swiss bank A) | EUR 3.1m | — | EUR 3.1m | EUR 0.5m | Surplus |
| HM Deutschland, EUR | EUR 1.9m | −2.3m (AP run released in SAP) | EUR −0.4m | EUR 0.25m | **Short EUR 0.65m** |
| HM Italia, EUR | EUR 0.4m | −1.2m (supplier, from Giulia's email) | EUR −0.8m | EUR 0.2m | **Short EUR 1.0m** |
| HM Suzhou, CNY | *stale — yesterday's file failed* | — | — | — | Excluded; not concentrated anyway |

**Exceptions (with evidence):**

1. HM Deutschland: unexpected debit EUR 186k, SEPA direct debit. *Likely explanation:* annual machinery-insurance
   premium — same creditor ID and similar amount debited in the same week last year (statement line linked). Not in the forecast.
2. HM Italia: expected customer receipt EUR 1.5m did not arrive. *Unexplained* — no remittance, no email. Italy has
   over-forecast receipts in 9 of the last 12 weeks (see [variance model](/lens/dw-forecast-variance)).
3. China: Chinese bank file failed — the bank delivers early (Asia is ahead of CET), so this is a connectivity problem, not timing.
   Ticket drafted to the connectivity provider; Wei asked for the portal balance.

**Proposals (policy-checked):**

| # | Action | Amount | Value date | Why | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intercompany loan HM AG → HM Deutschland | EUR 0.70m | Today | Cover AP run + buffer | Min balances ✓, existing IC loan agreement ✓ |
| 2 | Intercompany loan HM AG → HM Italia | EUR 1.00m | Today | Cover supplier payment up to the minimum balance | Min balances ✓ |
| 3 | No CHF action today; flag reducing the RCF drawing at Friday's rollover | CHF 5m | Friday | CHF deposits earn ~0%; drawn RCF costs interest | For Daniel's decision |

**Not sure about:** whether the EUR 1.5m Italian receipt arrives today; whether the insurance premium recurs quarterly.

**Daniel's response (09:25):** approves #1; changes #2 to **EUR 1.2m** ("Italy's receipts are unreliable — keep a bigger
buffer this week"); asks for #3 in Thursday's liquidity note. Lea enters both transfers; a second signatory releases
them in the bank's signature app.

**What the system records:** one accept, one edit with reason, one deferral; the insurance explanation marked correct;
Wednesday morning, projected vs actual closing balances are scored automatically (the Italian customer paid EUR 1.5m late in the
afternoon, so Italy closed at EUR 1.9m instead of EUR 0.4m — Daniel's extra buffer was prudent, not needed this time).

</Example>

## What this model deliberately leaves out

- **Releasing transfers.** Under the bank mandate and the [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle), two
  authorised people sign. The system never holds signing rights — this also makes it a poor target for
  [prompt injection](term:prompt-injection) via a fake supplier email.
- **Deciding the buffer.** The system proposes, Daniel's edit ("keep a bigger buffer for Italy") is the judgment. Over months
  those edits become a learned preference — still proposed, still approved.
- **Structural questions.** Whether Helvetic should set up a cash pool, which accounts to close, which bank gets the
  deposits — Daniel and the CFO's decisions, informed by months of these notes.

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions this model suggests">

To Lea: "What did you have to ask someone this morning — and how did the answer reach you?" To Daniel: "What did you
change in Lea's proposal last week, and why?" and "What would have to be true for you to approve a proposal you hadn't
seen Lea check?" If Daniel never changes anything, the value is in Lea's time; if he changes a lot, the value is in
learning his reasons.

</Callout>
