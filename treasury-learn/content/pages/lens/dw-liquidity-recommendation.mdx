Illustrative model, not a product description. Based on the [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) workflow
and the [Helvetic](co:helvetic) scenario: 12 operating accounts at 5 banks, six currencies, no cash pool — Daniel
concentrates cash manually by intercompany loan. Lens rating: [good agent candidate](/lens/matrix#daily-cash-positioning).
The worked morning below is the manual's shared [Tuesday 17 November 2026](/start) — the same numbers as the workflow
example, seen as the system's output.

## The assignment today

At about 07:30 Lea opens the multibank connectivity tool: [11 of the 12 statements are in](why:systems-11-of-12-statements).
The twelfth balance — HM Suzhou's account at a Chinese domestic bank — is not a file at all: that bank has no EBICS or
Swift reporting to the group, and Wei emails a portal export every morning which Lea types into the workbook
([why the fallback rule is what it is](why:day-china-manual-balance)). She compares yesterday's movements with what was
expected, reads the overnight emails from Giulia (Italy), Jens (Germany) and Wei (China), adds today's known flows, and
proposes transfers. Daniel reviews at about 09:10 and approves before the Swiss bank's early-afternoon cut-off
([the day runs backwards from the cut-offs](why:cut-offs-order-the-day)). It takes her 60–90 minutes on a normal day and
most of the morning when an input is missing or something odd shows up. Leading vendors now market agents for exactly
this step[](cite:sap-cash-agent,kyriba-tai-2025) — claims, but a sign of where the incumbents are going.

## A worked morning

<Example title="Tuesday 17 November 2026: what the system hands Daniel at 09:10" company="helvetic" open>

**Position (extract from the 12-account position, CHF equivalent at EUR/CHF 0.935, USD/CHF 0.80, illustrative):**

| Account | Opening, value-dated | Known flows today | Projected close | Minimum | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HM AG, CHF (Swiss bank A) | CHF 14.2m | −1.1m (Swiss supplier run) | CHF 13.1m | CHF [2.0m](why:buffer-layers) | Above minimum — but not surplus (see #3) |
| HM AG, EUR (Swiss bank A) | EUR 6.8m | +0.9m receipt (07:45 camt.052); −1.5m to Germany | EUR 6.2m | EUR 0.5m | OK |
| HM Deutschland, EUR | EUR 1.5m (expected 1.8m) | −2.6m (AP run released in SAP) | EUR −1.1m | EUR 0.25m | **Short EUR 1.35m** |
| HM Italia, EUR | EUR 2.9m (expected 5.15m) | — | EUR 2.9m | EUR 0.3m | Customer receipt missing |
| HM Suzhou, CNY | CNY 55m (*Wei's emailed export, typed in 07:35*) | — | CNY 55m | CNY 5m | Not concentrated ([trapped](term:trapped-cash)); [excluded from usable cash](why:china-cash-excluded) |

Group total about **CHF 41m** — about CHF 2.6m below yesterday's forecast. Tie-out line on the note: 12/12 balances
carried an as-of time and a source tag (11 tool statements + one email), and every opening balance equalled Monday's
value-dated close — the opening-equals-prior-close check ran clean today; a gap would have named the bank that
back-valued something and blocked the note until it was explained.

**Exceptions (each explanation links its evidence; no evidence, no explanation):**

1. **HM Italia:** expected customer receipt EUR 2.25m (≈ CHF 2.1m) did not arrive. *Unexplained* — no remittance, no
   email; Giulia emailed after the note went out. Italy has over-forecast receipts in 8 of the last 10 weeks (see the
   [variance model](/lens/dw-forecast-variance)).
2. **HM Deutschland:** unexpected debit EUR 0.3m by the municipality — the Gewerbesteuer (trade-tax) prepayment due
   15 November, collected by [direct debit](term:direct-debit); same creditor and amount last November (statement line
   linked), Jens confirms by email. *Missing input, not error* — the system proposes adding all four German trade-tax
   dates to the central calendar.
3. **HM AG, CHF:** CHF 180k debit to a known Swiss supplier — the fraud-shaped exception, so nothing is assumed: same
   beneficiary and IBAN as the supplier master record, same invoice reference as last Thursday's payment, and the
   payment tool shows one file uploaded twice. *Likely duplicate* — verified by Lea before the word is used; refund
   requested; booked as a timing difference.

**The weakest input is not a feed:** the HM Suzhou balance arrived in Wei's email, and that is normal. The guardrails
for it are different from the other eleven: the balance carries "source: email, as-of: Suzhou close"; if the export is
not in the mailbox by 08:30 the action is to **chase Wei** — never the connectivity provider — and the workbook carries
yesterday's balance flagged *unconfirmed*, excluded from concentration decisions. For the eleven connected accounts
the timing intuition is inverted: Asian statements arrive early and US statements last, so a missing statement there is
a connectivity problem, never a time-zone one.

**Proposals (policy-checked):**

| # | Action | Amount | Value date | Why | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intercompany loan HM AG → HM Deutschland, under the standing IC framework agreement | EUR 1.5m | Today | Covers the AP run and the EUR 0.25m minimum with a buffer | Min balances ✓, existing IC agreement ✓, within four-eyes ✓ |
| 2 | Alternative: pull EUR 1.5m from HM Italia | EUR 1.5m | Today | — | Not recommended: Italy's receipt is missing and a EUR 1.8m supplier run lands Thursday |
| 3 | No CHF action today; flag reducing the CHF 30m RCF drawing at the 15 January rollover | CHF 10–15m | 15 Jan | CHF earns ~0% on deposit but is needed for week 4; the drawing cannot be prepaid mid-period without break costs | For Daniel's decision |

**Not sure about:** whether the Italian receipt arrives this week; whether the CHF 180k refund lands before month-end.

**Daniel's response (09:10):** releases #1 — Lea prepared it and he releases it under
[four-eyes](why:lea-never-releases) before the Swiss bank's early-afternoon EUR cut-off — with one edit to the proposal:
**pay as an urgent EUR payment via T2, not a SEPA credit transfer** ("SEPA is not same-day guaranteed, and Germany must
cover today's run"). Leaves #2 standing ("if Italy's receipt lands by Thursday, we fund the Italian run from Italy").
Defers #3 to Thursday's liquidity note — the CHF 13.1m looks like surplus and is not: the Swiss payroll on
25 November and the December payrolls with 13th salaries
([the week-4 low point](why:week-four-trough)) are still ahead, and the CFO's CHF 10m
[group minimum](why:buffer-layers) is held at the parent, so [the RCF reduction waits for the rollover](why:fifteenth-rollover-repayment).

**What the system records:** one release with one edit and its reason ("same-day value needs T2"), one deferral with
its reason; the trade-tax explanation marked correct; the duplicate flag marked "right to flag — but verify before
naming a cause"; and next morning, projected vs actual closing balances per account are scored automatically (Italy
closed at its projected EUR 2.9m — the receipt stayed out all day). The reviewer is watched too: the note carries the
30-day accept-without-edit rate (78%) and this month's seeded test item — a plausible-looking duplicate debit that was
correctly flagged — because [automation bias](term:automation-bias) is the failure that arrives with success.

</Example>

<Callout type="confusion" title="The trap this page is built to avoid">

The digital-worker template warns: "assuming the ERP holds what actually arrives by email". Helvetic's positioning
model is where that trap lives — the twelfth balance has no file, no format and no provider to ticket. A system that
models it as "the Chinese bank file failed, ticket raised" is not a small error: it teaches a founder to ask mid-market
treasurers about failed statement files, when the real workflow is a person in Suzhou emailing a portal export and a
treasurer deciding what to do when it is late.

</Callout>

## What this model deliberately leaves out

- **Releasing transfers.** Under the bank mandate and the [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle),
  [Lea prepares and Daniel releases](why:lea-never-releases) (above CHF 2m the CFO and the CEO co-sign in addition). The
  system never holds signing rights — this also makes it a poor target for
  [prompt injection](term:prompt-injection) via a fake supplier email, and it is
  [why its output is a recommendation, not an instruction](why:lens-recommendation-not-instruction).
- **Deciding the buffer.** The system proposes, Daniel's edit ("pay via T2, not SEPA") is the judgment. Over months
  those edits become a learned preference — still proposed, still approved.
- **Structural questions.** Whether Helvetic should set up a cash pool, which accounts to close, which bank gets the
  deposits — Daniel and the CFO's decisions, informed by months of these notes.

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions this model suggests">

To Lea: "What did you have to ask someone this morning — and how did the answer reach you?" To Daniel: "What did you
change in Lea's proposal last week, and why?" and "What would have to be true for you to approve a proposal you hadn't
seen Lea check?" If Daniel never changes anything, the value is in Lea's time; if he changes a lot, the value is in
learning his reasons.

</Callout>
