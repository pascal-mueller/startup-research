<Callout type="synthesis">

Illustrative model based on the [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) workflow and [Helvetic](co:helvetic)'s five
banks. Lens rating: [good agent candidate](/lens/matrix#bank-fee-analysis), weak standalone business. The reference
workflow has Lea spend **about four days each January** on an annual review that feeds Daniel's wallet review — the
[mid-market equilibrium](why:fee-review-annual), not a skipped task; the model below is that review prepared by a
system, with one deliberate divergence: a system can afford to run the price check on every statement, so a mispricing
is caught in its first month instead of at the next January. AFP recommends treating banks as vendors and checking
negotiated pricing is actually applied[](cite:afp-bank-fee-analysis); the ISO 20022 camt.086 message is meant to
standardise fee reporting[](cite:hsbc-camt086).

</Callout>

## Why it is agent-shaped

Every charge can be compared with an agreed price; the comparison is objective; a mistake costs an awkward email, not
money; and the work is translation drudgery (each bank names the same service differently) that is nobody's hobby.
That makes it a clean test of whether an AI system can do a whole assignment end to end — and a natural wedge into the
more valuable conversation about which bank gets which business ([wallet share](term:wallet-share)).

But the task splits in two, and only one half is machine work. Checking is [automatable; the negotiation is not](why:lens-fee-check-vs-negotiation) — which banks to challenge, how hard, and what to trade for the
relationship is judgment whose outcome is never observable. And the reference workflow's named failure mode is the one
to reproduce deliberately: **if you analyse only explicit fees, you miss the largest cost**.

## A worked review

<Example title="January: the annual review, five banks, one price grid" company="helvetic" open>

Explicit fees for 2025 (excluding loan margins and RCF commitment fees): about **CHF 410k**. Only one of the five
banks sends structured [camt.086](term:bank-services-billing); the others send PDFs, and the Chinese bank only shows
fee debits on the account statement. The workflow is mostly translation — one line from the German bank's PDF,
`210150 SCT FILE  13,980 @ 0.90` ("SEPA-Überweisung Datei, per Stück"), becomes in the price grid "SEPA credit
transfer, file-based, 13,980 items at CHF 0.90, tendered price CHF 0.25" — and that mapping is the second row below.

| Finding | Bank | How found | Annual effect (CHF, illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly account-package fees still charged for three accounts closed in the 2024 rationalisation | Swiss bank B | Account register vs fee statement | 1,800 — refunded for 14 months |
| ~14,000 file-based SEPA payments priced at the manual e-banking rate (CHF 0.90 instead of the agreed 0.25) | German bank | Price grid vs statement | ~9,100 — refunded and corrected |
| "Lifting fee" of CHF 8 on ~1,100 incoming cross-border EUR payments, not in the agreement | Swiss bank A | Receipts net of fees on camt.053 | ~8,800 — waived going forward, no refund |
| Paper statements and six unused e-banking tokens | Two banks | Volume check | ~1,500 — cancelled |
| **CZK conversions executed ~0.35% from mid versus ~0.10% on the FX platform** | German bank | Executed rates vs mid-market at trade time | **~40,000 on ~CHF 16m converted** — the item that appears on no fee statement |

*Amounts illustrative.* Explicit-fee findings: roughly **CHF 21k** recovered or avoided. The larger number is the last
row: about **CHF 40k** of FX spread on about CHF 16m of CZK conversions — invisible in every fee report, found only by
comparing executed rates with mid at trade time. This is why "fee reconciliation" and "what banking actually costs"
are different assignments, and why the honest output is the **wallet-share page**: fees + FX + lending per bank against
what each bank provides. The workflow's own review found the bank with the smallest RCF share (20% of the CHF 200m
commitments) earning about 6% of Helvetic's roughly CHF 2.4m wallet — which is how the next EUR hedging tranche gets
allocated.

**Daniel's edits:** sends the Swiss bank B and German bank disputes (they are factual and the banks hold little
grudge value); drops the US wire-fee item ("not worth it"); keeps the Swiss bank A lifting-fee complaint for the next
pricing negotiation instead of a dispute email — the bank is in the RCF. The system drafted all of it; the choices of
target, tone and timing are his ([why that half cannot be automated](why:lens-fee-check-vs-negotiation)).

</Example>

<Callout type="field" title="A field note on formats">

Outside the US, most corporates still receive bank fees as statement lines and PDF invoices rather than structured
billing files — practitioner variation, not a survey finding. Helvetic's five banks illustrate the spread: one
camt.086, three PDF providers, one statement-debits-only bank.

</Callout>

## Limits

- **The price schedule has to exist.** Often the "agreed" price lives in an email from three years ago or in the head of
  the person who negotiated it. The Chinese bank has no written price schedule at all — flagged, not disputed.
- **Relationship banks.** A bank in the RCF can be challenged, but carefully. The system drafts; the treasurer decides
  tone and whether to send.
- **Small money.** Even done well, this is tens of thousands a year at a mid-market group — meaningful next to the
  review's four days, [small next to a software budget](why:lens-time-price-bound). It justifies itself as part of a
  broader cash-operations system, not as a product.

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions this model suggests">

"When did you last check bank fees against the agreed pricing — and what did you find?" "Where is your price schedule
for each bank?" "How do you measure what you pay inside the FX spread?" If the answer to the first is "never", ask why
— lack of time, or lack of value? If the answer is "annually, in January", ask what the last review changed: the
difference between a skipped task and a four-day routine is a different business.

</Callout>
