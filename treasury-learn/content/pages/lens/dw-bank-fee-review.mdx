<Callout type="synthesis">

Illustrative model based on the [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) workflow and [Helvetic](co:helvetic)'s five
banks. Lens rating: [good agent candidate](/lens/matrix#bank-fee-analysis), weak standalone business. AFP recommends
treating banks as vendors and checking negotiated pricing is actually applied[](cite:afp-bank-fee-analysis); the ISO 20022
camt.086 message is meant to standardise fee reporting[](cite:hsbc-camt086), but outside the US most corporates still
receive fees as statement lines and PDFs.

</Callout>

## Why it is agent-shaped

Every charge can be compared with an agreed price; the comparison is objective; a mistake costs an awkward email, not
money; and nobody enjoys doing it, so it is often not done at all. That makes it a clean test of whether an AI system can
do a whole assignment end to end — and a natural wedge into the more valuable conversation about which bank gets which
business ([wallet share](term:wallet-share)).

## A worked quarter

<Example title="Q3 review: what the system finds" company="helvetic" open>

| Bank | Charges Q3 | At agreed prices | Difference | Main finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swiss bank A (house bank) | CHF 18.4k | CHF 16.9k | CHF 1.5k | Two accounts closed in 2024 still charged account maintenance |
| Swiss bank B | CHF 7.2k | CHF 7.2k | — | — |
| German bank | EUR 9.8k | EUR 8.1k | EUR 1.7k | List price applied to SEPA payments since a July price change; negotiated price not loaded |
| Global bank (US/UK) | USD 14.6k | USD 14.1k | USD 0.5k | Account-analysis codes mapped; minor wire-fee differences |
| Chinese bank | CNY 21k | not mapped | — | No written price schedule found — flagged, not disputed |

*Amounts illustrative.* Total identified in the quarter: roughly CHF 3.5k equivalent — small against Helvetic's interest
and FX costs, which is precisely the point of the "weak standalone business" rating. The more useful output is the
wallet-share page: the German bank earns the highest fees per payment while providing the smallest share of the RCF.

**Daniel's edits:** sends the Swiss bank A and German bank disputes; drops the US wire-fee item ("not worth it"); asks
for the wallet-share page in the annual bank review.

</Example>

## Limits

- **The price schedule has to exist.** Often the "agreed" price lives in an email from three years ago or in the head of
  the person who negotiated it.
- **Relationship banks.** A bank in the RCF can be challenged, but carefully. The system drafts; the treasurer decides tone and whether to send.
- **Small money.** Even when done well, this is a few thousand francs a quarter at a mid-market group. It justifies itself as
  part of a broader cash-operations system, not as a product.

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions this model suggests">

"When did you last check bank fees against the agreed pricing — and what did you find?" "Where is your price schedule
for each bank?" If the answer to the first is "never", ask why — lack of time, or lack of value? Both answers matter.

</Callout>
