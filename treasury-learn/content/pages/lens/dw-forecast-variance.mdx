## Why variance first, not prediction

Prediction is where vendors compete, and its accuracy is hard to attribute (was the forecast better because of the
model, or because the quarter was calm?). Variance explanation is different: it is a **bounded** question about the past,
every answer can be **checked against evidence**, and "timing" answers are **scored automatically** when the money does
or does not arrive. It also produces what prediction later needs — categorised actuals and a history of *why* forecasts
missed.

## A worked week

<Example title="Week 23: the system's variance table and Daniel's verdicts" company="helvetic" open>

The system reconciles last week's actuals to the bank statements (group net −5.0m vs forecast −1.6m, CHF m) and proposes:

| Line | Variance | Proposed type | Proposed reason and evidence | Daniel's verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer receipts, Italy | −2.1 | Timing | Largest customer's invoice open in the Italian ERP, not disputed; paid 10 days late in 7 of last 10 invoices | **Right** |
| Customer receipts, UK | −0.7 | Permanent | Short payment against invoice; credit-control email mentions a disputed delivery | **Right** — "and flag it to the CFO" |
| Supplier payments, Germany | −0.3 | Timing | AP run released Thursday instead of Friday (SAP run log) | **Right** |
| Tax, Germany | −0.3 | Missing input | Trade-tax prepayment debit; not in Jens's submission; same debit last quarter | **Right** — and adds all four quarterly dates to the central calendar |
| Total | **−3.4** | | Ties to statements ✓ | |

**Proposed forward adjustments:** move Italy's CHF 2.1m into week 1 (accepted); haircut Italy's receipts by 15% in
weeks 5–13 because of a 9-of-12-week over-forecast pattern (accepted, with Daniel's note "review in a quarter"); add the
German tax calendar (accepted).

**Draft questions to subsidiaries:** one to Giulia ("Customer X's invoice was paid 10 days late again — should we
assume that in the template?"), one to Jens (tax dates). Lea edits the tone and sends them under her name.

**Four weeks later**, the system scores its own "timing" call: the Italian CHF 2.1m arrived in week 24 → correct. The UK
CHF 0.7m never arrived → the "permanent" call was also correct. Both enter the evaluation set.

</Example>

## What makes this hard despite the good rating

- **Categorising actuals** is the unglamorous prerequisite. If bank transactions cannot be mapped reliably to forecast
  lines (customer receipts vs intercompany vs tax), every variance is wrong. This mapping is where most effort goes.
- **Organisational inputs.** The biggest variances come from what subsidiaries submit. A system can detect copy-pasted
  templates and missing lumpy items; it cannot make local finance care.
- **Explanation ≠ cause.** A plausible explanation with evidence can still be the wrong cause. The "right / wrong /
  partly" verdicts, and the outcome scoring of timing calls, exist to measure exactly that.

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions this model suggests">

"Show me last week's variance analysis. Which lines did you explain, which did you leave, and why?" "How do you know,
four weeks later, whether 'timing' really was timing?" "Do you store old forecast versions?" — if not, there is no
feedback loop yet, and building one is the first job.

</Callout>
