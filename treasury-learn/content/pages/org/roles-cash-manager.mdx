## Two titles, one job family

"Cash Manager", "Liquidity Manager", "Cash & Liquidity Manager", "Cash Pool Manager", "Cash Operations Manager": job adverts use all of them for overlapping work[](cite:job-titles-cash-liquidity). In German-speaking companies the daily part is called *[Disposition](term:cash-disposition)*. A rough rule, not a standard:

- **Cash Manager** usually leans operational: today's position, transfers, pools, bank accounts.
- **Liquidity Manager** usually leans forward-looking: forecast, funding and investment planning, headroom, trapped cash.
- **Head of Cash & Liquidity** (Priya at [GlobalChem](co:globalchem)) leads both, often including the [in-house bank](term:in-house-bank).

Bosch's cash-manager advert shows the typical mix. It covers managing bank accounts, including openings, closings and bank KYC; managing global liquidity and the cash position; running daily cash operations, "including cash disposition and liquidity forecasts"; and supporting global cash-pooling structures[](cite:job-bosch-cash-manager).

## What a cash manager actually does

| Workflow | Cash manager's part | Example at GlobalChem |
|---|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Owns it. Analysts build the position, the cash manager decides the moves | 08:00: EUR and USD pool headers after overnight sweeps; manual funding for non-pooled entities (e.g. India, Brazil) |
| Liquidity monitoring | Intraday checks of large expected receipts; headroom vs policy | A USD 18m customer receipt expected by 15:00 CET arrives at 16:40; CP maturity cover is checked |
| [Cash pooling](term:cash-pooling) | Monitors sweeps, fixes failures, proposes new participants | A German entity's sweep failed because of a blocked account; a manual transfer is made and the bank chased |
| [Funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary) | Decides and executes intercompany loans within limits; involves Tax for new structures | Brazil needs BRL 40m for a plant expansion: IHB loan vs local bank loan, decided with Tax and Priya |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Consolidates the short-term forecast, tracks accuracy by entity | ±10% accuracy target on 4-week receipts; entities outside it get a call |
| [Surplus investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment) | Places deposits and MMFs within counterparty limits | EUR surplus into a 1-week deposit or MMF at around €STR levels. CHF balances earn about 0%, so they are kept to operating needs and any surplus reduces borrowings |
| Netting | Runs or coordinates the monthly [netting](term:netting) cycle | Disputed intercompany invoices resolved before the netting run |
| Bank accounts | Account structure, openings and closures (often with operations) | Closing dormant accounts after an acquisition |

## Overlap with the Treasury Manager

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Where one ends and the other starts">

At [Helvetic](co:helvetic) there is no cash manager: Daniel is the cash manager, the FX manager and the funding manager at once, and Lea prepares for all three. At GlobalChem these are three desks. A group usually splits off a cash manager when the daily position takes most of someone's day: many entities, pools, an in-house bank, several time zones. Below that point, cash management is one part of the Treasury Manager's day.

If you interview someone called Treasury Manager at a multinational who spends the day on pools and funding, they are functionally a cash manager. Ask what they do, not what they are called.

</Callout>

## Interview implications

<Callout type="interview">

A cash or liquidity manager is the best interviewee for anything daily and intraday: statements, cut-offs, pools, sweeps, [trapped cash](term:trapped-cash), counterparty limits and short-term investment decisions. They are also excellent on how good the forecast really is, because they rely on it every day.

- "What did you move today, and why that amount?"
- "What happened the last time a sweep or a statement failed?"
- "Which entities or countries give you the most trouble, and why?"
- "How do you decide how much buffer to keep in an account?"
- "Which decisions can you make alone, and which go to your head of cash or the Group Treasurer?"

They are usually weaker on hedge accounting, bond issuance and rating agencies.

</Callout>
