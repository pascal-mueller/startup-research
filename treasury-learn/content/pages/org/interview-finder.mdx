## How to read this finder

For every topic the finder lists three groups of roles:

- **Best people to interview** — the people who *perform or own* the work. They can tell you what they did last Tuesday, which is what you need.
- **Also worth talking to** — people who supply inputs, approve, or experience the work from the other side. Interview them second, to check and complete the picture.
- **Less useful at first** — people who are too far from the mechanics (or too close to only one slice of it). Not useless: often valuable *later*, for decisions, budgets or context.

Role names link to the role pages. Titles differ between companies, so read the "Depends on company size" box before booking anyone — at a 70-person company the "best" person for almost every topic is the CFO.

## The rule behind every entry

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Doer first, reviewer second, decider third">

The finder applies one rule: first interview the person who **did the work most recently**, then the person who **reviews or approves** it, then the person who **decides** based on it. Each layer upwards knows more about *why* and less about *how*.

- The doer can walk you through the last instance step by step (systems opened, files exported, people chased).
- The reviewer can tell you what they change, what they distrust, and what they escalate.
- The decider can tell you what the output is used for, what it is worth, and what went wrong when it failed.

If you start at the top — as founders often do, because CFOs are easier to reach through investors — you hear the process as it is *supposed* to work. Start at the bottom and you hear how it actually works.

</Callout>

<Callout type="confusion" title="Same title, different job">

"Treasurer" at a CHF 80m SME may be the head of accounting with treasury as 20% of the job; at a CHF 7bn group it is a Group Treasurer running 25 people who hasn't built a forecast in ten years. "Treasury Manager" at [Helvetic](co:helvetic) does FX, debt, cash and banks; at [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) the same title might sit in one desk doing one thing. Before the interview, ask: *"What were the last three things you worked on?"* — see [before and after the interview](/interview/before-and-after).

</Callout>

## When the org chart doesn't match

- **No treasury team at all** (most startups and many SMEs): interview the CFO and the head of accounting together for cash, payments and banks; the external fiduciary for payroll and statutory side; the bank relationship manager only for the bank's perspective.
- **A shared-service centre does the operations**: payment execution, reconciliation and static data may sit in Kraków, Manila or Porto rather than in HQ treasury. The HQ treasurer knows the design; the SSC team lead knows the exceptions.
- **A regional treasury centre** (e.g. Singapore for Asia): regional questions (restricted currencies, local banks, pooling in Asia) go there, not to HQ.
- **Consultants and ex-treasurers**: useful to learn vocabulary and patterns across companies, but they describe *many* companies' processes in general terms — the opposite of what a behaviour-based interview needs. Use them for preparation, not as evidence.
