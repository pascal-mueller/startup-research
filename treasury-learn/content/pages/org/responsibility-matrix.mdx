## How to read it

| Code | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **O** — Owner | Accountable for the outcome; answers when it goes wrong | Daniel owns the 13-week forecast at Helvetic |
| **P** — Performs | Does the hands-on work | Lea builds it |
| **D** — Contributes data | Supplies inputs without owning the result | Subsidiary finance managers, AP, FP&A |
| **A** — Approves | Authorises a decision, deal or payment (a signature or formal approval) | Thomas approves an RCF drawing above the threshold |
| **R** — Reviews | Checks and challenges the output; no formal sign-off | Thomas reviews the forecast before the board pack |
| **I** — Informed | Receives the result | Board receives the quarterly treasury report |
| **·** | Usually not involved | |

<Callout type="confusion" title="R means Reviews here, not Responsible">

In the classic RACI model, **R** = *Responsible* (does the work) and **A** = *Accountable*. This manual follows the codes practitioners use when describing treasury work: **P** for the person who does it, **O** for the accountable owner, **A** only for formal approval, and **R** for review. If a treasurer shows you their own RACI, check which convention they use. See [responsibility matrix](term:raci-matrix).

</Callout>

## Patterns across sizes

<Callout type="synthesis" title="What moves as companies grow">

- **From one person to many.** At [Kleio](co:kleio) the CFO owns 16 of the 19 rows (the accountant owns bank reconciliation; cash pooling does not apply). At [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) ownership is spread across five treasury desks, accounting, the SSC and subsidiaries — and the Group Treasurer *owns* few rows herself; she owns four, and approves or reviews most of the rest.
- **Execution leaves treasury before decisions do.** Payment preparation and execution sit with accounting or AP at small companies, with local finance in the mid-market, and with an SSC and payment factory in large groups. Treasury keeps the bank channels and controls.
- **Approval moves up only for exceptions.** In all sizes the board approves the policy and big financing; everyday approvals move from the CFO (small) to treasury within mandates (large).
- **Data suppliers multiply.** The cash forecast has two data contributors at Kleio (the accountant and department heads), six groups at Alpine (including project managers and purchasing), six functions plus eight subsidiaries at Helvetic, and five functions plus 40 reporting units at GlobalChem. The coordination work grows faster than the analysis.
- **Controls become separate roles.** Confirmation matching, user-rights review and limit monitoring are one person's side tasks at small companies and a dedicated middle/back office at large ones.

</Callout>

| Workflow | Small (Kleio) | SME (Alpine) | Mid-market (Helvetic) | Multinational (GlobalChem) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who builds the cash forecast | CFO | Head of Accounting | Treasury Analyst | Cash & liquidity team + 40 reporting units |
| Who trades FX | CFO (spot conversions) | CFO | Treasury Manager | FX desk (Financial Risk) |
| Who sends supplier payments | Accountant, CFO releases | Head of Accounting, CFO co-signs | Local finance in each subsidiary | Payment factory in the SSC |
| Who reconciles bank accounts | Accountant | Accounting team | Local and group accounting | SSC record-to-report + treasury back office |
| Who manages bank relationships | CFO | CFO | Treasury Manager (CFO for the RCF banks) | Group Treasurer + desk heads |

## Caveats

<Callout type="warning" title="This is a starting hypothesis, not a fact about any company">

The matrices describe the fictional reference companies, chosen to be typical. Real companies differ because of history (who was there when the task appeared), systems (a TMS moves work into treasury; an SSC moves it out), regulation (restricted currencies), and personalities. Rows vary most for payment execution, bank reconciliation, the ownership of the 13-week forecast (treasury vs controlling), and debt (treasury vs CFO vs corporate finance). No survey measures these splits across companies; the assignments are synthesis from practitioner descriptions and the ACT's segregation-of-duties principles[](cite:act-wiki-office-structure).

</Callout>

<Callout type="interview" title="Use the matrix in an interview">

Pick the three or four rows that matter for your topic, show the version closest to the company's size, and ask: *"Where is your company different?"* People correct a wrong picture far more readily than they describe a blank one, and the corrections are the insight. Follow with the hand-off questions on [Workflow hand-offs](/org/handoffs).

</Callout>
