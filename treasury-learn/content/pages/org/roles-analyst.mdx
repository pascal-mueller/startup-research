## What the role is

An analyst turns scattered information (bank statements, subsidiary emails, ERP payment lists, bank confirmations) into something a manager can decide on: a position with proposed transfers, a forecast with a variance table, a hedge-cover report against the policy band. In small teams the analyst also **enters** transactions, and someone senior releases them. That split is how a two-person treasury achieves [four-eyes control](term:four-eyes-principle) — and [why Lea prepares while Daniel decides](why:prepare-decide-split).

Career guides describe the same entry point. Analyst-level roles centre on short-term cash forecasting, bank reconciliations, payment processing and data analysis. After a few years people move up to Senior Analyst or Treasury Manager[](cite:learnsignal-career-2026). In the ACT's competency framework, analysts sit mainly at the *tactical* and *operational* levels[](cite:act-competency-framework).

<RoleExists id="treasury-analyst" />

<Callout type="confusion" title="Analyst ≠ junior accountant, ≠ FP&A analyst">

An FP&A analyst models revenue, costs and budget variances. A treasury analyst models **cash timing, bank balances and financial exposures**. Accountants reconcile what was *booked*. Treasury analysts track what is *in the bank and when*. The two overlap on bank reconciliation, which at mid-market companies is usually still done by accounting.

</Callout>

## Concrete examples of analyst work

These are drawn from Lea's work at [Helvetic](co:helvetic) (one analyst, reporting to Daniel) and from analysts on GlobalChem's desks.

| Task | What it physically looks like | Output | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Statement check** | 07:45: confirm all 12 prior-day statements (MT940/[camt.053](term:bank-statement)) arrived in the connectivity tool. The Chinese bank's file is missing, and since China is ahead of CET it should be in, so she opens a ticket with the bank. | Completeness list | Daniel; the day's position |
| **Cash position** | Refresh the Power Query in the positioning workbook. Add today's AP runs, payroll and tax, and any flows subsidiaries mentioned by email. Compare against yesterday's expectation. | Projected closing balance per account, list of variances, proposed transfers | Daniel decides by 09:00 |
| **Variance investigation** | HM Deutschland is EUR 0.3m lower than expected. She checks the statement detail and finds a trade-tax prepayment. She emails Jens to confirm and adds the date to the tax calendar. | Explained variance; corrected calendar | Forecast quality |
| **13-week forecast** | Monday: roll the model forward and send templates. Tuesday noon: collect them and chase France. Wednesday: consolidate and build the variance table by entity and category. | Consolidated forecast, variance table | Daniel overrides; a one-page view goes to Thomas [every Thursday](why:weekly-vs-monthly-forecast) |
| **FX exposure and hedge report** | Take subsidiaries' monthly currency forecasts (in by WD5) and the list of open forwards. Compute cover per currency and month against the [graded policy bands](why:hedge-bands-graded) (Q+1 60–90% … Q+4 0–40%). | Hedge-ratio workbook; "needs top-up" list | Daniel reviews on WD7, then deals |
| **Confirmation matching** | Compare the bank's PDF confirmation (rate, amount, value date, settlement account) with the deal log, and flag any difference immediately. | Matched / unmatched list | Control. At GlobalChem this is back-office work |
| **Intercompany loan register** | Record each transfer as a loan draw or repayment, compute interest at the agreed rate, and send month-end statements to the subsidiaries. | Register; interest statements | Group Accounting (Claudia), Tax |
| **Month-end pack** | Deal list with bank valuations, deposits, RCF drawings and interest, intercompany balances. | Excel files for accounting | Claudia's team books them |
| **Ad hoc analysis** | "What does a 5% EUR/CHF drop do to next year's EBIT after hedges?" "How much did we pay each bank in fees last year?" "Which subsidiaries forecast worst?" | Short Excel analysis | Daniel, Thomas |
| **Systems work** | Map a new bank account into the connectivity tool, fix a broken statement import, maintain macros. | Working tools | Everyone |

<Callout type="actually" title="What 'prepare the decision' means in practice">

Lea does not send Daniel a spreadsheet. She sends three lines. "HM DE short EUR 1.35m after tax prepayment (confirmed with Jens). Options: (1) EUR 1.5m IC loan from HM AG EUR, recommended; (2) from Italy, but Italy pays suppliers Thursday; (3) overdraft, against policy. Suzhou statement still missing, ticket open." Daniel's decision takes two minutes because the analysis is done. That preparation is most of the analyst's value.

</Callout>

## Interview implications

<Callout type="interview" title="Why analysts are underrated interviewees">

For a founder studying where the manual work is, analysts are often the most **precise** interviewees. They can tell you which file arrives at 07:40, which bank portal times out, how many minutes the consolidation takes and which tab of the workbook nobody dares touch. Managers compress all that into "we do a daily position".

Good questions:
- "Show me (or describe) the workbook. Which tabs do you touch every morning, and in what order?"
- "What did you copy and paste today?"
- "Which subsidiary do you chase most, and what do you do when they don't answer?"
- "What does your manager change in what you prepare, and why?"
- "When did something you prepared turn out to be wrong? How was it caught?"

Less useful: "Why is the hedge policy set in graded bands?" or "How did you choose your banks?" Those are manager or CFO questions.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">

Analysts may be less free to talk. Ask the manager for permission, and don't ask for internal files. Descriptions are enough.

</Callout>
