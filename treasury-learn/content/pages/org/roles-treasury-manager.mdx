## One title, four different jobs

The same title on a LinkedIn profile can describe very different jobs. Before you interpret anything a Treasury Manager says, work out which of these they are:

| Pattern | Typical company | Reports to | Team | What "Treasury Manager" means here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The treasurer in all but name** | Mid-market group, CHF 300m–2bn revenue, 5–20 subsidiaries ([Helvetic](co:helvetic)) | CFO | 0–2 analysts | Runs all of treasury: daily cash, forecast, FX hedging, intercompany funding, banks, RCF, covenants, policy drafts, CFO reporting. Decides within a mandate; the CFO decides above it. |
| **B. Number two under a Head of Treasury** | Larger mid-market or smaller multinational, 3–8 treasury staff | Head of Treasury / Group Treasurer | 1–3 analysts | Runs the operational core (cash, forecast, payments controls, often FX execution); the head of treasury takes funding, banks, policy and the CFO. |
| **C. Specialist inside a desk** | Multinational with specialised teams ([GlobalChem](co:globalchem)) | Desk head (e.g. Head of Cash & Liquidity) or, in US groups, an Assistant Treasurer | 0–2 analysts | Owns one process end to end — e.g. cash pools and in-house bank funding, or FX hedge execution — within the desk's limits. Does not set policy or own bank relationships at group level. |
| **D. First and only treasury hire** | SME or scale-up with a large credit line, lumpy cash or heavy FX | CFO or Head of Finance | none | Everything in A, plus usually payments administration, bank KYC and sometimes insurance or leasing. More operational, less strategic than the title suggests. |

<Callout type="confusion" title="Treasury Manager in a bank is a different job">

In banks, "treasury" means managing the bank's own balance sheet (asset-liability management, regulatory liquidity ratios, funding the loan book). A "Treasury Manager" at a bank, or a "Treasury Relationship Manager", which is a bank *sales* title[](cite:job-bank-treasury-rm), is not the person this manual is about. Check the employer before booking an interview.

</Callout>

<Callout type="field" title="What real job advertisements ask for">

- SIX Group's Zurich ad for a Treasury Manager describes someone who "supports and oversees treasury operations and financial risk management". The role links Treasury, Finance and the banks, takes "day-to-day" responsibility for cash management, bank relationship management and hedging, maintains the TMS and joins strategic projects. It asks for about three years of treasury or cash-management experience[](cite:job-six-treasury-manager). That is roughly pattern B.
- A Kraków ad for a Treasury Manager in a large group covers intercompany funding and cash repatriation across 38 countries, identifying FX exposures with business units, and maintaining cash-pooling arrangements[](cite:job-krakow-treasury-manager). That is pattern C: a specialist slice of a big treasury.
- The ACT maps treasury competencies to four job levels: tactical, operational, managerial, strategic[](cite:act-competency-framework). Treasury Managers sit mostly at operational/managerial. Pattern A people also do strategic work, because nobody else is there to do it.

These are single-employer adverts. They show the range, not a statistic.

</Callout>

## A normal Wednesday for Daniel (pattern A)

[Helvetic Machines](co:helvetic): CHF 650m revenue, 8 subsidiaries, 5 banks, 12 operating accounts, a CHF 200m RCF (~CHF 60m drawn), a CHF 100m bond due 2028. Treasury is Daniel (Treasury Manager) and Lea (Treasury Analyst), reporting to Thomas (CFO). There is **no cash pool**: Daniel concentrates cash manually by [intercompany loan](term:intercompany-loan). Subsidiaries run their own supplier payments locally. The [Tuesday morning on the Start page](/start) shows a positioning-heavy day. Wednesday is forecast day.

<Callout type="actually" title="Wednesday, 08:30–17:30">

1. **08:30** — Lea's position is ready: group cash CHF 43m. HM Deutschland is EUR 0.4m short for Thursday's payroll top-up. Daniel accepts her proposal to send EUR 1.2m from HM AG's EUR account as an intercompany loan and asks her to add it to the loan register. He does not rebuild the numbers. He reads her three flagged variances and asks one question: is the CHF 180k debit on HM AG's account really the insurance premium? Lea confirms it against the invoice.
2. **09:10** — He releases the transfer in the connectivity tool (Lea entered it, so two people are involved). He also releases a batch of 41 Swiss supplier payments prepared by accounting. He holds one: a supplier whose IBAN changed last week. He asks accounting to confirm the change by phone on a number from the supplier master file, not the one in the email.
3. **10:00–11:30** — **13-week forecast review.** Seven of eight subsidiaries submitted by Tuesday noon; France came in this morning. Lea's variance table shows HM Italia over-forecast receipts again. Daniel keeps Italy's 15% haircut on weeks 5–13. He moves a CNY 8m dividend from HM Suzhou out of week 4: Wei says the local tax clearance is not done. He also adds the bond coupon, which a copied template had dropped. New low point: CHF 17m in week 6, against the CHF 10m minimum Thomas wants. No RCF action needed.
4. **11:30** — The CHF surplus at the parent will not earn anything on deposit (CHF rates are about 0%). Daniel pencils in a CHF 5m repayment of the RCF drawing at next Friday's rollover. He sends a two-line email to Thomas, because repaying and later redrawing has a cost if the forecast is wrong.
5. **13:30** — **FX.** New US orders raised the USD exposure for Q1. Lea's hedge workbook shows Q1 USD cover at 52%, below the 60% floor in the policy. Daniel asks two RCF banks for prices on a USD 3m forward sale for March. He deals with the better one. The other bank is 0.0004 behind, and Daniel notes that it has won less business this year. The deal is within his [dealing mandate](term:dealing-mandate), so it needs no further approval. Lea will match the [confirmation](term:deal-confirmation) tomorrow.
6. **14:30** — Call with Jens (HM Deutschland): the German house bank wants refreshed [KYC](term:kyc) documents for the signatories. Daniel collects the passport copies and the extract from the commercial register and sends them to the bank.
7. **15:30** — TMS evaluation: a demo from a mid-market cloud TMS. Daniel checks the things he actually does every day. Can it import the Chinese bank's statements? Can it keep the intercompany loan register and calculate interest? Can it show hedge cover by month against the policy band?
8. **17:00** — Prepares Thursday's one-page note for Thomas: cash, low point, hedge ratio, the proposed RCF repayment, the Italian receivables pattern, and the Suzhou dividend delay.

</Callout>

In that day Daniel prepared almost none of the data himself. He **decided** five things: the funding transfer, the payment hold, the forecast overrides, the RCF repayment proposal and the USD hedge. He **executed** two: releasing payments and dealing FX. He **escalated** two, to Thomas: the Italian customer pattern and the repayment. The rest was talking to people.

## Who prepares, who executes, who decides — by workflow

At Helvetic (pattern A). In a multinational, the "Daniel" column is split across several desk heads and specialist Treasury Managers.

| Workflow | Lea (analyst) prepares | Daniel does himself | Daniel reviews / approves | Escalated to Thomas (CFO) when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Statement check, position, variances, proposed transfers | Decides transfers; releases them | Lea's variance explanations | Group cash would fall below the CHF 10m minimum, or an RCF drawing is needed |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Template roll-forward, chasing, consolidation, variance table | Overrides and haircuts; presents monthly | The consolidated forecast | The low point approaches the minimum; a large, permanent variance |
| [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Exposure and hedge-ratio workbook; confirmation matching | Gets quotes and deals forwards within his mandate | Exposure reported by subsidiaries | Deal size or tenor exceeds the mandate; an option or change of hedge band; a big mark-to-market move |
| [Funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary) | Loan register, interest calculation | Decides amount and form (loan vs dividend) | The subsidiary's request | New long-term loans, capital injections, anything with tax consequences (with Tax) |
| [Payment processing](wf:payment-processing) | Enters treasury transfers | Second signature on HM AG runs and treasury transfers | Changed beneficiaries, unusual items | Payments above CHF 2m need Thomas's signature; any suspected fraud |
| [Bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) | Signatory list, document collection | Opens/closes accounts; KYC; mandates | Local requests | Every new bank or account (Thomas co-approves) |
| [Credit facility](wf:credit-facility-management) / [covenants](wf:covenant-monitoring) | Debt schedule, covenant workbook | Drawings and repayments; agent-bank contact | Claudia's EBITDA figures | Thin covenant headroom; refinancing (the 2028 bond) |
| [Month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) | Deal lists, valuations, intercompany interest | Monthly treasury report | Accounting reconciliation | Always goes to Thomas; he decides what reaches the board |
| [Policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance) | Monitoring against limits | Drafts policy changes | Breaches | Every breach and every policy change |

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Operational vs strategic time — a rough picture, not a survey figure">

For a pattern-A Treasury Manager, the week is mostly operational. Positioning, forecast, hedging routine, payments and bank admin probably take well over half of the time. Most of the rest goes to projects (a TMS selection, a bank-account rationalisation like Helvetic's 2024 cut from 31 to 12 accounts), preparing the CFO's decisions and the occasional refinancing. The strategic part is real, but it happens in bursts: an RCF renewal, a bond, a policy rewrite. A pattern-C specialist is even more operational, but in a narrower area. This split is our synthesis from job adverts and typical practice. Ask your interviewee for their own split. The answer tells you which pattern they are.

</Callout>

## Where the judgment sits

Most of the Treasury Manager's value is in a small number of recurring judgment calls. The data work around them can increasingly be automated; the calls themselves rest on context that is rarely written down.

- **Is this variance noise, timing or a problem?** A CHF 2m receipt one day late from a customer who always pays late is noise. An unknown CHF 200k debit is not. The difference is knowing the customers, the banks and the subsidiaries.
- **Whose forecast to believe.** Daniel haircuts Italy because it over-forecast in 9 of 12 weeks. He does not haircut Germany. Overriding a subsidiary also has a political cost.
- **How much buffer to keep, and where.** The CHF 10m minimum is a CFO decision. Keeping EUR 250k extra in Germany before a payroll week is Daniel's call.
- **When to hedge within the band.** The policy says 60–90%. Whether to top up to 65% today or wait for a better rate is inside the mandate, and it is judgment. Many companies layer hedges mechanically to take this judgment out.
- **Act before cut-off without full information, or wait.** A missed payroll costs more than an unnecessary transfer. An unnecessary RCF drawing costs interest and commitment fees.
- **What to escalate.** A good Treasury Manager escalates early and rarely. The CFO should never learn about a problem from the bank.

## Interview implications

<Callout type="interview" title="First two minutes: which pattern is this person?">

Ask these before anything else:

1. "Who do you report to, and how many people are in treasury?" Reporting to the CFO with 0–2 people means pattern A or D. Reporting to a Head of Treasury means B. Reporting to a desk head means C.
2. "Do you deal FX or place deposits yourself, or does someone else?" This tells you whether they execute or only prepare.
3. "Who executes supplier payments: treasury, local finance, or a shared-service centre?" This tells you how centralised the company is.
4. "Walk me through what you did this morning, from the moment you opened your laptop." Pattern A describes positioning and decisions. Pattern C describes one process in depth.

</Callout>

**Pattern A and D Treasury Managers are among the best first interviews in the whole profession.** They do the operational work and make the judgment calls, and they know why the CFO decides what he decides. They also usually built the spreadsheets themselves. Good questions for them:

- "Walk me through the last 13-week forecast. Which subsidiary's input did you change, and why?"
- "When did you last escalate something to the CFO? What happened?"
- "What does your analyst do that you'd never want to do again? What do you still check yourself?"
- "Which parts of your Excel set-up would break if you left for a month?"
- "What would you never let a system do without you seeing it first?"

**What they cannot answer well:** board-level discussions of capital allocation, M&A pipelines, or, in pattern C, how the other desks work. For how the numbers are physically produced (file timings, copy-paste steps), their analyst is often more precise. See [Treasury Analyst](/org/roles-analyst).

<Callout type="warning" title="Common interview mistakes with this role">

- Assuming a pattern-C Treasury Manager at a multinational knows the whole treasury. They know their desk.
- Assuming a pattern-A Treasury Manager has a TMS, a cash pool or a back office. Many have none. Helvetic has no pool and no TMS yet.
- Asking about "treasury strategy" in the abstract. Ask about the last decision instead.
- Confusing them with the controller or FP&A. Treasury Managers forecast **cash timing**, not profit. See [Treasury and its neighbours](/start/neighbours).

</Callout>
