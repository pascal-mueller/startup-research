## Who owns what

| | [FP&A](term:fpa) (and DACH "Controlling") | Treasury |
|---|---|---|
| **Main forecast** | Revenue, margins, costs, EBITDA — monthly, budget year plus 1–3 year plan | Cash receipts and payments — daily/weekly for ~13 weeks, monthly further out |
| **Basis** | Accruals (when revenue is earned, cost incurred) | Cash (when money moves, by [value date](term:value-date)) |
| **Customers** | CEO, business units, board | CFO, banks (covenants, headroom), board (liquidity) |
| **FX** | Sets [budget rates](term:budget-rate); explains FX variances in the P&L | Measures exposure against the forecast; hedges; reports hedge rates achieved |
| **Capex** | Plans and approves projects | Times the cash out; funds it |
| **Medium-term cash** | Provides the plan | Converts it to cash ([indirect method](term:indirect-method)); owns liquidity and funding conclusions |

## What moves between them

| From → to | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| FP&A → treasury | Budget and rolling forecast (revenue, EBITDA, capex, working-capital assumptions) | Monthly / quarterly |
| FP&A → treasury | Budget FX rates and currency split of the plan | Annually, with updates |
| Treasury → FP&A | Short-term cash forecast and actual cash vs forecast | Weekly / monthly |
| Treasury → FP&A | Hedged rates for the coming year; interest cost forecast | When hedges are placed; at budget time |
| Both → CFO | Two views of the future that must tell one story | Monthly |

## Common confusions

<Callout type="confusion" title="Two forecasts, both right">

FP&A at [Helvetic](co:helvetic) shows Q3 EBITDA of CHF 22m. Treasury shows Q3 net cash flow of −CHF 4m. Both are right: customers pay on 60–90-day terms, the CHF 9m tax prepayment falls in Q3, and inventory is building for Q4 deliveries. The CFO needs both. A beginner's mistake is to ask a treasurer about "the forecast" without saying which one — see also [Treasury and its neighbours](/start/neighbours).

</Callout>

<Callout type="confusion" title="Who owns the 13-week forecast?">

At most groups with a treasurer, treasury owns it. But in SMEs and in companies where controlling is strong (common in DACH), the controller may own a monthly cash forecast and treasury only the next few weeks — or the CFO asks FP&A because FP&A has the headcount. Ask who produces it, not who "should".

</Callout>

## Where it breaks

- **Different assumptions.** FP&A assumes a big order in September; the subsidiary told treasury it slipped to November. Nobody reconciles until the CFO sees both.
- **Budget rates vs hedging.** If FP&A set EUR/CHF at 0.94 and spot is 0.93, the business asks treasury why it "lost" money — even if the hedges did exactly what the policy required. Clear agreement on what hedging is supposed to protect (budget rate? margin? cash?) prevents this.
- **Medium-term gaps.** Treasury's weeks 9–13 and months 4–12 depend on FP&A's plan being translated into cash; payment terms, tax timing and capex phasing are often missing from the plan.
- **Timing.** FP&A's monthly forecast arrives after the close, treasury needs it earlier to plan funding.

<Example title="Reconciling the two views at Helvetic" company="helvetic" open>

Every month, Lea (Treasury Analyst) and a member of Marco's FP&A team compare the treasury forecast for months 2–4 with FP&A's rolling forecast converted to cash. In October the gap is CHF 11m:

| Item | FP&A-based cash view | Treasury forecast | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer receipts | CHF 162m | CHF 155m | Treasury uses subsidiaries' view of actual payer behaviour; two Italian customers pay 30 days late |
| Capex | CHF 12m | CHF 14m | New machining centre deposit falls earlier than FP&A phased it |
| Tax | CHF 6m | CHF 8m | Tax's calendar has a German prepayment FP&A did not phase |
| **Net difference** | | **−CHF 11m** | |

Lea documents the gap; Daniel decides the treasury view is more reliable for months 2–3 but uses FP&A's growth assumptions for months 4+. Marco adjusts the working-capital assumption for Italy. Thomas sees one reconciled number and a note, not two competing forecasts.

</Example>

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions to ask">

- "How does your cash forecast relate to FP&A's forecast — who reconciles them?"
- "Who sets the budget rate, and how is hedging performance judged against it?"
- "When the forecasts disagreed last, whose was right and why?"

</Callout>
