## Who owns what

| Topic | Tax owns | Treasury owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Intercompany loans** | Arm's-length interest rate ([transfer pricing](term:transfer-pricing)), [thin-capitalisation](term:thin-capitalisation) limits, documentation | Amount, timing, currency, the loan agreement (with legal), execution |
| **Cash pooling** | Pool leader remuneration, allocation of pool benefit, deductibility, local restrictions | Pool design with banks, operation, balances |
| **Dividends and [repatriation](term:repatriation)** | [Withholding tax](term:withholding-tax), treaty relief, timing constraints | When cash is needed centrally, FX conversion, transfers |
| **Tax payments** | Amounts and calendar | Having the cash in the right entity on the due date |
| **Treasury centres, IHB, re-invoicing** | Substance, location, incentives, VAT for [COBO](term:cobo)/[POBO](term:pobo) and re-invoicing | Operating model and the case for doing it |
| **FX and hedging** | Tax treatment of derivative gains and losses | Hedging decisions |

<Callout type="concept" title="Why tax got more involved in treasury after 2020">

In February 2020 the OECD added Chapter X to its Transfer Pricing Guidelines, its first dedicated guidance on financial transactions: treasury functions, intra-group loans, cash pooling, hedging, financial guarantees and captive insurance[](cite:oecd-tpg-chx-roedl). It expects cash-pool benefits to be shared among members and the pool leader to be rewarded according to its actual functions and risks, and it has raised documentation expectations. The practical effect: tax departments now ask treasury for more evidence about why a loan has a given rate and who in the group really bears the risk.

</Callout>

## What moves between them

| From → to | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| Tax → treasury | Tax payment calendar by entity (prepayments, final payments, VAT) | Annually + updates; feeds the [cash forecast](wf:cash-forecasting) |
| Tax → treasury | Interest rates for IC loans (often safe-harbour or benchmark studies), equity vs debt guidance | Per loan / annually |
| Tax → treasury | Withholding-tax cost of dividend vs loan repayment routes | Per repatriation |
| Treasury → tax | New or changed IC loans, pool members, netting flows, guarantees | Per transaction; year-end list |
| Treasury → tax | Plans for pools, IHB, regional centre, repatriation | Early in the project |

## Where it breaks

- **After the fact.** Treasury lends EUR 5m to a subsidiary on Friday because it is short; tax finds out at year-end the rate is off-market or local thin-cap rules make part of the interest non-deductible.
- **Trapped cash.** A subsidiary has surplus cash but the only exit routes (dividend, loan, fees) cost withholding tax or need approvals — treasury sees an idle balance, tax sees a cost, and nothing happens. See [trapped cash](term:trapped-cash).
- **Pool surprises.** A cash pool is set up for operational reasons; tax later objects to how interest is allocated among members.
- **Payment calendar gaps.** A large tax prepayment is missing from the forecast because nobody told treasury the amount changed.

<Example title="Funding HM Italia" company="helvetic" open>

HM Italia needs EUR 5m for six months: a large Italian customer pays late and a new production line is being installed. Daniel ([Helvetic](co:helvetic) Treasury Manager) prefers an intercompany loan from the parent because the RCF is drawn in CHF and the parent has EUR from the German entity's surplus.

1. **Daniel → group tax** (Monday): amount, tenor, currency, purpose. Asks for the rate and any constraints.
2. **Group tax** checks HM Italia's existing debt, Italian interest-deductibility limits and the group's transfer-pricing policy; proposes a rate based on the group's benchmark, and checks whether Italian withholding tax applies to interest paid to the Swiss parent and which relief (treaty or Swiss–EU agreement) is available.
3. Tax suggests EUR 3m as a loan and EUR 2m as a capital contribution because of the interest limit — which needs an Italian shareholder resolution (legal) and CFO approval.
4. **Thomas (CFO)** approves; **legal** prepares the loan agreement and resolution; **Lea** prepares the transfer; **Daniel** and **Thomas** release it.
5. **Claudia's team** and **Giulia** (HM Italia finance) book both sides; Lea adds the loan to the IC schedule for month-end.

Total elapsed time: four working days, three of them waiting for tax and legal. Treasury's frustration ("we just need to move EUR 5m") and tax's caution ("this sets a precedent for the Italian tax audit") are both legitimate.

</Example>

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions to ask">

- To treasury: "When do you involve tax — before or after you move money between entities?" "Where do you have cash you cannot move?"
- To tax: "What treasury transactions give you the most work?" "What do you wish treasury told you earlier?"
