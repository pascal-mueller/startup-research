## What the CFO owns in treasury

Whatever the size, the CFO answers to the CEO and the board for four things:

1. **The company does not run out of cash.** Liquidity, headroom and minimum cash levels.
2. **Funding is in place and affordable.** Credit lines, bonds, covenants, bank relationships at the top level.
3. **Financial risks are within appetite.** FX, interest rates, counterparties, fraud.
4. **Controls work.** Who can sign, deal and move money, and whether the policy is followed.

What varies is how much of the **work** behind those four things the CFO does personally.

## What escalates to the CFO

Escalations are the best way to understand how a CFO relates to treasury. Typical triggers, in practice and in treasury policies:

- **Liquidity:** the forecast low point breaks the minimum cash level or headroom target, or an RCF drawing is needed. See [liquidity crisis](wf:liquidity-crisis).
- **Anything outside the mandate:** hedges larger or longer than the treasurer may deal, a new instrument such as options, a new counterparty, a new bank.
- **Large or unusual payments:** above a signing threshold (a single payment above CHF 2m at [Helvetic](co:helvetic) needs Thomas and the CEO [together](why:ceo-cosign-threshold)), or unusual in pattern. See [large payment approval](wf:large-payment-approval).
- **Covenant headroom thinning**, rating pressure, refinancing timing.
- **Losses and incidents:** fraud attempts that got far, material FX losses, a bank failure or downgrade, a failed payroll.
- **Tax-driven cash moves:** dividends and repatriation from subsidiaries (with the Head of Tax).

<Callout type="synthesis" title="What a good escalation looks like">

At every size, CFOs want the treasury person to arrive with a recommendation, not a question. At Helvetic, Daniel writes "Group low point CHF 18m in week 4 — December payrolls including the 13th salary — against the CHF 10m minimum, so no action needed; I propose repaying CHF 5m of the RCF at the [next 15th rollover](why:fifteenth-rollover-repayment) because CHF deposits earn about 0%." Thomas replies "OK". The judgment is Daniel's; the accountability is Thomas's.

</Callout>

## Relationship with the Head of Treasury

- **Mid-market:** the treasury manager or head of treasury reports directly to the CFO and meets them weekly or fortnightly. (Field impression, not a measured fact: many practitioners describe the CFO as a former auditor or controller who relies on treasury for market and banking expertise.)
- **Multinational:** the Group Treasurer reports to the CFO. Contact is a monthly report, a quarterly treasury committee and joint bank and rating-agency meetings. The Group Treasurer is often a candidate for CFO roles later.
- **US variant:** the Treasurer and the Controller often both report to the CFO as peers. Payments and bank reconciliation may sit under the Controller, and funding and investments under the Treasurer.

<Callout type="field" title="Where treasury reports">

Treasury nearly always reports into the CFO, occasionally through a deputy CFO or VP Finance in large US groups. It rarely reports to the CEO directly. See [Organisational structures](/org/structures) for variants.

</Callout>

## When the CFO is — and isn't — the right interviewee

<Callout type="interview" title="Choose the CFO by company size">

- **Startup or SME (under ~CHF 150m revenue, no treasurer):** the CFO is **the best interviewee**. They do or directly supervise the work. Ask them to walk through last week's cash review, the last FX decision and the last bank conversation. At SMEs, also interview the head of accounting (Petra), who does the hands-on work.
- **Mid-market with a treasury manager:** the CFO is a **good second interview**, for why things are organised as they are, what they escalate, budget for tools and appetite for change. For how the work is done, start with the treasury manager.
- **Multinational:** the CFO is **usually too far removed** for workflow discovery. They will describe treasury as it is presented to them, polished. They are useful for strategic priorities, buying decisions and board concerns. For operational reality, talk to desk heads, managers and analysts.

</Callout>

Good CFO questions:
- "What was the last treasury decision that reached you? What did you need to decide it?"
- "What would make you uncomfortable about a system moving money without a person reviewing it?"
- "When did treasury last surprise you, good or bad?"
- "If you could add one person to treasury, what would they do?"

Avoid asking a large-company CFO how the daily cash position is built. They probably don't know, and the answer will be generic.
