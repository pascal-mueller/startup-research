## Title differences

| Title | Where you see it | What it usually signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Group Treasurer** | UK, Swiss and European groups with many subsidiaries | Head of treasury for the whole group. The word "Group" signals subsidiaries and central control |
| **Corporate Treasurer** | Used interchangeably with Group Treasurer; sometimes to distinguish from a business-unit or regional treasurer | Same role; "corporate" = head office |
| **Treasurer / VP Treasury** | US companies | Often a corporate officer named in the bylaws, with legal authority to sign financing documents. Below them come an Assistant Treasurer (or several) and Treasury Managers |
| **Head of Treasury / Leiter Treasury** | Generic; very common in DACH and mid-sized companies | Could be a 25-person department or a one-person function. Always ask about team size |
| **Treasury Manager (acting as head)** | Mid-market groups like [Helvetic](co:helvetic) | Does the head-of-treasury job without the title. See [Treasury Manager, pattern A](/org/roles-treasury-manager) |
| **Deputy Group Treasurer** | Large groups | Usually runs operations (cash, ops, systems) so the Group Treasurer can focus on funding, banks and the CFO |

<Callout type="confusion" title="Treasurer of a club vs corporate treasurer">

Searching "treasurer" returns a lot about associations and charities, where the treasurer is the bookkeeper. A corporate treasurer is not a bookkeeper. In most large companies they never post a journal entry.

</Callout>

## Strategic and operational responsibilities

In a large department, the Group Treasurer oversees everything without being the expert in every area and relies on specialists in the team. The ACT's competency framework maps this kind of role to its *strategic* level[](cite:act-competency-framework). The European associations' *Guiding Principles* frame the function's responsibilities: liquidity first, then financial risk, funding and control[](cite:eact-guiding-principles).

| Area | Strategic (sets the frame) | Operational (runs within it) | Delegated at GlobalChem to | Delegated at Helvetic to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Minimum cash, headroom targets, pooling/IHB design | Daily positioning, funding transfers | Priya (Cash & Liquidity) | Nobody: Daniel does it, Lea prepares |
| Funding | Funding plan, maturity profile, bond timing, RCF size | Drawings, CP issuance, covenant data | Olivier (Corporate Finance) | Daniel, with Thomas deciding |
| Financial risk | Hedging policy, hedge ratios, instruments | Exposure collection, dealing | Jonas (Financial Risk) | Daniel deals; Lea reports |
| Banking | Bank panel, wallet allocation, fee strategy | KYC, mandates, accounts | Anna herself + Olivier; ops by Marta | Daniel; Thomas for top banks |
| Investments | Investment policy, counterparty limits | Deposits, MMFs | Priya | Rare: CHF surplus repays the RCF |
| Controls | Policy, signing and dealing mandates, segregation | Confirmations, reconciliations, user rights | Marta (Operations) | Split across Daniel, Lea, accounting |
| Systems | Roadmap, budget | TMS administration | Ben | Daniel (TMS evaluation) |
| Reporting | What the CFO and board see | Producing the reports | All desk heads | Daniel, from Lea's data |

<Callout type="synthesis" title="The test: what does this person do at 08:30?">

At GlobalChem, Anna reads a dashboard and an exception list and asks one or two questions. At Helvetic, the equivalent person, Daniel, is making the day's funding decisions from Lea's position. The title may be similar, but the jobs are different. Operational load falls as the team grows. External work (banks, rating agencies, investors) and organisational work (people, projects, audit) rise.

</Callout>

## What stays with the Head of Treasury even in large teams

- **The CFO relationship and the treasury committee.** Proposals go up through them.
- **Core-bank relationships.** Heads of treasury typically spend meaningful time with the relationship banks, especially those lending in the RCF. Practitioner guidance puts bank relationship management within the treasurer's purview, using wallet-share analysis and scorecards[](cite:ctm-brm-leading-practices).
- **Policy ownership** and sign-off on exceptions.
- **Crisis leadership:** bank failure, a large fraud, a sudden funding need.
- **People and organisation:** hiring, what goes to the shared-service centre, what stays in head office.

## Interview implications

<Callout type="interview">

- **Small team (2–4 people):** an excellent interviewee for both strategy and operations. Treat them like a pattern-A Treasury Manager with more authority.
- **Large department:** the best source for *why* treasury is organised the way it is, what the function struggles with, where budget goes, what the audit committee asks about, and what they would buy. For how the work is done, ask them to introduce you to a desk head or analyst. Senior treasurers usually agree if you ask specifically ("the person who builds the daily position").
- Questions that work: "Which decisions still come to you personally, and why haven't you delegated them?" "What would you move to Kraków [the SSC] if you trusted the controls?" "What did your last audit find?" "Which project in the last three years failed, and why?"
- Avoid: asking about specific files or spreadsheets. They will answer from memory of how it used to work.

</Callout>
