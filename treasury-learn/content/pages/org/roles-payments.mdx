## Five questions for any payment

For every supplier payment, ask separately:

1. **Who decides it should be paid?** Usually AP, after invoice approval and three-way match, following payment terms that procurement negotiated.
2. **Who prepares it?** AP builds the [payment run](term:payment-run) in the ERP.
3. **Who approves / signs it?** Authorised signatories under the [signing authority matrix](term:signing-authority-matrix), often two people. Treasury or the CFO for large items.
4. **Who executes it?** The file goes to the bank through e-banking, EBICS, host-to-host or SWIFT, sometimes via a [payment factory](term:payment-factory).
5. **Who reconciles it?** Accounting or the SSC matches the bank debit to the open item.

Treasury's role is to make sure the money is there ([cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning)), that channels and controls work, and that unusual or treasury-originated payments (intercompany loans, deal settlements) are handled.

## Payment factory, SSC, finance operations — what's the difference?

- A **[shared-service centre](term:shared-service-centre)** performs many finance processes (AP, AR, accounting) for many entities.
- A **payment factory** centralises payment *execution* for many entities through one process and one set of bank connections. It is often described as a shared-service centre for payments[](cite:dbs-payment-factory). It may sit inside the SSC, inside treasury operations, or be mostly a system.
- **Payments-on-behalf-of (POBO)**: the payment factory pays suppliers from central (in-house bank) accounts on behalf of subsidiaries.
- **"Finance operations"** is a newer umbrella term, common in tech companies, that can include AP, payments and billing.

<Callout type="survey">

PwC's 2025 treasury survey reports that among organisations above USD 10bn revenue, 60% operate a payment factory and 50% a payments-on-behalf-of model[](cite:pwc-gts-2025). Below the large-multinational tier these structures are much less common.

</Callout>

## What a payments lead actually does (multinational)

- Owns payment formats (e.g. [pain.001](term:pain-001)), status reports ([pain.002](term:pain-002)) and channel set-up with the systems team.
- Runs validation: duplicate checks, [sanctions screening](term:sanctions-screening), format checks, cut-off management.
- Handles rejected and returned payments, urgent manual payments, and bank incidents.
- Enforces the fraud controls: [call-back verification](term:call-back-verification) of changed bank details, dual approval, and holding suspicious payments. See [fraud investigation](wf:fraud-investigation).
- Reports volumes, rejection rates, fraud attempts.

Payment policy (which banks, which channels, which approvals) stays with the Group Treasurer. What gets paid stays with AP and the business.

## Interview implications

<Callout type="interview" title="Don't ask treasury why a supplier was paid late">

- **Why** a payment was made or when it was due: **AP / procurement**.
- **How** it left the company, and which controls applied: **payments lead / treasury operations** (large companies), **head of accounting** (SME), **treasury manager** (mid-market, for treasury payments and channels).
- **Whether** the money was there: **cash manager / treasury manager**.
- **Fraud attempts**: payment operations and AP see them first; the CFO sees the ones that got far.

A useful opener for any of them: "Walk me through the last payment run from invoice to bank debit. Who touched it?"

</Callout>
