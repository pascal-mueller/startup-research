## What Lea's job really is

Lea is the person who turns 12 bank statements, 8 subsidiary templates, SAP exports and a stream of emails into a few numbers Daniel can decide on. Her work is the **preparation half** of almost every workflow in the [Cash & liquidity](/workflows) group:

- **Daily:** statements in, balances checked, known flows added, gaps explained, proposal sent — the [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) workflow. She is also the second treasury signatory on transfers Daniel instructs.
- **Weekly:** roll the [13-week forecast](wf:cash-forecasting), collect and chase submissions, consolidate, compute variances; produce the EUR [hedge-ratio](term:hedge-ratio) report.
- **Monthly:** intercompany interest, bank balance confirmations for accounting, counterparty exposure, data for Daniel's report.
- **Constantly:** fixing data problems. A duplicated statement, a missing file, a subsidiary's copied template.

Her Monday and Tuesday are the upstream half of [Daniel's](/day/daniel). The timelines link to each other at the hand-over points.

<Callout type="field" title="Missing statements are rarely time zones">

For a Swiss company, Asian banks' prior-day statements should be the *first* to arrive — their day ended hours before Europe's. When the Chinese statement is missing at 07:30, it is a connectivity or bank problem (a failed relay, a format change, an expired key), and it should be chased, not waited for. US statements are the ones that legitimately arrive last.

</Callout>

## Interviewing someone like Lea

<Callout type="interview" title="Good questions — and what she won't know">

- "Show me the workbook. Which tabs do you touch every morning, and which ones break?"
- "Which subsidiary do you chase most, and what do they send you?"
- "What did you export from SAP this week, and what did you do to it in Excel?"
- "When you find a gap you can't explain before Daniel needs the proposal, what do you do?"
- "If you were away for two weeks, what would go wrong?"

Analysts are the best source for **what is manual, where data breaks and how long things take** — the details managers have stopped seeing. They usually cannot tell you why the hedging policy has the bands it has, how the RCF was negotiated, or what the CFO worries about. See [Treasury Analyst role](/org/roles-analyst).

</Callout>
