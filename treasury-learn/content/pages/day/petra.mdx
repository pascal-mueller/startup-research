## How treasury fits into Petra's job

Petra's title says accounting, and most of her week is accounting: the close, reconciliations, the auditor, VAT. But Alpine has no treasurer, so the jobs that a treasury team would do fall to her by default — the daily balance check, the twice-weekly [payment runs](term:payment-run), the weekly cash sheet, FX conversions, funding the German and US subsidiaries, and bank KYC paperwork. Martin (CFO) owns every decision that moves real money: drawing the credit line, hedging, subsidiary funding above small amounts. See [treasury and accounting](/org/with-accounting).

Alpine's defining problem is **lumpy project cash**: a robot project pays in milestones (deposit, delivery, acceptance), and one delayed acceptance certificate moves the month by CHF 1–2m. Petra's forecast is essentially a list of milestones with her own estimate of when each will really be paid.

<Callout type="confusion" title="Accounting's view and cash's view of the same project">

The controller recognises revenue on the Duisburg project as work progresses; Petra's cash sheet only counts the EUR 1.9m when the customer signs acceptance and pays. On Monday, the P&L and the cash sheet tell different stories about the same project — which is exactly why an SME needs someone looking at cash separately from the ledger. See [Treasury and its neighbours](/start/neighbours).

</Callout>

## Interviewing someone like Petra

<Callout type="interview" title="Good questions — and what she won't know">

- "Walk me through last Tuesday's payment run from the Business Central journal to the bank's status report."
- "Which of your cash tasks gets squeezed in the first week of the month?"
- "How do you find out when a milestone will really be paid? Who tells you, and how often are they right?"
- "What would you need to see before you trusted a tool to prepare the weekly cash sheet?"

Petra is an excellent source on **how treasury work is actually done without a treasurer**: payments, bank files, the cash sheet, FX tickets, the pain of doing it during the close. She is the wrong person for strategic questions (credit-line negotiation, hedging policy) — ask Martin — and she will describe FX as "what Martin approved", not as a programme. See [who knows what](/org/who-knows-what).

</Callout>
