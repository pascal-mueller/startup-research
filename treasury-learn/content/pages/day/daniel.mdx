## How Daniel's week is built

Helvetic centralised treasury in 2021. There is **no cash pool**: Daniel concentrates cash manually, by [intercompany loans](term:intercompany-loan) from the Swiss parent to the subsidiaries that need money and back. EUR revenues are hedged with rolling [forwards](term:forward) under a policy with bands per quarter. The CHF 200m [RCF](term:rcf) is about CHF 60m drawn, and the CHF 100m bond due 2028 is the big item on the horizon.

His day has three layers:

- **Every morning, decisions before the cut-offs.** Lea builds the position and proposes; Daniel reviews, decides funding, and gives the second signature. On the Tuesday shown here he also opens the tools himself before her proposal arrives — the same Tuesday as on the [Start page](/start).
- **Every week, the forecast and the hedges.** He challenges subsidiary inputs, applies overlays, decides deposits, repayments and forward trades, and takes material items to the CFO's [jour fixe](term:jour-fixe).
- **Around it, the structural work.** The TMS evaluation, bank relationships, the refinancing plan, covenants, repatriation from China.

<Callout type="concept" title="What changes at CHF ≈ 0%">

With the SNB policy rate and SARON around 0%, surplus Swiss francs earn close to nothing on deposit. A company with a drawn credit facility therefore uses surplus CHF to reduce drawings at the next rollover rather than place deposits — but only when the saving (margin, less the commitment fee payable on the repaid amount) outweighs the risk of having to redraw. EUR and USD surpluses are different: they can earn €STR- or SOFR-level rates. See Daniel's Monday 09:05 decision.

</Callout>

## Interviewing someone like Daniel

<Callout type="interview" title="Good questions — and what he won't know">

- "Walk me through this morning: what had Lea sent you, what did you change, what did you sign?"
- "When did you last override a subsidiary's forecast? How did they react?"
- "What goes on the agenda of your jour fixe with the CFO — and what never does?"
- "What would you need to see before letting a system release an intercompany transfer without you?"
- "How would a TMS change your week? What would it not change?"

A treasury manager at this size is often **the single best interviewee** for workflow detail: he both does the work and makes the decisions. He will know less about how subsidiaries produce their numbers (ask [Giulia or Jens](/org/with-subsidiaries)) and about how the board thinks about risk appetite (ask the CFO). See [Treasury Manager role](/org/roles-treasury-manager).

</Callout>
