## Profile

Helvetic builds CNC machining centres in Switzerland, Germany and the Czech Republic and sells them worldwide through its own sales and service subsidiaries. A machine costs CHF 0.3–2m; customers pay a down payment, the balance on delivery, often on 60–90-day terms. About half of revenue is in euros, while the Swiss parent's costs are in francs.

Treasury was **centralised in 2021**, when the group refinanced with a CHF 200m syndicated [RCF](term:rcf) and a CHF 100m bond. Before that, each subsidiary managed its own banks and the parent's head of accounting "did treasury". Now **Daniel (Treasury Manager)** and **Lea (Treasury Analyst)** report to **Thomas (CFO)**. They own group liquidity, bank relationships, FX hedging, funding of subsidiaries and covenant reporting. The eight subsidiaries still run their own payments and bookkeeping and feed treasury with data.

Helvetic is the size where the job becomes a job — too big for one person's spreadsheet, too small for a department with specialists. That tension shows up in almost every workflow. [Why this is roughly the revenue at which the first dedicated analyst appears](why:helvetic-first-analyst).

The Chinese bank is there because onshore CNY business needs an onshore bank.

<Callout type="field" title="Why 12 accounts is low for this size">

Twelve operating accounts for nine entities in six currencies is lean. Many groups of Helvetic's size have several dozen, because acquisitions bring their own banks and nobody closes the accounts. Helvetic reached 12 only through a deliberate [2024 clean-up](why:helvetic-twelve-accounts) on top of the 2021 bank panel — so it sits at the rationalised low end of the mid-market range in [Treasury by Size](/size/midmarket).

</Callout>

Treasury has **no back office**. Segregation of duties is stretched across people: Daniel trades, Lea matches the bank confirmations, and Claudia's group accounting settles and books. When either treasury person is absent, Claudia or Thomas steps in — a known key-person risk.

| Semi-annually | Covenant calculation (Claudia computes the LTM ratios, Lea supplies the net-debt schedule), review and presentation by Daniel, certificate signed by Thomas and sent to the RCF agent |

## Systems

- **SAP S/4HANA** in the parent, Service AG, Germany and Czechia; **five local ERPs** in Italy, France, the UK, the US and China (legacy of earlier acquisitions and local choices).
- **A multibank connectivity tool** receiving prior-day statements from four of the five banks (camt.053 from the Swiss banks, [MT940](term:mt940) from the German bank) and sending the parent's payments over [EBICS](term:ebics) and [SWIFT](term:swift) — [11 of the 12 statements](why:systems-11-of-12-statements) arrive this way. The Chinese bank has no corporate reporting channel, so Wei emails a portal export daily and Lea types in the CNY balance; when it is missing, that is a connectivity and process problem, never a time-zone one.
- **Bank portals** for FX dealing and for the Chinese bank.
- **Excel**: the positioning workbook, the 13-week forecast (8 subsidiary templates plus central flows), the hedge register and hedge-ratio report, the covenant model, the counterparty-limit sheet, the bank-account and signatory list.
- **A TMS project in evaluation** — see below.

## The cash-flow pattern

- **Seasonality:** machine deliveries and final payments cluster in Q4 and late Q2; August is quiet in Italy and France; December receipts are high and so are outflows (13th salaries in Switzerland and Italy).
- **Monthly:** German payroll (about EUR 4.2m, paid at month-end) and the German supplier runs mean HM Deutschland is short around payroll week and funded from the parent's EUR account — one reason the group [keeps a EUR drawing rather than swapping a CHF one](why:helvetic-short-eur).
- **Annually:** the bond coupon, the shareholder dividend after the AGM, the Chinese subsidiary's dividend after its statutory audit, and quarterly VAT and tax prepayments in every country.
- **Structural:** euros accumulate in Italy, France and the parent; francs are needed at the parent; CNY accumulates in Suzhou and is hard to move ([trapped cash](term:trapped-cash)).

| Bucket | CHF m | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Parent and Service AG (CHF, EUR, USD) | 28 | Includes the CHF 10m minimum the CFO wants at the parent |
| Euro-area subsidiaries (DE, IT, FR) | 12 | Germany often near zero before payroll |
| UK, US, Czechia | 8 | |
| China (CNY ≈ 55m) | 6 | Visible but not freely movable — it leaves mainly as an annual dividend |
| **Group cash** | **54** | |
| RCF drawn | 60 | CHF 35m + EUR 27m; interest periods roll on the 15th |
| Undrawn RCF | 140 | of the CHF 200m facility |
| Bond | 100 | CHF 100m, due 2028 |

The covenant arithmetic, in one line: gross debt is CHF 160m (RCF drawn 60 + bond 100); net of the CHF 54m of group cash that is **CHF 106m of [net debt](why:net-debt-lender-view)**; against LTM EBITDA of about **CHF 78m** (the 12 months to 30 September 2026) that is **net debt / EBITDA ≈ 1.36x** against the 3.25x [covenant](term:covenant) — comfortable, which is why the covenant is a semi-annual calculation rather than a weekly worry. Two definitional caveats a lender interview will surface: Helvetic reports under **Swiss GAAP FER**, so [leases stay out of the net-debt definition](why:covenant-excludes-leases), and the [CNY 6m in China](why:china-cash-excluded) counts in the cash line even though it can only leave as a dividend.

Drawings change only at [rollovers](why:fifteenth-rollover-repayment): the split was CHF 35m + EUR 27m at the September close, and at the 15 October 2026 rollover it moved to **CHF 30m + EUR 35m** (≈ CHF 63m drawn, **≈ CHF 137m of the CHF 200m undrawn**) — the EUR drawing exists because the group is structurally short euros, not because it is swapping a franc loan.

## Typical problems

- **Two people, no backup.** When Lea is ill, Daniel builds the position; when both are away, the CFO signs and the position is checked every few days.
- **Five ERPs, eight templates.** Subsidiary forecasts arrive late, in modified templates or copied from last week. Italy has over-forecast receipts in 9 of the last 12 weeks.
- **Manual concentration.** Every transfer between entities is an instruction plus an intercompany-loan record. A [cash pool](term:cash-pooling) has been discussed but not implemented ([why Helvetic concentrates manually](why:helvetic-no-cash-pool) — tax, the Czech and Chinese entities, and bank negotiations make it a project, not a switch).
- **China.** CNY ≈ 55m sits in Suzhou; it comes out mainly as an annual dividend after the audit, with withholding tax, so it is excluded from group headroom.
- **Hedging depends on the forecast.** The [graded bands](why:hedge-bands-graded) apply to *forecast* EUR net inflows — 60–90% for the next quarter down to 0–40% four quarters out; if the subsidiaries' forecasts are optimistic, Helvetic over-hedges in the front quarters.
- **Refinancing ahead.** The RCF matures in **October 2028** and the CHF 100m bond is due in 2028 — the same year — so in 2026–27 Daniel and Thomas are preparing one refinancing project covering both, extra work on top of daily operations.
- **Spreadsheet fragility.** The positioning workbook has links into three other workbooks; one broken link once doubled the Czech balance in the CFO note.
- **Payment control in subsidiaries.** Treasury sets the rules but does not see local payment runs until the next day's statements.

## Why Helvetic is evaluating a TMS

<Callout type="synthesis" title="An illustrative weekly time budget (Daniel + Lea, about 80 hours)">

| Activity | Hours / week | Mostly |
|---|---|---|
| Daily cash position and funding | 10 | Collecting, reconciling to expected flows, deciding transfers |
| 13-week forecast: collection, chasing, consolidation, variance | 14 | Chasing and reshaping templates |
| FX: exposure update, hedge-ratio report, dealing, confirmations | 6 | Workbook maintenance |
| Payments: approvals, bank-detail queries, urgent payments | 6 | Review and exceptions |
| Reporting (CFO note, monthly report, covenant model) | 8 | Assembling numbers from other workbooks |
| Banks: KYC, signatories, fee queries, relationship meetings | 6 | Documents and emails |
| Refinancing preparation (2026–27) | 10 | Lender materials, model, legal documents |
| Ad hoc (CFO questions, audit, tax, projects) | 20 | Everything else |

These are this manual's estimates for a two-person mid-market treasury, not survey data. The point: most hours go into collecting and reshaping data, not deciding. A TMS would automate statement import, the position, the deal register and parts of the forecast; it would not make the forecast inputs from Italy any better. Practitioners at this size routinely describe forecasting as still spreadsheet-based even after a TMS arrives (field observation).

</Callout>

## The workflows, as run at Helvetic

Helvetic is the reference company for most worked examples in the [workflows](/workflows) section.

| Workflow | How it happens at Helvetic |
|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Lea's workbook from [11 statements in the tool plus Wei's emailed China export](why:systems-11-of-12-statements) by 09:00; Daniel decides transfers (e.g. EUR 1.5m to Germany) and small EUR deposits. |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Weekly 13-week forecast by entity and currency; variance analysis; monthly presentation to the CFO. |
| [Liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning) | 18-month plan with FP&A, rebuilt quarterly; focused on 2028 — the RCF matures Oct 2028, the same year as the bond. |
| [Surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment) | [Surplus CHF reduces RCF drawings at the next 15th rollover](why:fifteenth-rollover-repayment); surplus EUR goes into short EUR deposits at ≈ €STR (2.2–2.4%, illustrative) within counterparty limits. |
| [Funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary) | Intercompany loans under a framework agreement (e.g. EUR 3m to HM Deutschland, USD 5m to HM Inc.). |
| [Payment processing](wf:payment-processing) | Decentralised: each subsidiary pays locally; for the parent's runs accounting prepares, Lea checks, Daniel releases. |
| [Large payment approval](wf:large-payment-approval) | CHF 2–10m non-routine: CFO and CEO; above CHF 10m, the board. [Lea prepares the evidence; she never releases](why:lea-never-releases) — Daniel does, or the CFO and CEO sign. |
| [Bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation) | Done by local accounting teams; treasury reconciles its own accounts and deals. |
| [Bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) | Daniel owns the account and signatory register and approves every opening with Thomas; local managers sign local bank documents. |
| [Bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) | Annual review of fee statements from five banks — tedious, rarely complete. |
| [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management) | Forecast net flows by currency from the 13-week forecast and subsidiary templates. |
| [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Rolling EUR forwards against [graded quarterly bands](why:hedge-bands-graded) (60–90% / 40–70% / 25–55% / 0–40%); USD raised to 60% after a large US order. Under Swiss GAAP FER the bands are [management policy, not IFRS 9 designation](why:fer-vs-ifrs-pack). |
| [Credit facility management](wf:credit-facility-management) | RCF drawings and rollovers with the agent bank; refinancing preparation for the 2028 maturities. |
| [Covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) | Claudia computes the LTM ratios, Lea supplies the net-debt schedule, Daniel reviews and presents; Thomas signs the certificate; quarterly projection in the treasury committee. |
| [Month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) | Treasury report to the CFO; cash and debt postings with Claudia's team. |
| [Policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance) | Quarterly check of hedge ratios, counterparty limits and minimum cash against the treasury policy. |
| [Acquisition integration](wf:acquisition-integration) | The last acquisition's three accounts at a regional Chinese bank were not connected for months. |
| [Currency shock](wf:currency-shock) | EUR/CHF down 8%: hedges cover next quarter; the committee decides whether to extend cover and how to re-price. |

<Callout type="interview" title="If you interview a Helvetic-type treasury team">

This is the richest interview for workflow detail: the people who decide are also the people who build the spreadsheets. Ask the analyst to share their screen and walk through this morning's position; ask the manager what they changed in last week's forecast and why. Expect strong opinions about subsidiaries' data quality and about TMS vendors. Expect them to be busy: a two-person team rarely has spare hours for long interviews.

</Callout>
