## Where each vendor type sits in the FX workflow

<Flow steps={["Identify exposure|ERP, sales forecasts, subsidiaries", "Decide hedge|policy, hedge ratio, instrument", "Execute|multi-dealer platform or bank", "Capture & confirm|TMS, back office", "Value & account|month-end, hedge accounting"]} caption="FX hedging chain — see the fx-exposure-management and fx-hedging workflows" />

| Step | Workflow | Vendor types | Human judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify exposure | [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management) | TMS exposure modules, [hedging automation](term:currency-management-automation) (e.g. Kantox), Excel | Is the forecast exposure real? Who is accountable for the number? |
| Decide hedge | [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Mostly Excel and policy; advisory firms (e.g. Chatham) | Hedge ratio, tenor, instrument within [policy](term:treasury-policy) |
| Execute | [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | [Multi-dealer platforms](term:multi-dealer-platform) (360T, FXall, Bloomberg FXGO), bank portals, cross-border payment/FX providers (e.g. Ebury — Santander-owned) | Timing; which banks get the business (wallet share) |
| Capture, value, account | [Month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) | TMS, hedge-accounting tools (e.g. Hedgebook, Reval) | Hedge-accounting designation and effectiveness |

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Where the category is mature and where it is not">

Execution venues have existed for decades and are embedded with banks and TMS. The weaker link in practice is upstream: getting a reliable exposure number from sales, procurement and subsidiaries, and turning policy into a decision. Hedging-automation vendors target exactly that; whether mid-market companies trust rules to trade on their behalf is a good interview topic. See also [currency shock](wf:currency-shock).

</Callout>

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Bank ownership blurs the 'non-bank' line">

The tidy story — banks serve large corporates, fintechs serve everyone the banks ignore — does not survive contact with the ownership column. **Kantox is 100% BNP Paribas since July 2023**[](cite:comp-kantox-bnp-2023) and **Ebury is 55% Santander after the April 2026 raise**[](cite:comp-ebury-santander-2026). Both keep the fintech product and the underserved-SME pitch; the balance sheet, and increasingly the distribution, is the bank's. For a founder the conclusion is double-edged: the SME wedge is real, but it is being acquired into rather than left open — [the reasoning in full](why:comp-bank-owned-fx).

</Callout>

## Cash investment portals

The execution side of [surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment): [cash investment portals](term:cash-investment-portal) for [money-market funds](term:money-market-fund) and fixed-term products. The de-facto standard portal treasurers name is ICD (the example used in the workflow itself); banks' own investment portals compete here and are usually free with the relationship. Atlar's 2025 partnership with TreasurySpring is an example of treasury platforms adding investment access through partners[](cite:comp-atlar-treasuryspring).

<Callout type="interview" title="Questions">

- "Where does your FX exposure number come from, and how much do you trust it?"
- "Who decides how much to hedge, and what does the decision look like on paper?"
- "How do you get prices — one bank, several banks by phone, or a platform?"
- "Who values the hedges at month-end and posts them?"
- For investments: "What does the investment policy allow, and how do you check counterparty limits before placing cash?"
