## The rules this section follows

1. **What a vendor sells** may be described from the vendor's own documentation, but it is labelled as a **vendor claim**. Documentation tells you what a product is designed to do, not how well it does it or how many customers use it that way.
2. **Ownership, funding and acquisitions** need a dated source: a press release from a party to the deal, a filing, or reputable trade/financial press. Aggregator databases (PitchBook, Crunchbase, Tracxn) are leads, not proof.
3. **Positioning** (who a vendor says it is for) comes from the vendor; **target customer** is the author's reading of that positioning, which is why the size map says "synthesis".
4. **Apparent gaps** are allowed in only two forms:
   - **From documented scope** — the gap follows from what the vendor says it is. Example: a multi-dealer FX platform executes trades; it does not calculate the exposure. That is not a criticism, it is the product boundary.
   - **Open question** — something worth testing in interviews, phrased as a question. It is *not* a claim that the gap exists.
   Anything else ("clunky UI", "poor support", "expensive") needs evidence from several independent users and is not stated here.
5. **Every entry is dated.** Treasury software ownership changed repeatedly in 2025–2026 (for example GTreasury's sale to Ripple[](cite:comp-ripple-gtreasury-pymnts)). A fact without a date is a liability.

## Reading vendor claims

| Claim type | Example wording | What it usually means | How to test it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer count | "Trusted by N,000 companies" (generic) | The counting basis is rarely stated (active vs ever-signed; single module vs full suite; direct vs via a bank or reseller) | Ask for references *at your target size and region*; ask customers which modules they use |
| Connectivity | "Connects to N,000 banks" (generic) | A format/channel library; each new customer bank still needs set-up, mandates and testing | Ask customers: "How many of your banks were live on day one? Which ones took months?" |
| Automation | "Automates cash positioning" | Statements are imported and aggregated; judgement on funding moves remains human | Ask: "What do you still do by hand between 08:00 and 10:00?" |
| AI / agents | "AI-powered forecasting", "treasury agents" | Ranges from a statistical model on bank history to an LLM assistant over the vendor's data | Ask: "What does it decide or do without a person clicking approve? Show me last week's corrections." |
| Implementation time | "Live in weeks" | Core module, favourable scope, responsive banks | Ask customers for the date contract signed vs date the treasury team stopped using the old process |
| Survey statistics | "X% of treasurers say…" | Often a vendor-sponsored survey; sample and question wording matter | Check sponsor, sample size, region; prefer AFP, EACT, PwC, Deloitte, KPMG, ACT |

<Callout type="field" title="Analyst reports and award lists">

Analyst rankings and "best TMS" lists are widely used in selections, but the business models behind them differ (some are paid by vendors, some by buyers). Use them to build a long list; do not cite them as evidence of product quality. Practitioner publications (Treasury Today, TMI, The Global Treasurer, Strategic Treasurer) are useful for who-bought-what announcements, which are usually vendor-issued.

</Callout>

## How to verify in interviews

Customer interviews are the best evidence you will get about competitors, because the treasurer has used the product under real conditions. The goal is behavioural evidence, not opinions.

<Callout type="interview" title="Question script: competitive landscape from the customer's side">

- **Stack:** "Walk me through the systems you touched yesterday, in order." Note which is the system of record, which is only viewed.
- **Selection history:** "How did you end up with this system? Who else was on the shortlist? Why did they lose?" (Reveals real alternatives, including "stay on Excel".)
- **Implementation:** "How long from signature to switching off the old process? What took longest?" (Often bank connectivity and ERP interfaces.)
- **Workarounds:** "What do you still do in Excel or in a bank portal, even though the system could in theory do it?"
- **Trust boundary:** "What does the system do without anyone approving it? What would you never let it do?"
- **Switching:** "What would have to happen for you to replace it? When does the contract renew?"
- **Ownership changes:** "Has your vendor changed owners? Did anything change for you?" — relevant for GTreasury (Ripple), and wherever an entry marks a deal.

</Callout>

<Callout type="confusion" title="Beginner traps">

- **Confusing a bank's product with an independent vendor.** Many banks white-label or resell TMS and forecasting software; a treasurer may name the bank, not the vendor.
- **Assuming the logo list is current.** Customers churn; logos stay on websites for years.
- **Treating a partnership as an integration.** "Partnership" can mean a co-marketing agreement. Ask customers whether data actually flows.
- **Believing that one interviewee represents the market.** One treasurer's hatred of their TMS is one data point. Five at the same company size in the same region is a pattern.

</Callout>

## Maintenance

Re-verify the dated facts at least every six months and after any announced deal. When updating an entry, change the badge only if you opened (or saw a snippet from) a primary or reputable source, and register it in `content/sources/competitors.yaml`.
