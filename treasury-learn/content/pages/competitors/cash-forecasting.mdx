## Why this category is crowded

Forecasting is where treasurers report the most pain. In AFP's 2026 benchmarking, about half named cash and liquidity forecasting the most challenging activity (49% as reported; figure not confirmed against the primary text)[](cite:afp-bench-2026); in the (vendor-sponsored) Strategic Treasurer/TIS survey, 53% called forecasting "difficult" in 2025, up from 39% in 2018[](cite:st-tis-cfv-2025); Deloitte's 2024 survey reports roughly a fifth say their cash-positioning capability requires development (≈22% as reported by Deloitte, figure not confirmed against the primary text)[](cite:deloitte-gts-2024). That is [why the category is crowded](why:comp-forecasting-pain): almost every vendor — TMS suites, AR-automation vendors, SME cash tools — claims forecasting.

## What the tools automate and what they don't

Walk through the [cash forecasting workflow](wf:cash-forecasting) and mark who does each step:

| Step | Typically automated by tools (vendor scope) | Still human (in nearly all documented scopes) |
|---|---|---|
| Collect actuals | Bank transactions imported and categorised | Fixing miscategorised flows; unconnected banks |
| Near-term receipts/payments | ERP open AR/AP pulled; payment-date predictions (some vendors use ML on history) | Judging which big customer will actually pay late |
| Business input | Templates sent to subsidiaries | Chasing [subsidiary finance](/org/with-subsidiaries); judging whether their numbers are sandbagged |
| Non-routine items | — | Tax, dividends, M&A, capex timing: entered by hand from conversations |
| Variance analysis | Forecast vs actual reports | Explaining *why* and deciding what to change |
| Decision | — | Funding, investing, drawing the [RCF](term:rcf) |

<Callout type="synthesis" title="The startup question in this category">

The data-collection steps are well served (on paper) by many vendors; the steps that hurt are the judgement ones — business input quality, non-routine items, explaining variances. A new entrant that only automates the first two rows competes with a crowd. See [Agentic Treasury](/lens) for whether the judgement steps are candidates for AI.

</Callout>
