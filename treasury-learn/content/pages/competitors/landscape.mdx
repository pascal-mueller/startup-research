<Callout type="synthesis" title="What the map shows at a glance">

- The **multinational column is crowded with systems of record and execution venues** — established TMS suites, ERP treasury, payment hubs, multi-dealer platforms. A newcomer there usually sells *on top of* those, not instead of them.
- The **mid-market column is where established SaaS TMS vendors and newer API-first platforms overlap**. It is also where the status quo (Excel plus bank portals) is most often still the real incumbent.
- The **SME and startup columns are thin for treasury-specific software**. What exists is forecasting/cash visibility and FX payment providers; most companies here use their banks and a spreadsheet.

## Reading across the entries

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Patterns worth testing in interviews">

1. **Scope creep is the norm.** Nearly every category is expanding into its neighbours: forecasting tools add payments, payment hubs add cash visibility, TMS suites add AI and supply-chain finance. The cleanest single case: **TIS, a payment hub, bought the forecasting specialist Cashforce in June 2022**[](cite:comp-tis-cashforce-2022) — one deal that spans two categories. When two vendors "compete", check whether the customer is actually buying the same workflow from both.
2. **The human judgement steps are rarely what's sold.** Products automate data collection (statements, open items, exposures) and execution (trades, payments). The decision in between — how much to hedge, whether to fund a subsidiary today, why the forecast missed — is still the treasurer's in nearly all documented product scopes. That is where the [Agentic Treasury](/lens) section looks for opportunities, and where trust is hardest to earn.
3. **Ownership changes create windows.** A change of owner (e.g. GTreasury to Ripple in 2025[](cite:comp-ripple-gtreasury-pymnts)) is a moment when customers re-evaluate. Whether they actually switch is an empirical question — ask them.
4. **Swiss and European specifics matter.** In Switzerland, EBICS and bank-specific ISO 20022 implementations, QR-bill and the SIC system shape connectivity and payments; tools that started in the US may have been built first around US bank APIs and ACH. Ask European interviewees how well a US-built product handled their banks.

</Callout>

## Missing from this landscape (known omissions)

- **Banks' own treasury tools** (multi-bank reporting, liquidity portals, FX e-platforms from UBS, J.P. Morgan, Citi, HSBC and others) are major de-facto competitors but are not profiled. Note the mirror image too: several *profiled* vendors are bank-owned (Kantox, Ebury, Fides).
- **Swiss-specific software** (e.g. treasury and payment modules used by Swiss mid-market companies alongside Abacus or SAP) is not covered.
- **Consultancies, SIs and bank resellers** shape every selection ("often with consultants" appears in the TMS entries) but have no entry: treasury consultants run RFPs, SIs implement, banks resell or white-label vendors. Treat this channel as a competitor for the customer's *budget and attention*, and ask every interviewee who advised their last selection.
- **Outsourced treasury / treasury-as-a-service** appears only via Chatham Financial's advisory model.
- **US startup-banking yield products** that market themselves as "treasury" for startups are not profiled; they compete for the [Kleio](co:kleio)-sized company's idle cash, not for the treasurer's workflow.
- **Names often raised but out of scope:** HedgeGuard is buy-side trading/portfolio software (hedge funds, crypto asset managers), not corporate treasury; "Predica" surfaces as an IT consultancy or a bank's insurance arm, not a treasury product. If either comes up in an interview, it is probably a different market.
- **Vendor scale is not researched** (employees, revenue): a 100-person Embat (company figure) and a multi-thousand-person FIS sell into the same map. Size the vendor yourself before treating entries as comparable — see [Method](/competitors/method) for a ten-minute recipe.
- **This section does not size the market.** No market-size or share claims appear, because none were verified. To size a segment for a pitch: count candidate companies (e.g. Swiss groups between CHF 200m–2bn revenue), estimate the share running the workflow in scope, and ask five interviewees what they pay — triangulate, do not cite a vendor's TAM slide.
