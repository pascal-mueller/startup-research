## Profile of the tier

| Dimension | Typical range (indicative) | Alpine |
|---|---|---|
| People | ~100–500 | ~320 |
| Revenue | CHF 20–250m | CHF ~80m |
| Legal entities | 2–5 (parent + sales/service subsidiaries) | 3 |
| Banks / accounts | 2–4 banks, 5–20 accounts | 2 Swiss banks + a US bank, 9 accounts |
| Currencies | 2–4 | CHF, EUR, USD |
| Funding | A house-bank credit line, mortgage, leasing, sometimes export finance | CHF 12m committed line, CHF 6m mortgage |
| Finance team | 4–15, of whom nobody is dedicated to treasury | 7 |
| Treasury people | CFO (decisions) + head of accounting (execution), part-time | Martin + Petra, 20–30% of Petra's week |

In Switzerland and Germany this is the *KMU / Mittelstand* world: often owner-managed or family-owned, frequently export-oriented, with a long-standing **house bank** (typically a cantonal bank or a regional bank in Switzerland, a Sparkasse or cooperative bank in Germany) and a large bank added for FX and international payments.

## Team structure

<OrgChart id="controller-led" />

- **CFO** (sometimes titled head of finance, or the owner's finance director) — owns bank relationships, credit lines, FX decisions and the board's view of liquidity. See [CFO role](/org/roles-cfo).
- **Head of accounting / chief accountant** — does the operational cash work: daily balances, payment-run release, the cash sheet, bank queries, intercompany transfers. See [a day with Petra](/day/petra) and [treasury and accounting](/org/with-accounting).
- **AP and AR clerks** — payment proposals, vendor master data, collections ([AP](/org/with-ap), [AR](/org/with-ar)).
- **Subsidiary managing directors and bookkeepers** — local payments and receipts; often the weakest link in information ([subsidiaries](/org/with-subsidiaries)).
- **House bank relationship manager** — the most important external contact; annual credit review.

There is typically no treasury job description. When one appears — "finance manager with treasury responsibility" — it is usually a sign the company is moving toward the mid-market model.

## Systems

- **ERP**: Microsoft Dynamics (Business Central / NAV), Abacus (widespread in Swiss SMEs), SAP Business One or an older SAP ECC, Sage, and others.
- **Bank connectivity**: e-banking portals; file upload of [pain.001](term:pain-001) payment files; [EBICS](term:ebics) is common in Switzerland, Germany, France and Austria and is often built into the ERP or a small banking module.
- **Excel**: the weekly cash sheet, the FX list, the credit-line tracker.
- **No TMS** in most cases. Older survey data show spreadsheets as the dominant tool for daily cash management among small companies[](cite:kyriba-act-2013); at this size a TMS is typically hard to justify because the complexity is in the business, not in the number of accounts.

## Workflows at this size

| Workflow | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Head of accounting checks portals daily (20–40 min), more on payment days. |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Weekly or monthly Excel sheet, 8–13 weeks, from ERP AR/AP plus what the CFO, sales and project managers know. No formal variance analysis. |
| [Liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning) | Budget-based 12-month plan; the credit line is the backstop; a board rule on minimum headroom if anything. |
| [Funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary) | Ad hoc transfers from the parent, sometimes a bank sweep; intercompany agreements drafted by the tax adviser. |
| [Payment processing](wf:payment-processing) | ERP payment proposal → head of accounting release → file to bank; CFO co-signs large amounts. |
| [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Ad hoc forwards on signed contracts, decided by the CFO with the bank's dealer. |
| [Credit facility management](wf:credit-facility-management) / [Covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) | Annual review with the house bank; a simple covenant (e.g. equity ratio) checked from annual accounts. |
| [Bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation) | In the ERP, by the accountants; largely automated for domestic receipts with structured references (QR-bill in Switzerland). |
| [Bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) | CFO decides, head of accounting does the paperwork; signatory lists often out of date. |

## Biggest problems

- **Treasury is always second priority** to the close, the audit and tax deadlines — until something goes wrong.
- **Key-person dependency.** One person knows the cash sheet, the EBICS set-up and the bank contacts.
- **Forecast inputs live outside finance.** In project, seasonal or order-driven businesses, the real information is with sales, project managers or production planning.
- **FX handled ad hoc.** No record of what was deliberately left unhedged; decisions influenced by recent moves.
- **Subsidiary blind spots.** Local managing directors run payments and forget to mention large items (tax prepayments, bonuses).
- **Bank dependence.** One house bank provides the credit line, guarantees, payments and often the mortgage; switching is hard.
- **Fraud exposure.** Enough payment volume to be a target, not enough staff for strong segregation of duties.

<Callout type="survey" title="Not every SME borrows">

In a SECO-commissioned survey of 2,712 Swiss SMEs (2021), 32% had any bank financing[](cite:seco-kmu-finanzierung-2021). The sample includes many micro-firms, so industrial SMEs are more likely to borrow — but it is a reminder that "funding" and "covenants" are not universal SME concerns. For those that do borrow, the house bank's annual credit review is the main treasury event of the year.

</Callout>

## Centralisation

Decisions are centralised in the CFO by default; execution is split — the parent's finance team handles the parent, subsidiaries handle themselves. There is rarely a cash pool; at most a bank sweep from a subsidiary account into the parent's account at the same bank. Intercompany funding is by ad hoc transfer, which tax advisers increasingly insist is documented as an [intercompany loan](term:intercompany-loan) at an arm's-length rate.

## Who owns which tasks

| Task | Owns | Performs | Approves / informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily cash check | Head of accounting | Head of accounting | CFO informed of anomalies |
| Weekly cash sheet | CFO | Head of accounting | CFO reviews Monday |
| Payment runs | Head of accounting | AP clerk prepares | Head of accounting releases; CFO co-signs above a threshold |
| FX hedging | CFO | CFO with the bank | Board sets a broad rule |
| Credit line, bank relationships | CFO | CFO | Board approves new facilities |
| Subsidiary funding | CFO | Head of accounting | — |
| Bank accounts and signatories | CFO | Head of accounting | Board resolutions where required |
| Reconciliation | Head of accounting | Accountants | Auditor reviews annually |

## What pushes an SME into the mid-market model

A third or fourth foreign subsidiary; the first syndicated or multi-bank facility with quarterly covenants; an acquisition; the head of accounting leaving; or the CFO realising that FX decisions now move annual profit by more than a percentage point. The usual first step is hiring one treasury-minded person, not buying a system.

## Recognising this tier in an interview

Interview **both** the CFO and the person who actually does the cash work (usually the head of accounting). They use plain words — "cash sheet", "payment run", "the bank" — rather than "liquidity forecast" or "counterparty". Ask about the last time a large customer paid late and what they did, how they decide whether to hedge, and who covers when the key person is away.
