## Two sub-tiers

"Multinational" covers a wide span. This page distinguishes:

- **The lower multinational tier** (CHF 5–10bn revenue, 10,000–25,000 people, 30–100 entities) — [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) is the reference.
- **The large multinational** (CHF 20–60bn revenue, around 50,000 people or more, 200–600 entities in 60–100+ countries) — described in the section "A 50,000-person group" below.

| Dimension | Lower tier (GlobalChem) | ~50,000-person group (indicative) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal entities | 30–100 (GlobalChem ~40 operating) | 200–600, including holdings, finance companies, dormant entities |
| Countries | 30–60 | 60–100+ |
| Banks | 15–30, 5–10 core (GlobalChem ~20 / 7) | 30–80+, 10–20 core relationship banks |
| Bank accounts | 200–800 (GlobalChem ~280) | 1,000–5,000 |
| Currencies with material flows | 20–40 (GlobalChem ~30) | 40–80+ |
| Treasury staff | 15–40 (GlobalChem ~25) | 50–150 in treasury proper; more in shared-service operations |
| Funding | Bonds, CP, RCF backstop, investment-grade rating | Multi-currency bond programmes (EMTN, US market), CP in several markets, large RCFs, hybrid instruments, sometimes a captive finance arm |
| Structures | In-house bank, pools, netting, payment factory (varies) | Several in-house banks or regional hubs, netting, global payment factory, POBO/COBO |

All ranges are indicative — this manual's synthesis from practitioner descriptions and public company reports, not survey statistics. Surveys confirm the structures, not the counts: among organisations above USD 10bn revenue, PwC's 2025 survey reports 67% with an in-house bank, 60% with a payment factory and 50% with payments-on-behalf-of[](cite:pwc-gts-2025).

## Team structure (lower tier)

A Group Treasurer ([role](/org/roles-head-of-treasury), [a day with Anna](/day/anna)) reporting to the CFO leads five or six teams:

| Team | Typical size | Work |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate finance / capital markets | 2–6 | Bonds, CP, RCF, rating agencies, M&A financing, maturity profile ([debt role](/org/roles-debt)) |
| Cash & liquidity / in-house bank | 3–10 | Group position, pools, IHB, subsidiary funding, investments, forecasting ([cash manager](/org/roles-cash-manager), [a day with Priya](/day/priya)) |
| Financial risk | 2–6 | FX and interest-rate exposure, hedging programmes, hedge accounting, counterparty risk ([FX / risk](/org/roles-fx-risk)) |
| Treasury operations / back office | 4–15 | Confirmations, settlements, bank-account administration, KYC, static data, treasury accounting ([operations](/org/roles-operations)) |
| Treasury systems | 2–5 | TMS, connectivity, formats, user rights ([systems role](/org/roles)) |
| Regional treasury centre(s) | 2–10 each | Regional funding, FX in local hours, restricted-market documentation |

Plus a **payments lead** or payment-factory team, usually inside a [shared-service centre](term:shared-service-centre) ([payments roles](/org/roles-payments)). Real examples of this split are visible in company reports: Clariant describes a central Corporate Treasury managing financial risk under board-approved policies[](cite:clariant-frm-2020) and advertises treasury operations roles in its Polish hub[](cite:clariant-cash-mgmt-job).

## Systems

ERP (often SAP, frequently many ERPs across the group), a full TMS (SAP treasury modules, or a specialist TMS), SWIFT connectivity (directly or via a service bureau) and host-to-host links for high-volume banks, multi-bank FX trading platforms, market data (Bloomberg, LSEG), a cash-forecasting tool or TMS module, bank-account-management software, sanctions-screening and payment-fraud tools in the payment factory, and a data warehouse or BI layer for reporting. One concrete stack: [GlobalChem's](co:globalchem) — SAP S/4HANA with SAP treasury modules as the TMS, a Swift service bureau for connectivity, a multi-bank FX platform, a separate cash-forecasting tool and a bank-account-management tool. Spreadsheets survive for ad hoc analysis, board packs and anything the systems do not cover.

## Workflows at this size

| Workflow | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Automated collection; people work exceptions, IHB external positions and cut-off funding across time zones. |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Multi-horizon, dozens to hundreds of submitting units, accuracy KPIs, reconciliation with FP&A. |
| [Liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning) | Multi-year funding plan, rating-agency liquidity metrics, CP backstop sizing. |
| [Surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment) | Policy-bound portfolios, counterparty limits monitored daily, money market funds and government paper. |
| [Payment processing](wf:payment-processing) | Payment factory, POBO/COBO, central sanctions and fraud controls; local payments where regulation requires. |
| [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Exposure collection, [netting](term:netting), layered programmes, options, hedge accounting; front/middle/back-office split. |
| [Credit facility management](wf:credit-facility-management) | Large syndicated RCFs, often undrawn backstops; bank-group management. |
| [Bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) | Central register and tool; hundreds of KYC files; annual signatory reviews. |
| [Policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance) | Daily limit monitoring in the TMS, monthly compliance reporting, internal-audit cycles. |
| [Acquisition integration](wf:acquisition-integration) | A standard playbook, repeated several times a year in acquisitive groups. |

## Biggest problems

- **Forecast quality from many units.** Tools make submission easy; they do not make local forecasts accurate. The EACT's 2025 survey of European multinationals ranked cash-flow forecasting as the top treasury priority and cited non-standardised data as a barrier to AI use[](cite:eact-2025).
- **Restricted markets and [trapped cash](term:trapped-cash).** Cash in countries with capital controls cannot be pooled; repatriation needs dividends, documentation and withholding-tax planning ([restricted currency](term:restricted-currency)).
- **Counterparty exposure across dozens of banks** — deposits plus derivatives, monitored daily.
- **Controls across thousands of users and signatories** — joiners, movers and leavers across hundreds of mandates.
- **Permanent system change** — ERP consolidation, TMS upgrades, ISO 20022 migration, bank-connectivity changes, acquisitions to connect.
- **Distance from the operational detail.** Senior treasurers manage policies and teams; the reality of a subsidiary's cash is several layers away.

## A 50,000-person group

GlobalChem is a multinational, but a small one. A group with around 50,000 employees — think of a large Swiss or German industrial, chemicals, consumer-goods or engineering group with CHF/EUR 20–40bn revenue — adds layers that change the treasury job again. Public examples of the model exist: Schindler (about 70,000 employees) states in its holding-company accounts that the holding is responsible for financing and for hedging the foreign-currency risks of group companies[](cite:schindler-holding-2021), i.e. these are run centrally rather than by each subsidiary.

What is different at this size (field observation and synthesis, indicative):

- **Organisation in layers.** A Group Treasurer (sometimes titled Head of Group Treasury & Corporate Finance, occasionally with a seat on the CFO's leadership team) with heads of department below — each of whom runs what would be a whole mid-market treasury. Typical departments: capital markets & funding; cash management & in-house banking; financial risk management (FX, rates, sometimes commodities); treasury operations; treasury systems / digital; bank relationship management; often also pension asset oversight, insurance / captive, trade finance and guarantees, or customer / vendor financing programmes.
- **Regional treasury centres in three time zones** (e.g. Europe, Americas, Asia-Pacific), each with its own cash, FX and funding responsibilities, reporting both to the regional CFO and to group treasury.
- **Several in-house banks or regional hubs** instead of one; global netting across 100+ entities; POBO and COBO (collections on behalf of) in regions where regulation allows; multiple physical and notional pools by currency.
- **A payment factory processing hundreds of thousands to millions of payments a year**, run inside global business services with its own management; treasury owns policy, bank connectivity and controls, not the daily operation.
- **A treasury middle office** or treasury controlling function: limit monitoring, performance and risk reporting, hedge-accounting effectiveness, policy compliance — separate from the traders and from the back office.
- **Capital markets as a routine activity**: bond issuance several times a year in several currencies, a standing debt-issuance programme, CP in Europe and the US, rating-agency management as a continuous task.
- **Bank relationship management as a formal discipline**: a panel of 10–20 core banks, [wallet-share](term:wallet-share) tracking, periodic mandates and RFPs for cash management by region.
- **Governance**: a treasury or financial risk committee chaired by the CFO, a board-approved policy suite, internal audit on a multi-year cycle, and external auditors reviewing hedge accounting.

What does **not** change: somebody still has to understand why a Brazilian subsidiary's receipts were BRL 40m below forecast, whether a supplier's changed bank details are genuine, and how much of a forecast exposure is real enough to hedge.

<Callout type="confusion" title="The Group Treasurer at a 50,000-person group is not the person to ask about the morning cash position">

At this size the Group Treasurer's week is funding strategy, rating agencies, bank-panel decisions, M&A, the CFO and the audit committee. For how the daily position, the forecast or the payment factory actually work, interview the heads of cash management, operations or systems — and the analysts and SSC staff below them. See [who knows what](/org/who-knows-what).

</Callout>

## Centralisation

<OrgChart id="large-rtc-ssc" />

High but never total. Decisions, funding, hedging and bank selection are centralised; execution is centralised through in-house banks, pools, netting and payment factories in regions where regulation and tax allow; local finance keeps AR, AP, local tax, and banking in restricted or regulated markets. The limits to centralisation are legal (capital controls, local-account requirements), tax (withholding, transfer pricing, permanent-establishment risk), operational (time zones, local languages and holidays) and political (strong local management). See [treasury centralisation](term:treasury-centralisation).

## Who owns which tasks

| Task | Owns | Performs | Approves / informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily cash position | Head of cash & liquidity | Cash analysts; regional centres; TMS collects | Group Treasurer informed by dashboard |
| Forecasting | Head of cash & liquidity | Central forecasting team; entity finance teams submit | CFO sees group liquidity monthly |
| Funding and capital markets | Head of corporate finance | Corporate finance team with banks | CFO / board approve issuance programmes |
| FX and rate hedging | Head of financial risk | Dealers (front office); back office confirms | Treasury committee sets policy |
| Payments | Payments lead / GBS | Payment factory operators | Treasury signatories release non-routine; policy by treasury |
| Bank accounts, KYC | Head of treasury operations | Back office with the bank-account tool | Treasury management approves openings |
| Policy compliance | Treasury middle office | Automated limit checks + reporting | Group Treasurer, CFO, audit committee |
| Treasury systems | Head of treasury systems | Systems team, IT, vendors | — |

## Recognising this tier in an interview

Expect titles, not tasks: "Head of Cash Management EMEA", "Treasury Operations Lead", "Global Payments Manager". Ask each person where their responsibility starts and ends, which teams hand them work, and which exceptions reach them. Expect long procurement cycles and strong internal standards.
