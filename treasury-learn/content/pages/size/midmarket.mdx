## Profile of the tier

| Dimension | Typical range (indicative) | Helvetic |
|---|---|---|
| People | ~1,000–10,000 | ~2,600 |
| Revenue | CHF 250m–3bn | CHF ~650m |
| Legal entities | 5–30, several countries, often some acquired | 9 |
| Banks | 4–10 (3–6 in a syndicated facility) | 5 (4 in the RCF) |
| Bank accounts | 10–100 — the low end only after a deliberate rationalisation | 12 (from 31) |
| Currencies | 4–10 | 6 |
| Funding | Syndicated RCF with covenants; sometimes a bond, Schuldschein or private placement | CHF 200m RCF + CHF 100m bond |
| ERPs | Often 2–6 (main ERP plus acquired or local systems) | SAP in 4 entities + 4 others |
| Treasury people | 1–5 dedicated, reporting to the CFO | Daniel + Lea |

<Callout type="survey" title="Team size in this tier">

The AFP's 2025 benchmarking survey (US) found that companies with under USD 1bn revenue typically have 1–3 treasury staff[](cite:afp-bench-2025-article); its 2026 edition found 46% of responding organisations have fewer than five treasury staff[](cite:afp-bench-2026). Both samples are US companies that already have a treasury function. European mid-market groups follow a similar pattern in practice (field observation), with the difference that in DACH companies treasury often sits next to a strong *controlling* function and reports to a CFO who was formerly head of accounting or controlling.

</Callout>

## Team structure

<OrgChart id="helvetic" />

The common shapes, from smallest to largest:

1. **A single treasury manager** (sometimes "Group Treasurer" on the business card), reporting to the CFO, with an assistant or a borrowed accountant. Common at CHF 250–500m revenue.
2. **Treasury manager + analyst** — Helvetic's model. The analyst builds; the manager decides and deals with banks. See [Treasury Manager](/org/roles-treasury-manager) and [Treasury Analyst](/org/roles-analyst), and the days of [Daniel](/day/daniel) and [Lea](/day/lea).
3. **Head of treasury + 2–4 staff** — a cash/forecasting analyst, someone for FX and hedging, sometimes a back-office or systems person. Common at CHF 1–3bn.

Around the team: **group accounting** (confirms deals, books hedges, reconciles intercompany), **FP&A** (supplies the longer-horizon forecast), **tax** (intercompany rates, dividends, withholding tax), and **subsidiary finance managers** who submit forecasts and run local payments. The CFO remains closely involved — approving policy, funding and large payments, and owning the relationship with the lending banks at the top level.

Segregation of duties is the recurring structural problem: a team of two cannot separate dealing, confirming and settling internally, so confirmation is usually given to accounting.

## Systems

- **ERP(s)**: typically SAP or Microsoft Dynamics in the main entities plus different systems in acquired or small subsidiaries.
- **Bank connectivity**: a multibank connectivity tool, a bank-provided multibank portal, or a light cloud TMS collecting statements ([MT940](term:mt940) / camt.053) and sending payment files via [EBICS](term:ebics), [SWIFT](term:swift) or [host-to-host](term:host-to-host).
- **Excel everywhere**: positioning workbook, forecast consolidation, hedge register, covenant model, counterparty limits, bank-account list. Spreadsheets remain the working layer for forecasting in most mid-market treasuries (field observation; see the size gradient on the [overview](/size)).
- **TMS**: this is the tier where the TMS question is most live. Some groups have a cloud TMS; many are "evaluating"; many decide the cost and implementation effort outweigh the gain for 12–50 accounts. See [TMS](/systems/tms).

## Workflows at this size

| Workflow | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| [Daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) | Daily position by entity and currency by mid-morning; manual funding transfers before cut-offs. |
| [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | Rolling 13-week forecast from subsidiary templates plus central flows; weekly variance analysis; the main weekly workload. |
| [Liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning) | 12–18-month plan with FP&A, covenant projections, refinancing timing. |
| [Funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary) | [Intercompany loans](term:intercompany-loan) under framework agreements; in some groups a [cash pool](term:cash-pooling) with one bank for the main currency. |
| [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management) / [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | Written policy with hedge-ratio bands; forwards (sometimes simple options); hedge accounting for larger groups. |
| [Payment processing](wf:payment-processing) | Mostly decentralised; treasury sets rules and signs for the parent. Some groups centralise payments through one connectivity tool. |
| [Credit facility management](wf:credit-facility-management) / [Covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) | Drawings and rollovers under the RCF; semi-annual or quarterly compliance certificates. |
| [Bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) / [Bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) | A register in Excel; periodic [rationalisation](term:bank-account-rationalisation); fee analysis done rarely because it is tedious. |
| [Month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) | Treasury report to the CFO; cash, debt and hedge entries with accounting. |
| [Acquisition integration](wf:acquisition-integration) | The acquired company's banks, accounts and ERP arrive; integration can take a year. |

## Biggest problems

- **Data collection eats the time.** Most hours go into chasing, reshaping and reconciling inputs from subsidiaries and banks rather than deciding. (See the illustrative [time budget at Helvetic](/companies/helvetic).)
- **Several ERPs, no single source of truth.** Forecast templates, exposures and payment data come in different formats from different systems.
- **Key-person risk.** One or two people; holidays and illness hurt; knowledge sits in workbook formulas.
- **Spreadsheet fragility.** Linked workbooks, manual rate updates, copy-paste from ERP exports.
- **Visibility of subsidiary payments and accounts.** Treasury sets rules but often learns of local payments only from the next day's statement; acquired entities keep accounts treasury does not know about.
- **Covenants and refinancing.** The RCF and bond maturities create multi-month projects on top of daily operations.
- **Too big for spreadsheets, too small for a department** — hard to justify both headcount and a TMS.

## Centralisation

<OrgChart id="decentralised-group" />

The typical mid-market model is **centralised decisions, decentralised execution**: treasury owns banks, funding, hedging and policy; local finance keeps payments, AR, AP and local bank contacts. Cash concentration is often manual (transfers documented as intercompany loans) or through a single-currency pool at one bank. In-house banks, netting centres and payment factories are rare at this size; the surveys that report high adoption of those structures refer to very large groups[](cite:pwc-gts-2025). See [treasury centralisation](term:treasury-centralisation) and [org structures](/org/structures).

## Who owns which tasks

| Task | Owns | Performs | Approves / informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily cash position | Treasury manager | Treasury analyst | CFO informed of material issues |
| 13-week forecast | Treasury manager | Analyst; subsidiary finance contributes | CFO reviews monthly |
| Liquidity plan, covenants | Treasury manager | Treasury manager with FP&A | CFO; banks receive certificates |
| FX hedging | Treasury manager (within policy) | Treasury manager deals | Treasury committee / CFO sets ratios; accounting confirms |
| Subsidiary funding | Treasury manager | Analyst prepares, manager approves | Tax sets rates |
| Payments | Local finance | Local AP | Two local signatories; treasury rules and co-signs parent |
| Large / unusual payments | CFO | Analyst checks and releases | CFO (+ CEO / board above thresholds) |
| Bank accounts, signatories | Treasury manager | Analyst | CFO; board resolutions per entity |
| Bank relationships | CFO + treasury manager | Treasury manager | — |
| Treasury policy | CFO | Treasury manager drafts | Board or audit committee approves |

## What pushes a mid-market group toward the multinational model

Rapid international growth, especially into restricted-currency markets; a rating and access to bond markets; enough payment volume to justify a shared-service centre; a series of acquisitions that multiply banks and ERPs. The first structural steps are usually a cash pool, then a TMS, then specialist roles; an in-house bank and payment factory come later, if at all.

## Recognising this tier in an interview

This is the best tier for workflow-level interviews: the people who decide also build the spreadsheets, so they can explain both *why* and *how*. Ask the analyst to walk through this morning's cash position and last week's forecast variance; ask the manager what they overrode in a subsidiary's submission and why, and what the TMS evaluation is really about.
