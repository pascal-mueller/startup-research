<TreasuryMap focus="fx" />

A Swiss company that sells in euros and pays its staff in francs has an FX problem whether or not anyone manages it: if EUR/CHF falls from 0.94 to 0.87 (−7.5%), every euro of margin buys fewer francs. FX management is **finding those exposures, measuring them, and deciding which to reduce** — through business decisions (where to produce, which currency to invoice), [natural hedges](term:natural-hedge) and financial instruments. The dealing itself is a small part of the job; getting a reliable exposure number from the business is the large part.

<Callout type="concept" title="Why Swiss companies care more than most">

The franc is a safe-haven currency that tends to strengthen in crises; Swiss exporters with CHF costs and EUR/USD revenues are structurally "short" foreign currency. The 2015 removal of the EUR/CHF floor is still the reference event for Swiss treasurers — see [economic exposure](term:economic-exposure). For scale: global FX turnover was USD 9.6tn a day in April 2025, with the USD on one side of 89% of trades[](cite:bis-triennial-2025) — corporate hedges are a tiny share, priced off that market.

</Callout>

## Exposure

**Plain:** anything whose value in CHF changes when exchange rates move. **Professional:** [FX exposure](term:fx-exposure) splits into **transaction** exposure (contracted or forecast cash flows in a foreign currency), **translation** exposure (foreign subsidiaries' balance sheets and profits converted into the group's presentation currency) and **economic** exposure (long-term competitiveness). Exposure is measured *net* per currency pair and per entity's [functional currency](term:functional-currency): EUR revenue in a EUR-functional German subsidiary is not an exposure for that subsidiary.

<Example title="Helvetic's EUR exposure, from the forecast" company="helvetic" open>

Lea's quarterly exposure report (a November 2026 cycle — one snapshot; exposure and hedge figures move every monthly cycle — CHF-functional parent and group, EUR m):

| | Q+1 (Q4 2026) | Q+2 (Q1 2027) | Q+3 (Q2 2027) | Q+4 (Q3 2027) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR sales invoiced from Switzerland | 48 | 45 | 42 | 50 |
| EUR costs in Switzerland (German components) | −14 | −13 | −12 | −14 |
| **Net EUR long** | **34** | **32** | **30** | **36** |
| Hedged (forwards outstanding) | 25 | 12 | 10 | 5 |
| **Hedge ratio** | **74%** | **38%** | **33%** | **14%** |
| Policy band | 60–90% | 40–70% | 25–55% | 0–40% |
| Internal target | 75% | 55% | 40% | 25% |

One quarter is out of band: Q1 2027 at 38% sits below its 40–70% band, so Daniel adds hedges this month — [about EUR 6m of new forwards](why:hedge-topup-judgment) to reach his 55% internal target (EUR 0.8m would merely scrape back over the policy floor). Q3 2027 at 14% looks alarming next to a flat "60–90% for everything" reading of the policy, but under the [graded bands](why:hedge-bands-graded) it is inside its 0–40% band by design — below Daniel's 25% internal target, which he builds towards as the quarter comes closer and the forecast firms up. Q4 2026 comes mostly from order backlog (reliable) and sits at 74%, on Daniel's 75% target. Where do the exposure numbers come from? Q+1 mostly from order backlog; Q+3–Q+4 from FP&A's sales forecast — which is precisely why the bands decline.

</Example>

- **Drives:** [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management), [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging), [currency shock](wf:currency-shock).
- **Terms:** [FX exposure](term:fx-exposure), [functional currency](term:functional-currency), [economic exposure](term:economic-exposure), [booked exposure](term:booked-exposure), [exposure report](term:exposure-report).

**Ask:**
- "Where does your exposure number come from, and who at the subsidiaries provides it?"
- "Do you hedge booked exposures only, or forecast ones too? How far out?"
- "What share of your exposure do you think is wrong at the time you hedge it?"

## Transaction exposure

**Plain:** a specific future payment or receipt in a foreign currency whose CHF value is uncertain. **Professional:** [transaction exposure](term:transaction-exposure) arises from receivables, payables, firm commitments (signed orders) and highly probable forecast transactions in a currency other than the entity's functional currency. It hits P&L and cash when the transaction settles at a different rate from the one assumed (often the [budget rate](term:budget-rate)).

<Example title="'USD revenues, CHF costs' at Kleio" company="kleio">

Kleio invoices ~40% of revenue in USD (about USD 6m a year at USD/CHF 0.80) but pays its cloud provider and its 8 US employees in USD too (about USD 5m). Net USD surplus: about USD 1m a year. At USD/CHF 0.80, a 10% USD fall costs about CHF 80k a year — small against CHF 22m of cash. Sarah converts surplus USD to CHF at spot two or three times a year and does not hedge. This is the right answer for Kleio; the same exposure at 20× the size would not be.

</Example>

- **Terms:** [transaction exposure](term:transaction-exposure), [budget rate](term:budget-rate), [spot](term:spot-fx).

**Ask:** "Which currencies do you invoice in, and who decided?" · "Is the budget rate used for pricing, and who sets it?" · "Has a currency move ever changed a pricing or sourcing decision?"

## Translation exposure

**Plain:** the change in the CHF value of a foreign subsidiary when its accounts are converted for the group report. **Professional:** [translation exposure](term:translation-exposure) arises when subsidiaries with other functional currencies are consolidated (IAS 21): net assets are translated at closing rates with differences in other comprehensive income (equity), profits at average rates. It is an accounting effect without an immediate cash flow — until a dividend, sale of the subsidiary or a covenant or rating ratio depends on it. Some groups hedge it with **net investment hedges** (debt or derivatives in the subsidiary's currency).

Example: HM Deutschland's net assets are EUR 80m. If EUR/CHF falls 8% from 0.935 to 0.86, group equity falls by about CHF 6m through OCI. No cash moves. Helvetic does not hedge this; GlobalChem partially hedges its USD net investment because USD-denominated debt naturally offsets it.

- **Terms:** [translation exposure](term:translation-exposure), [functional currency](term:functional-currency), [hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting).

<Callout type="confusion" title="Translation losses are not cash losses">

A CFO may say "we lost CHF 20m on currency this year" — often mostly translation. Ask which part was cash (transaction) and which was translation, and whether they hedge translation at all (many don't, deliberately).

</Callout>

**Ask:** "Do you hedge net investments? Why or why not?" · "Which covenants or rating ratios move with translation?" · "How does the board react to translation losses?"

## Spot

**Plain:** exchanging one currency for another now (in practice, in two business days). **Professional:** a [spot](term:spot-fx) transaction settles [T+2](term:t-plus-2) for most pairs (T+1 for USD/CAD and some others); same-day or next-day value is possible at a small cost and subject to cut-offs. Corporates use spot for converting receipts, funding foreign-currency payments and settling maturing hedges. Price = market mid plus the bank's spread; competition between banks (or a [multi-dealer platform](term:multi-dealer-platform)) keeps spreads down, and [fixings](term:fx-fixing) are used for benchmarkable execution.

Example: Lea needs USD 2m in the US subsidiary's account on Wednesday for a supplier payment; on Monday she buys USD against CHF at spot from two bank quotes, the better one at 0.8010 (illustrative), and instructs settlement: CHF 1.602m leaves Wednesday, USD 2m arrives Wednesday.

- **Terms:** [spot](term:spot-fx), [T+2](term:t-plus-2), [settlement](term:settlement), [FX fixing](term:fx-fixing).

**Ask:** "How do you get FX prices — phone, bank portal, multi-dealer platform?" · "Do you check what spread you pay?" · "Who confirms and settles the trade?"

## Hedging

**Plain:** taking a position that gains when your business exposure loses, so the two roughly cancel. **Professional:** FX hedging reduces the variability of cash flows or values from rate moves, by policy — not to profit from views. The core decisions: **whether** to hedge (materiality, margin sensitivity, ability to reprice), **how much** ([hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), often layered by horizon), **how far ahead**, **which instrument** (forward, option, swap), and **with whom** (bank limits, ISDA in place). A [treasury policy](term:treasury-policy) frames these, and speculative positions are normally forbidden. [Hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting) is a separate decision about how the hedge shows up in P&L — and the applicable framework matters: Helvetic's Swiss GAAP FER treatment is far lighter than the IFRS 9 regime GlobalChem applies.

<Example title="'Should we hedge, how much, which instrument?' at Alpine" company="alpine">

Alpine has just signed a EUR 3.0m project (payments: 30% on order, 50% on delivery in month 5, 20% on acceptance in month 7). Its margin is ~15%, and CHF costs are ~85% of the price. A 7% EUR fall would erase about half the margin. Martin sells EUR 2.1m forward (the delivery and acceptance payments) with the large Swiss bank; the 30% deposit already arrived. He does **not** hedge a EUR 4m bid Alpine may lose — for that the right tool, if any, would be an option. The only "policy" is a board minute allowing hedges of signed contracts up to 12 months.

</Example>

- **Drives:** [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging), [policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance), [currency shock](wf:currency-shock).
- **Terms:** [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), [layered hedging](term:layered-hedging), [hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting), [economic hedge](term:economic-hedge), [hedge documentation](term:hedge-documentation), [ISDA](term:isda).

<Callout type="confusion" title="A hedge that 'lost money' did its job">

If EUR strengthens after Alpine sold EUR forward, the forward shows a loss — and the underlying EUR receipt is worth more. Judging hedges by their own P&L is the most common misunderstanding among non-specialist executives, and a real source of pressure on treasurers.

</Callout>

<Callout type="concept" title="Hedge accounting: what actually bites mid-market groups">

Two different things get called "hedging" in accounting conversations. An **economic hedge** reduces risk and that is the end of it: the derivative's [mark-to-market](term:mark-to-market) runs through P&L and roughly offsets the exposure's own revaluation, with no documentation prepared. **Hedge accounting** is the optional regime that removes that P&L noise by deferring the hedge's value changes in equity until the hedged item itself hits P&L. The burden differs sharply by framework: under IFRS 9 (which [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) applies) a cash-flow hedge of forecast sales needs formal designation, documentation and effectiveness assessment; under Swiss GAAP FER 27 (which [Helvetic](co:helvetic) applies) the market value of forwards hedging future sales is simply booked in equity until the sale is recognised — no effectiveness testing. Ask a mid-market interviewee which framework they report under before assuming IFRS 9-style paperwork.

</Callout>

**Ask:**
- "Walk me through the last hedge decision: who proposed, who approved, what did you look at?"
- "What does your policy say, and when did you last deviate or ask for an exception?"
- "How do you explain hedge results to management when the hedge shows a loss?"
- "Do you apply hedge accounting? Who does the documentation?"

## Forwards

**Plain:** agreeing today on the exchange rate for a future date. **Professional:** an OTC [forward](term:forward) fixes the rate for delivery on a future value date; forward rate = spot ± [forward points](term:forward-points), reflecting the interest differential. With €STR ≈ 2.2–2.4% and SARON ≈ 0% (illustrative), EUR/CHF forwards are below spot: at spot 0.935 the 12-month forward is about 0.913–0.915 — [the points are the interest differential, not a bank fee](why:forward-points-below-spot). Forwards are the workhorse of corporate hedging: no upfront premium, but a binding obligation — and a credit line at the bank. Maturing forwards whose underlying flow is late are extended by [hedge rolls](term:hedge-roll) (swaps).

Example: against its Q1 2027 receipt dates Helvetic sells about EUR 6m forward at roughly 0.928 (illustrative: spot 0.935 less roughly four months of the ≈2.3% euro–franc interest differential), split between two banks for price competition and limit usage. Lea books the deals in the hedge spreadsheet and matches the bank confirmations the same day.

- **Terms:** [forward](term:forward), [forward points](term:forward-points), [OTC](term:otc), [hedge roll](term:hedge-roll), [deal confirmation](term:deal-confirmation).

**Ask:** "Why forwards rather than options?" · "What happens when the customer pays late and the forward matures?" · "Do you compare forward quotes between banks?"

## Swaps

**Plain:** exchanging currencies now and reversing the exchange at a fixed rate later — effectively borrowing one currency against another. **Professional:** an [FX swap](term:swap) combines a spot and an opposite forward; it has no net FX exposure and is used for **liquidity** (funding a EUR need from CHF surplus), **rolling** hedges and **balance-sheet hedging** of intercompany loans. Don't confuse with [cross-currency swaps](term:cross-currency-swap) (multi-year, with interest exchanges, used for debt) or [interest-rate swaps](term:interest-rate-swap).

Example: GlobalChem's in-house bank lends USD 150m to its US subsidiary but is funded in EUR. Jonas's team hedges the USD loan receivable with a rolling 3-month EUR/USD swap; the loan revaluation and the swap MtM offset in P&L ([balance-sheet hedging](term:balance-sheet-hedging)), without hedge accounting.

- **Terms:** [FX swap](term:swap), [cross-currency swap](term:cross-currency-swap), [balance-sheet hedging](term:balance-sheet-hedging), [economic hedge](term:economic-hedge).

**Ask:** "Do you use swaps for funding, for rolling hedges, or for balance-sheet positions?" · "Who decides the roll tenor?" · "How much of your FX volume is swaps versus outright hedges?"

## Options

**Plain:** paying a premium for the right, but not the obligation, to exchange at a set rate — like insurance. **Professional:** an [FX option](term:option) (e.g. a EUR put/CHF call) protects against adverse moves while keeping the upside; the premium depends on volatility, tenor and strike. Useful for uncertain exposures (bids, forecast sales further out) where a forward could turn into an unwanted position. Zero-premium structures ([collars](term:collar)) reduce cost by giving up upside; more exotic structures are usually restricted by policy and complicate hedge accounting.

Example: For Alpine's EUR 4m bid, a 6-month EUR put at 0.92 might cost in the order of 1–2% of notional (illustrative; depends on volatility) — EUR 40–80k that is lost if Alpine loses the bid or EUR rises. Martin decides the bid price will include a currency clause instead. GlobalChem uses options for about a fifth of its forecast hedges beyond six months.

- **Terms:** [FX option](term:option), [collar](term:collar), [hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting).

**Ask:** "Does your policy allow options? Which ones?" · "How does management react to paying premium that 'expires worthless'?" · "What hedges bids or tenders?"

## Natural hedging

**Plain:** matching currencies of income and costs so they cancel out without derivatives. **Professional:** [natural hedges](term:natural-hedge) come from operational choices — producing or sourcing in the currency of sales, invoicing in the cost currency, borrowing in the currency of assets or cash flows (e.g. a EUR [Schuldschein](term:schuldschein) for EUR earnings) — and from [netting](term:netting) opposing exposures across entities before hedging. It reduces cost and hedge volume but is slow to change and imperfect in timing.

Example: Helvetic's German and Czech plants give it EUR and CZK costs against EUR revenue; about a third of its EUR sales are naturally hedged. GlobalChem nets ~30 currencies through the in-house bank each month, cutting the gross amount to hedge by more than half before Jonas's team trades.

- **Terms:** [natural hedge](term:natural-hedge), [netting](term:netting), [economic exposure](term:economic-exposure).

**Ask:** "Which natural hedges do you have, and who knows about them outside treasury?" · "Do you net before hedging?" · "Has FX ever influenced where you produce or buy?"

## Hedge ratios

**Plain:** the percentage of an exposure that is hedged. **Professional:** [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio) = hedged amount ÷ exposure, typically set as bands per horizon in the policy ([layered hedging](term:layered-hedging)), declining with forecast uncertainty. In the vendor-sponsored MillTech 2025 survey of ~750 mid-sized corporates, the average reported hedge ratio was about 48% with an average tenor of about 5.3 months[](cite:milltech-fx-2025) — treat as indicative only.

Example: Helvetic's policy, approved by the board in December 2025 — [60–90% for the next rolling quarter (Q+1)](why:hedge-bands-graded), 40–70% for Q+2, 25–55% for Q+3, 0–40% for Q+4, nothing beyond 12 months; Daniel's internal targets sit mid-band at 75 / 55 / 40 / 25%. The 60–90% band is the Q+1 band only. In the exposure table above, Q+1 at 74% is on target; Q+2 (Q1 2027) at 38% is below its band and needs about EUR 6m of new forwards; Q+4 at 14% is inside its 0–40% band by design.

- **Terms:** [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), [layered hedging](term:layered-hedging), [treasury policy](term:treasury-policy), [cash flow at risk](term:cash-flow-at-risk).

**Ask:** "Why those percentages? Who chose them and when were they last reviewed?" · "What happens when the exposure forecast falls and you are over-hedged?" · "Do you report hedge ratios to the board?"
