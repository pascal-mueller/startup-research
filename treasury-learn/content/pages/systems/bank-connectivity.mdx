## What has to flow

Between a company and each of its banks, two directions of traffic:

- **Outbound:** payment instructions ([pain.001](term:pain-001) or legacy [MT101](term:mt101) and bank-proprietary formats), sometimes FX or deposit instructions, account-maintenance requests.
- **Inbound:** end-of-day statements ([camt.053](term:camt-053), [MT940](term:mt940), [BAI2](term:bai2) in the US), intraday reports ([camt.052](term:camt-052), MT942), payment status reports ([pain.002](term:pain-002)), debit/credit notifications ([camt.054](term:camt-054)), fee statements ([camt.086](term:bank-services-billing)), deal confirmations.

[Bank connectivity](term:bank-connectivity) is the set of channels, formats, keys and contracts that make this flow automatically.

## The five channels

### E-banking portals

Each bank's website or app. A person logs in (hardware token, app or card reader), keys single payments or uploads a pain.001 generated by the ERP, approves with a second user, and downloads statements. **Everyone** uses portals: for [Kleio](co:kleio) they are the whole connectivity; at [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) they remain for the few accounts not worth automating and as a fallback when the main channel fails.

What breaks: user administration per bank (leavers keep tokens), statement downloads forgotten on busy days, no single view across banks, and approval limits set separately in each bank's portal rather than in one place.

### EBICS

[EBICS](term:ebics) is a file-exchange protocol used in Germany, France, Switzerland and Austria. The company runs an EBICS client — an ERP add-on, a [multibank connectivity tool](term:multibank-connectivity), a TMS or a small desktop client — which exchanges files with each bank's EBICS server.

**Setup, per bank:** sign an EBICS contract; the bank sets up the customer and users with their rights; each user generates keys in the client; the key "initialisation letters" (INI/HIA) are signed and sent to the bank; the bank activates the keys; test files are exchanged. Payment approval uses [distributed electronic signatures](term:distributed-signature): a file sent with a transport signature waits at the bank until enough authorised signatures are added, so signing authority is enforced partly bank-side.

**Currentness:** EBICS 3.0 harmonised the German, French and Swiss variants. In Switzerland, Zürcher Kantonalbank discontinued EBICS 2.4 on 21 November 2025 and, from November 2026, supports the 2019 ISO 20022 versions (pain.001.001.09, camt.05x.001.08) exclusively via EBICS 3.0[](cite:zkb-ebics3); other Swiss banks publish their own dates. Combined with the retirement of 2009 message versions in Switzerland in November 2026[](cite:six-sps-cash-mgmt), many Swiss companies need an EBICS client and format upgrade in 2026.

What breaks: expired or blocked keys (one bank blocked keys with insufficient key length from February 2025[](cite:zkb-ebics3)), a user leaves and nobody can sign, a bank changes its server certificate and the client rejects it, a format version is rejected after a bank's release.

### Swift

[Swift](term:swift) is the banks' messaging network. A corporate joins it to use **one channel for many banks** in many countries. Access options for corporates are: direct (own Swift infrastructure), Swift's cloud offering Alliance Lite2 (including when embedded in a partner application such as a TMS), or indirect via a Swift-certified [service bureau](term:service-bureau)[](cite:swift-lite2). Corporates exchange traffic with banks either in SCORE (multi-bank) or in one bank's closed user group ([SCORE / MA-CUG](term:swift-score)), and still need bilateral relationship keys with each bank.

Messages used by corporates: FIN messages (MT101 payment requests, MT940/942 statements, MT300 FX confirmations) and [FileAct](term:fileact) for whole files (pain.001 batches, camt reports).

**Currentness:** The November 2025 end of MT/ISO 20022 coexistence applied to cross-border payment messages *between banks* (CBPR+)[](cite:jpm-iso-2026,swift-iso-mt9xx). It did not switch off corporate-to-bank MT101 or MT940/942 reporting, which banks continue to support on their own timelines — but richer ISO 20022 data now travels through the bank chain, and banks push clients towards pain.001 with structured data.

Who uses it: typically multinationals and larger mid-market groups with many banks across countries. GlobalChem connects roughly 20 banks through a service bureau. For [Helvetic](co:helvetic), Swift carries the global bank's US and UK accounts, EBICS the European ones.

What breaks: onboarding each bank takes weeks to months (bank implementation queues, testing); annual security attestation under Swift's Customer Security Programme applies to Swift users (a reason many choose a service bureau or Lite2); relationship keys expire; a bank sends MT940 with proprietary codes in the free-text field.

### Host-to-host

[Host-to-host](term:host-to-host) (H2H) is a dedicated link — typically SFTP with PGP encryption or certificates — between the company (or its TMS/connectivity provider) and **one** bank. Formats are that bank's variants. Used for high-volume flows (payroll, large AP runs), with banks in markets where EBICS does not exist (US, UK, Asia), or where a bank offers it as its preferred corporate channel.

What breaks: each link is its own project; certificate and key rotations; the bank changes its file specification; monitoring is whatever the company builds (a file that silently didn't arrive is found when someone notices a missing statement).

### Bank APIs

[Bank APIs](term:api-banking) are REST interfaces for balances, transactions, payment initiation and status. Large banks offer corporate API portfolios; newer cash platforms rely on them for intraday visibility. In the EU/UK, [open banking](term:open-banking) under PSD2 standardised account access for licensed third parties, but it was built for retail and small business use: multi-user approvals, bulk files and corporate entitlements are not what it was designed for. In Switzerland, API access has developed through market initiatives and bank-by-bank offerings rather than regulation.

What breaks: every bank's API differs in coverage, data model and authentication; history and full remittance detail may be missing compared with a camt.053; token expiry and consent renewal. At most companies APIs **complement** file channels — intraday balances via API, the official end-of-day statement still as a file.

## Formats in one table

| Purpose | ISO 20022 | Legacy / regional | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payment instruction (company → bank) | pain.001 | MT101; bank-proprietary; US NACHA files | Country variants (Swiss Payment Standards, CGI-MP); structured addresses increasingly required[](cite:postfinance-iso-updates) |
| Payment status (bank → company) | pain.002 | proprietary acknowledgements | Rejections arrive here; someone must read them |
| End-of-day statement | camt.053 | MT940; BAI2 (US) | Basis for position and [reconciliation](term:reconciliation) |
| Intraday report | camt.052 | MT942; BAI2 current-day | Provisional; do not double count with camt.053 |
| Debit/credit notification | camt.054 | proprietary | Breaks down batch bookings; QR/creditor references in Switzerland |
| Bank fees | camt.086 | TWIST BSB; AFP service codes; PDF | Needed for [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis)[](cite:hsbc-camt086) |

"Same format" rarely means identical: banks implement usage rules differently (optional fields, codes, how batch bookings are shown), which is why connectivity vendors maintain libraries of bank-specific variants.

## Who sets it up, who owns it

- **Treasury** usually owns the *decision* (which channel, which banks) and the bank-side contracts and mandates.
- **IT / finance systems** own the technical side: servers or SaaS configuration, ERP interfaces, security review, certificates.
- **Banks** each run an onboarding process: implementation manager, test environment, format certification, go-live.
- **Vendors / service bureaus** provide the connectivity software and often do bank onboarding on the customer's behalf.
- At large groups a [treasury-systems role](/org/roles-operations) (GlobalChem's Ben) coordinates all of them. At SMEs, it is whoever set it up — often the head of accounting and an ERP partner — and knowledge sits with one person.

Practitioner accounts consistently describe the same work: bank-by-bank onboarding queues, test cycles on format variants, signatory and user set-up at each bank, and re-doing it when a bank changes its platform.
