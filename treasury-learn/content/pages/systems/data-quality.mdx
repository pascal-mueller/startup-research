## Why this page matters

Most of the time analysts and operations staff spend on "systems" is spent on exceptions: the statement that didn't arrive, the bank line nothing matched, the subsidiary forecast that doesn't add up. These exceptions are where manual work concentrates at every company size — and where the people you interview can describe their work most concretely. European treasurers themselves cite missing central data and non-standardised data as the reason AI ranks low on their agendas[](cite:eact-2025).

## Bank statements

| Failure | What it looks like | How it's noticed | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement missing | 11 of 12 accounts in the position; one bank shows yesterday's balance | Completeness check (if someone built one) or an odd number in the position | Analyst chases the bank or the local team; treasury ops raises a ticket with the connectivity provider |
| Statement late | A bank's prior-day file arrives after the morning decision | Position built without it; decision taken on estimates | Analyst notes the estimate; treasury asks the bank for an earlier delivery or an intraday report |
| Duplicate import | Balances and flows doubled after a re-send | Position jumps; reconciliation fails | Ops deletes the duplicate; checks the import rules |
| Balance break | Opening balance today ≠ closing balance yesterday | Automated continuity check in a TMS; otherwise at reconciliation | Ops asks the bank for the missing statement or a correction |
| Intraday vs final | Provisional [camt.052](term:camt-052) entries counted again when the [camt.053](term:camt-053) arrives | Position too high by the amount of intraday credits | System rules; analyst awareness |
| Value date vs booking date | Balance "available" differs from "booked"; interest calculated on the wrong one | Cash seems to be there but a payment bounces / overdraft interest | Analyst uses the right balance type; ops checks [value dating](term:value-date) against bank terms |
| Format change | After a bank release, a field moves or a code changes; import rejects the file or mis-maps it | Import error, or silently wrong categories | Treasury systems / connectivity vendor adjusts mapping |
| Account not connected | Accounts at banks that offer no corporate channel at acceptable cost — Helvetic's Chinese bank account is the standing example — or newly acquired subsidiaries not yet onboarded never come electronically | They are always the manual lines in the workbook | A connectivity project, or accepted as manual |

<Example title="The missing statement" company="helvetic">

On a Tuesday, the multibank tool shows 10 of 11 electronic statements at 07:45 — the German bank's EUR account is missing. Lea checks the EBICS job log: the download ran, but the bank returned "no data available". She calls the bank's electronic-banking helpdesk, which confirms a processing delay on their side, and she uses the bank portal's intraday view to get a provisional balance (EUR 3.1m). She marks the line "portal, provisional" in the position and tells Daniel, who [postpones a EUR 2m transfer out of that account](why:systems-postpone-on-missing-statement) until the statement arrives at 10:20. The fix was a phone call; the cost was two hours of uncertainty on the day's biggest EUR balance.

</Example>

## Mapping and codes

Statement lines have to be classified — into [cash-flow categories](term:cash-flow-category) for treasury and into GL postings for accounting. The classification relies on [bank transaction codes](term:bank-transaction-code), counterparty names and text patterns.

- **Different codes for the same thing.** Bank A reports a card-acquirer settlement with one ISO family code; bank B uses a proprietary code; the MT940 from bank C puts it in free text. Each needs a rule.
- **Rules decay.** A customer changes its name, a bank changes its fee text, a new subsidiary opens an account — the rule no longer fires and lines fall into "unclassified" or onto a [suspense account](term:suspense-account).
- **Batch bookings.** One statement line for a whole payment run or a whole card-settlement day; the detail comes separately ([camt.054](term:camt-054)) or not at all, so matching to invoices requires the detail file.
- **Intercompany.** Transfers between group accounts must be recognised as internal on both sides or the forecast and the position double count.

Who fixes it: accounting maintains [posting rules](term:posting-rules) in the ERP; treasury (or its systems team) maintains classification rules in the TMS. When they are maintained separately, treasury's and accounting's views of the same bank line can disagree.

<Example title="Rule decay at scale" company="globalchem">

One of GlobalChem's banks changes the code family it uses for card-acquirer settlements. About 600 statement lines a month — one subsidiary's card takings — stop firing their classification rule and fall into "unclassified" instead of "customer receipts". Nothing breaks loudly: the cash position is still right, but the receipts-vs-forecast variance for that entity looks like a mysterious shortfall until a back-office analyst traces it to the code change. Ben's treasury-systems team patches the mapping rule in the TMS and reclassifies the backlog. The patch is an hour; noticing it took the variance review.

</Example>

## Remittance information

Receipts are only useful for reconciliation if they say what they pay. [Remittance information](term:remittance-information) breaks in predictable ways:

- **Missing or generic** — "payment June" instead of invoice numbers. AR matches by hand.
- **Truncated** — MT940's free-text field is limited (six lines of 65 characters); unstructured ISO 20022 remittance is limited to 140 characters per occurrence, but the field repeats (up to ten occurrences in cross-border usage), which is why the "get cut" story is milder there. Long invoice lists still get cut in MT940.
- **Lost in the chain** — intermediary banks or conversion between formats drop data (the ISO 20022 migration is intended to reduce this for cross-border payments[](cite:swift-iso-mt9xx)).
- **Structured references ignored** — in Switzerland the QR-bill reference makes matching near-automatic ([QR-bill](term:qr-bill)), but only for customers who use it.

Owned by: AR / [cash application](term:cash-application) in accounting, with treasury affected through reconciliation and forecast accuracy.

## Master and static data

| Data | Failure | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor bank details (ERP) | Changed after a fraudulent email; typo in IBAN | Payment to a fraudster or rejection ([payment fraud](wf:fraud-investigation)) |
| Bank account register | New account not added to TMS / connectivity; closed account still in it | Invisible cash; false "missing statement" alarms |
| Bank codes (BIC, clearing numbers) | Bank merger or BIC change not reflected | Payment files rejected |
| Counterparty settlement instructions ([static data](term:static-data)) | Out of date in the TMS | FX settlement to the wrong account; failed settlement |
| Signatories and users | Leavers not removed | Control failure found by audit ([eBAM](term:ebam)) |
| Holiday calendars | Local holiday missing | Value dates and cut-offs wrong; payment late |

## Market data

Stale rates after a feed failure, the wrong fixing for month-end, missing holiday calendars, onshore vs offshore rates for restricted currencies. See [Market data](/systems/market-data#failure-modes).

## Forecast submissions

The largest and least technical data-quality problem. Typical failures in [forecast submissions](term:forecast-submission) from subsidiaries and business units:

| Failure | Example | How treasury catches it |
|---|---|---|
| Late | HM Suzhou submits on Wednesday for a Tuesday consolidation | Chasing; last week's numbers rolled forward with a flag |
| Copied | The UK entity's receipts are identical to last month's to the franc | Variance review; comparison with prior versions |
| P&L instead of cash | Revenue submitted by month of invoicing, not by week of receipt | Comparison with AR ageing and customer payment behaviour |
| Wrong sign or currency | A payment entered as a receipt; EUR amounts in a CHF column | Plausibility checks; totals vs prior week |
| Missing categories | Tax payments, bonuses, capex left out | Treasury's own calendar of known large flows |
| Intercompany mismatch | Italy expects EUR 1.2m from Germany; Germany doesn't plan to pay it | Intercompany matrix must net to zero |
| Template changed | Someone inserted a column; the consolidation link now reads the wrong cell | Totals check against the submission |

Actual-vs-forecast [variance analysis](term:variance-analysis) is the main feedback loop: it shows which entities and categories are systematically wrong, and gives treasury the evidence to push back. See [Cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting).

<Callout type="synthesis" title="Pattern across all of these">

The failures that cost the most time are not technical outages but **silent mismatches**: a line that is classified wrongly, a rate that is a day old, a forecast that is copied. Completeness and plausibility checks — did every account report, does the opening balance match, did every entity submit, do the totals move plausibly — catch most of them. Where they exist, they were usually built by the analyst who got burned.

</Callout>
