## What market data treasury needs

| Data | Used for | Typical source | Who looks at it |
|---|---|---|---|
| FX spot rates | Converting the cash position to CHF; indicative prices before dealing | Bank FX portal, Bloomberg / LSEG, free central-bank publications | Analyst, cash manager, FX manager |
| FX forward points | Pricing and valuing [forwards](term:forward) ([forward points](term:forward-points)) | Bank quotes, trading platform, terminal | FX/risk manager, middle office |
| FX fixings / reference rates | Accounting revaluation, contracts, budget rates ([FX fixing](term:fx-fixing)) | ECB reference rates, WM/Reuters, tax authorities' rates | Accounting, treasury |
| Interest-rate fixings | Interest on floating debt and deposits: SARON (SIX), €STR (ECB), SOFR (NY Fed), Euribor (EMMI) | Published by administrators; terminal feed | Corporate finance, back office |
| Yield curves | [Mark-to-market](term:mark-to-market) of swaps and forwards; discounting | Terminal / TMS feed | Middle office, risk |
| Money-market fund and deposit rates | [Surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment) decisions | Bank offers, fund platforms | Cash manager |
| Credit spreads, ratings, CDS | [Counterparty limits](term:counterparty-limit), funding decisions | Terminal; rating agencies | Head of treasury, risk |
| Commodity prices | Exposure for commodity-intensive groups | Exchanges, terminal | Risk manager |

Current levels in this manual's illustrative autumn-2026 setting: EUR/CHF around 0.93–0.94, USD/CHF around 0.79–0.81, SNB policy rate and SARON around 0%, €STR around 2.2–2.4%, SOFR around 3.5–4%. With CHF rates near zero, surplus CHF earns almost nothing, which is why a group with drawn bank debt reduces drawings rather than placing CHF deposits, while EUR and USD surpluses still earn meaningful overnight rates.

## Three rates for "the same" exchange rate

<Callout type="confusion" title="Screen rate, deal rate, accounting rate">

- **Screen / indicative rate** — what a terminal or bank portal shows right now. Nobody has agreed to trade at it.
- **Deal rate** — the rate actually agreed with a bank for a specific trade, including the bank's margin. It lands in the TMS or hedge register from the confirmation.
- **Accounting rate** — a fixing (e.g. the ECB reference rate of month-end, or a group rate set monthly) loaded into the ERP for revaluation and reporting.

When Helvetic's month-end result shows an "FX loss" that surprises Thomas, the difference between these three is usually part of the explanation.

</Callout>

<Example title="CHF 8.2k of 'FX loss' on one EUR 2m conversion" company="helvetic" open>

Lea buys EUR 2m for the European payment run. The bank portal shows a screen rate of 0.9350; the desk deals at 0.9362 after its margin; the month-end ECB fixing is 0.9321. In the position the EUR cost CHF 1,872,400; at month-end the EUR balance is revalued at 0.9321 = CHF 1,864,200, and the CHF 8,200 difference lands in the P&L as an FX loss that Thomas asks about — of which CHF 2,400 is the bank's margin (0.9362 vs 0.9350) and the rest the market's move since the deal date. Economically nothing went wrong: the euros were bought to be spent. [Where the CHF 8.2k comes from](why:systems-three-rates-loss).

</Example>

## Dealing platforms

Mid-market and large treasuries increasingly execute FX and money-market deals on electronic platforms instead of by phone: multi-dealer request-for-quote platforms (360T, owned by Deutsche Börse; FXall, part of LSEG; Bloomberg FXGO) or single-bank portals. The actors make it concrete: at [Helvetic](co:helvetic) it is Daniel, after Lea's forecast and exposure report have told him what to hedge and before the bank's cut-off — he sends the same request to several relationship banks, takes the best quote, and the deal lands in the hedge register. At [GlobalChem](co:globalchem) it is Jonas's desk putting the RFQ on the multi-dealer platform and Marta's back office matching the confirmations into SAP. The benefits practitioners cite are evidence of best execution for auditors and fewer manual keying errors; the limit is that the platform executes decisions — it does not decide what to hedge.

## Cost and administration

Terminal licences are per user and a material budget item for small teams, which is why treasuries below the large-multinational tier rely on bank portals, bank research and free central-bank data. Rate feeds into a TMS are licensed separately from the terminal. Someone (at GlobalChem, Ben's systems team) must monitor that the feed arrived: a stale rate table silently revalues everything at yesterday's prices.

## Failure modes

- **Stale rates.** The feed failed overnight; the TMS values the portfolio at yesterday's rates and nobody notices until the valuation looks odd.
- **Wrong fixing.** Accounting uses one reference rate, treasury another; month-end reconciliation shows a difference nobody can explain for a day.
- **Holiday calendars.** A fixing is not published on a local holiday; value dates of forwards roll incorrectly if calendars are not maintained.
- **Illiquid or restricted currencies.** Onshore and offshore rates differ (e.g. CNY vs CNH); which one applies depends on where the cash is.
