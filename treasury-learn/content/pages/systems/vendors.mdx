## How to read this page

Treasurers name products constantly ("we're on Kyriba", "we run SAP TRM", "we use Agicap"). The name tells you the **category** of system and the order of magnitude of the company; it does not tell you which modules are in use, how well connected the banks are, or what still lives in Excel — two "Kyriba customers" can be a scale-up with two banks connected and a multinational with twenty. Always follow up with "which parts do you use every day?"

Each profile below states the category, typical company size, current ownership and product state where it matters, why companies use it, the data flows, the users, and what it does not solve. Each ends with a currentness marker: **checked** means ownership and product facts were confirmed against search-result text from the vendor's or a news publisher's own pages in September 2026 (pages seen as snippets, not read in full[](cite:sap-mbc,sap-ecc-maintenance,kyriba-ga-2024,kyriba-live-2026,fis-quantum-cloud-2025,ripple-gtreasury-2025,ripple-treasury-rebrand,gtreasury-solvexia-2026,ion-treasury,coupa-bellin-2020,trovata-atom-2025,embat-series-b-2026)); **not re-verified** means a long-established description that you should check on the vendor site before relying on details.

<Callout type="warning" title="Vendor claims are not evidence">

Product pages describe capabilities; they do not tell you adoption, implementation time or customer satisfaction. Customer counts, "automates X% of tasks" and similar figures are company claims. Features announced at user conferences (e.g. agentic AI, stablecoin settlement in 2026) are announcements, not evidence of use. Ask customers.

</Callout>

## Which products the reference companies use

- **[Kleio](co:kleio)** (startup): no treasury product. Cloud accounting with bank feeds, bank portals, a Google Sheet. A cloud cash tool such as Agicap is the kind of product a company like Kleio might consider once it has several banks and currencies.
- **[Alpine](co:alpine)** (SME): Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central for payments and bank reconciliation, EBICS upload, Excel. No TMS — with 9 accounts, the ERP and a spreadsheet cover the need.
- **[Helvetic](co:helvetic)** (mid-market): SAP S/4HANA in three countries, a multibank connectivity tool, Excel. The TMS evaluation typically compares a cloud cash platform (cheaper, faster, less deal functionality) with an enterprise TMS (more depth, heavier project) and — because of SAP — SAP's own treasury components.
- **[GlobalChem](co:globalchem)** (multinational): SAP S/4HANA treasury modules as the TMS, Swift via a service bureau, a multi-dealer FX platform, Bloomberg, and a separate forecasting tool.

## Not profiled here

Other names you will hear, not profiled on this page: Serrala, TIS (Treasury Intelligence Solutions, payments and connectivity), Salmon Software, SAP add-on vendors, banks' own treasury portals, planning tools used for cash forecasting (e.g. Anaplan, Jedox), and Swiss SME finance tools. Their absence says nothing about quality. The [Competitor Landscape](/competitors) covers positioning and gaps.
