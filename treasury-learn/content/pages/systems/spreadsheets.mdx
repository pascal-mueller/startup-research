## Why Excel persists

<Callout type="survey">

In Deloitte's 2024 global treasury survey, about a fifth of respondents — 22%, as reported by Deloitte (figure not confirmed against the primary text) — said their cash-positioning capability still requires development[](cite:deloitte-gts-2024). A 2025 Strategic Treasurer/TIS survey (vendor-sponsored) found the share of practitioners calling forecasting "difficult" rose from 39% in 2018 to 53% in 2025[](cite:st-tis-cfv-2025). An older, vendor-sponsored ACT/Kyriba survey showed the size gradient: small companies relied on Excel-type tools for daily cash management far more than large ones[](cite:kyriba-act-2013) — directionally still what practitioners describe.

</Callout>

The reasons treasurers give are rarely "we don't know better" — the pattern is systematic, [not nostalgic](why:systems-spreadsheets-survive):

- **The data comes from everywhere.** When inputs arrive from five ERPs, four bank channels and eight subsidiaries' emails, the spreadsheet is the only tool that accepts all of them without an IT project.
- **Judgment overlays.** A forecast is data plus adjustments ("the Milan customer pays 20 days late", "the tax payment moved"). Spreadsheets make overlays easy and visible to the person who made them.
- **Cost and scale.** For [Alpine](co:alpine), with 2 banks and 9 accounts, a well-built workbook costs nothing and does the job. A TMS would be overhead.
- **Ad hoc questions.** "What if the RCF is cut by CHF 50m and EUR/CHF drops 8%?" is faster to model in Excel than to configure in a system.
- **Gaps between systems.** Even with a TMS, the last mile — board packs, covenant models, scenario analysis, bank fee comparisons — often lives in Excel.
- **Distrust and parallel running.** New systems run alongside the old workbook until treasury trusts them; some workbooks never get switched off.

<Example title="Kleio's one Google Sheet" company="kleio" open>

Every Monday, Sarah spends about 20 minutes on the Google Sheet that is Kleio's treasury system. She logs into the two Swiss banks' e-banking portals and the US bank's login, downloads balances for the seven accounts, exports the week's Stripe payouts from the dashboard, and pastes both into the sheet — the accounting software already carries payroll, cloud and rent, so the sheet's job is the runway line. The one input that moves the number this week is a deferred renewal: an enterprise customer's annual prepayment, expected in July, now landing in August. Runway refreshes at [about 24 months](why:systems-kleio-runway) — balance ÷ burn is trivial arithmetic; the judgment is which inflows are prepayments that inflate the balance and which hires are actually committed. This is what "the CFO is the integration layer" means at startup scale.

</Example>

## The typical treasury workbooks

| Workbook | What's in it | Who maintains it | Updated | Size where common |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily cash position | Balances per account, today's expected flows, target balances, CHF equivalent | Analyst ([Lea](/org/roles-analyst)); Petra at Alpine | Daily | SME, mid-market |
| 13-week cash forecast | Receipts and payments by week, by entity; actual vs forecast | Analyst; reviewed by treasury manager | Weekly | Everywhere |
| Subsidiary [forecast submission](term:forecast-submission) template | One tab per entity, fixed categories, local currency | Subsidiary finance fill it in; treasury consolidates | Weekly/monthly | Mid-market and up |
| FX exposure and hedge register | Forecast exposures, hedge ratios, open forwards, maturities | FX manager or treasury manager | Weekly + on each deal | Mid-market |
| Intercompany loan register | Lender, borrower, amount, currency, rate, maturity | Treasury; checked by accounting | On each change | Mid-market |
| Debt and covenant model | Facilities, drawings, interest, covenant ratios and headroom | Treasury / corporate finance; reviewed by accounting | Monthly, and before each test | Anyone with covenants |
| Bank account and signatory list | Accounts, purpose, signatories, e-banking users | Treasury ops / head of accounting | On change; quarterly review | SME, mid-market |
| Counterparty limit monitor | Deposits and derivative exposure per bank vs limits | Treasury | Daily/weekly | Mid-market and up |
| Bank fee analysis | Fees per bank vs agreed pricing | Treasury / ops | Quarterly/annually | Mid-market and up |
| Board / CFO liquidity pack | Headroom, forecast, covenants, hedging summary | Treasury manager / head of treasury | Monthly/quarterly | Everywhere |

<Example title="Helvetic's workbook landscape" company="helvetic">

Lea maintains six workbooks that the treasury process depends on: the daily position (one tab per bank plus a summary), the 13-week forecast (eight subsidiary input tabs linked to a consolidation tab), the hedge register (about 40 open EUR/CHF and USD/CHF forwards), the intercompany loan register, the covenant model for the RCF (net debt/EBITDA [≤ 3.25x](why:covenant-baseline), interest cover [≥ 4x](why:cover-not-binding)), and the bank account list for 12 accounts at 5 banks. When the TMS project team asked what the future system had to replace, this list was the scope document.

</Example>

## The risks

| Risk | What it looks like | Typical control |
|---|---|---|
| Key-person dependency | Only Lea understands the forecast links; she is on holiday during the covenant test | Documentation, a second trained person, simpler structure |
| Formula and link errors | A row inserted in a subsidiary tab shifts a SUM range; a link points to last month's file | Check totals against source; locked formulas; review by a second person |
| Version confusion | "Forecast_v7_final_DK2.xlsx" vs "Forecast_v7_final.xlsx" | One storage location, naming rule, change log |
| No audit trail | Nobody can show who changed an assumption, and when | Change-log tab; storing snapshots; moving critical registers into a system |
| Stale inputs | Balances pasted yesterday reused today | Date stamps on every input; completeness checks |
| Unauthorised change | Hedge register edited to hide a deal | [Segregation of duties](term:segregation-of-duties): deals confirmed by back office against bank confirmations, not against the register |

Auditors call these [end-user computing (EUC)](term:euc) risks and increasingly ask for an inventory of critical spreadsheets and evidence of review.
