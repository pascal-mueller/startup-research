## The principle

Every strong question on this page asks about **a specific, recent, real instance** of work — "the last forecast", "this morning", "the last time a payment was rejected" — and then **excavates** it step by step: what triggered it, where each input came from, which system, who else, what judgment, what happened next. People describe what they *actually* did far more reliably than what they *usually* do or *would* do[](cite:torres-wrong-questions,mom-test); the interviewer's job is to keep the story specific and keep it moving[](cite:torres-story-based). Switch-interview practice adds a timeline lens for changes and purchases: from the "first thought" that the old way wasn't working to the decision[](cite:moesta-switch).

<Callout type="synthesis" title="What you are listening for">

In every answer, try to capture six things: **trigger** (why the work started), **inputs and their sources**, **systems and files touched**, **people contacted** (hand-offs), **judgment** (where rules stop and experience starts), and **consequence** (what the output changed, and what happens when it's wrong). These map directly onto the workflow pages' structure, which makes the [debrief](/interview/before-and-after) easy.

</Callout>

## By interview phase

A 45-minute interview typically runs: context (5 min) → one last-instance walk-through (20–25 min) → judgment, escalation and failure (10 min) → tools, workarounds and change (5–10 min) → close (3 min). Don't try to cover every workflow; one walk-through done properly beats five done superficially.

### 1. Context — who is this person, really?

| Question | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|
| "What were the last three things you worked on?" | "Which of those happens every week?" · "Which did you hand to someone else?" |
| "Who do you hand work to, and who hands work to you?" | "Who's the last person you emailed about it?" · "Who do you sit closest to?" |
| "What's a normal Monday for you — and when did you last have one?" | "What broke the last one?" |
| "How is treasury set up here — who does what, roughly?" | "Who does the payments?" · "Is there anyone in the subsidiaries who does treasury work?" |

**Good answer sounds like:** named tasks, days and counterparts. *"Monday I consolidate the 13-week forecast — Italy and China are usually late, I chase them by 11 — then Daniel reviews it Tuesday before the CFO call."*

**Evasive / generic answer sounds like:** functional descriptions. *"We're responsible for group liquidity and financial risk management."* → Probe: *"What did that mean last Monday?"* If they cannot get concrete, you may be talking to a decider, not a doer — adjust your guide (see [wrong-person signals](/interview/before-and-after#red-flags-youre-talking-to-the-wrong-person)).

### 2. The last-instance walk-through (the core)

Pick **one** workflow the person actually performs and walk through the most recent instance.

| Question | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through what you did this morning, from the first screen you opened." | "What were you looking for?" · "What did you do with it?" · "What was missing?" |
| "Walk me through the last cash forecast you produced." | "Where did the first number come from?" · "Which line took longest?" · "Who did you have to chase?" |
| "What triggered that work?" | "Is it calendar-driven, or did something happen?" · "Who would notice if you didn't do it?" |
| "Where did each input come from?" | "Is that a system extract, an email, a file someone sends?" · "How late does it usually arrive?" · "How do you know it's right?" |
| "Which systems did you open?" | "In what order?" · "What do you use that one for that the other can't do?" · "How many logins?" |
| "What did you export into Excel?" | "What do you do to it there?" · "Why can't that be done in the system?" · "Who else uses that file?" |
| "Then what happened?" (repeat) | "Who got it next?" · "What did they send back?" · "What did they change?" |
| "How long did that take, start to finish — and how much was waiting?" | "Is that typical?" · "What made last week longer?" |

**Good answer sounds like:** sequence, specifics and friction. *"I log into three bank portals and the connectivity tool — the Chinese bank doesn't feed it, so I screenshot the balance and type it into the sheet. Then I compare against yesterday's forecast. Germany was CHF 600k below; I checked the open-items list, saw a customer receipt hadn't arrived, emailed Jens, and flagged it to Daniel."*

**Evasive / generic answer sounds like:** "the system does it", "it's pretty automated", "we have a process for that". → Probe: *"What's the last thing you had to do by hand in it?"* or *"What did you check before you trusted it this morning?"* Automation claims are often true for the happy path and false for exceptions — the exceptions are the work.

### 3. Judgment, escalation and failure

| Question | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|
| "What required judgment in that?" | "What would a new joiner get wrong?" · "How did you learn that?" |
| "Which numbers did you change, and why?" | "What did you know that the system didn't?" · "Who told you?" |
| "What makes you escalate to your manager?" | "When did you last do it?" · "Is there a written threshold, or is it a feeling?" |
| "What gets escalated to the Head of Treasury — or the CFO?" | "What happened the last time?" · "What did they want to see?" |
| "What happened the last time the forecast was badly wrong?" | "Who noticed first?" · "What did it cost, or nearly cost?" · "What changed afterwards?" |
| "What happens when something goes wrong — a payment fails, a statement is missing, a subsidiary runs short?" | "Tell me about the last time." · "Who did you call?" · "How long until it was resolved?" |
| "What happens after you identify excess cash?" | "Who decides how much is really surplus?" · "Where did the last surplus go?" · "What stopped you investing more?" |

**Good answer sounds like:** a story with a date, an amount range, a person and a consequence. *"In March a big US customer paid two weeks late; we'd assumed it in week 3. The UK entity needed funding for payroll, so we drew CHF 5m on the RCF for ten days. Since then Lea marks every receipt above CHF 1m as 'confirmed' or 'unconfirmed'."*

**Evasive / generic answer sounds like:** *"We're quite conservative"*, *"We always escalate anything material"*. → Probe: *"What was the last thing you considered material?"* Also note non-answers on failure — some people won't discuss mistakes with a stranger. Offer a safer frame: *"Without details — what's the kind of thing that goes wrong?"*

### 4. Tools, workarounds and change

| Question | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|
| "What have you built internally?" | "Macros, templates, scripts, Power Query?" · "Who maintains it when you're away?" · "What happens if it breaks at month-end?" |
| "What would you never trust software to do automatically?" | "Why that one?" · "Has something gone wrong there before?" · "What would you need to see to trust it?" |
| "What would you happily delegate if you trusted the system?" | "What stops you today?" · "What does 'trusted' mean — who'd have to sign off?" |
| "What changed in your job in the last 2–3 years?" | "What got easier, what got harder?" · "What's new that you didn't do before?" |
| "When did you last look at a new tool or change a process?" | "What triggered it — the first thought?" · "Who was involved?" · "What happened in the end, and why?" |
| "If you were away for two weeks, what would break?" | "Who covers?" · "What do you leave them?" |

**Good answer sounds like:** named artefacts and specific trust boundaries. *"I built a Power Query that merges the eleven subsidiary files — it breaks whenever someone inserts a column. I'd never let anything release a payment to a new beneficiary without a person calling them. I'd happily let something prepare the first draft of the variance commentary."*

**Evasive / generic answer sounds like:** *"We're always open to innovation"*, *"AI is interesting"*, *"we'd need to see a business case"*. → Probe: *"When did you last change a tool, and how did that go?"* — a past switch story (trigger, search, decision, what got in the way) is far more informative than an opinion about future tools[](cite:moesta-switch).

### 5. Close

- "What should I have asked that I didn't?"
- "Who else should I talk to — who does the part before or after yours?" (ask for the **role**, then the person)
- "May I come back with a couple of follow-up questions once I've spoken to others?"

Keep your promise: send the anonymised summary you offered, and don't follow up with a pitch.

## By workflow

Use these when you already know which workflow the person performs. Each block: opener, probes, and what distinguishes a real answer from a rehearsed one. The workflow pages have the full step-by-step reference to compare answers against.

### Daily cash positioning — [workflow](wf:daily-cash-positioning)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through this morning's cash position, from the first screen."
- **Probes:** "Which bank data was missing or late?" · "What did you compare the balances against?" · "Which difference did you investigate — how?" · "What transfer did you make or propose, and who approved it?" · "What's the latest you can decide before the [cut-off](term:cut-off-time)?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers mention specific banks, entities, a time of day, an unexplained difference and a person contacted. "We have full visibility in the TMS" without any exception is a rehearsed answer — ask about the last day visibility failed.

### Cash forecasting — [workflow](wf:cash-forecasting)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through the last forecast you produced — start with the first input you opened."
- **Probes:** "Which entities sent a [submission](term:forecast-submission), in what format?" · "What did you pull yourself from the ERP?" · "Which lines did you override, and based on what?" · "How do you check last week's forecast against actuals — what did you learn last time?" · "Who reads it, and what did they decide from it?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers distinguish lines they trust from lines they don't, name the late entities, and describe a variance discussion. "Our accuracy is about 95%" without saying *of what* (which horizon, which lines, measured how) is a signal to probe, not a fact to record.

### Payments and payment approval — [processing](wf:payment-processing), [large payments](wf:large-payment-approval)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through the last payment run — from invoice to confirmation that it left the bank."
- **Probes:** "Who created the proposal?" · "Who approved it, in which system?" · "What was rejected or returned, and why?" · "What does an urgent out-of-cycle request look like?" · "What makes you refuse to approve a payment?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers name the channel (e-banking, [EBICS](term:ebics), SWIFT), a rejection reason and a person. **Do not ask** for approval thresholds, signatory names or call-back procedures — ask for the *sequence* instead ("then who looks at it?").

### FX exposure and hedging — [exposure](wf:fx-exposure-management), [hedging](wf:fx-hedging)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through the last hedge you executed — what triggered it?"
- **Probes:** "Where did the exposure number come from — who sent it?" · "How did you decide how much to hedge?" · "How many banks did you get quotes from, how?" · "What happens after the deal — confirmation, accounting?" · "The last time the currency moved sharply, what did you do that week?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers give a trigger (monthly cycle, a big order, a policy breach), a data source they partly distrust, and an execution method. "We hedge according to policy" is true but empty — ask what the policy *left to their judgment* last time.

### Liquidity planning and surplus investment — [planning](wf:liquidity-planning), [investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment)

- **Opener:** "What happened the last time you had more cash than you needed for a few months?" (or: "…the last time cash got tight?")
- **Probes:** "How did you decide what was really surplus?" · "Where did it go — which instrument, which bank?" · "What limited you — policy, limits, the CFO?" · "When did you last draw on a facility, and why then?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers mention a [minimum cash buffer](term:cash-buffer), a counterparty constraint and a person who decided. "We optimise yield within our policy" → ask for the last placement.

### Bank accounts and connectivity — [account management](wf:bank-account-management), [reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through the last bank account you opened or closed."
- **Probes:** "How long did it take, and what held it up?" · "What documents did the bank want?" · "How did it get connected to your systems?" · "How do you know today who can sign on which account?" · "Which bank statements arrived late or incomplete last month?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers include weeks of [KYC](term:kyc) back-and-forth, a board resolution or signatory list, and a connectivity step. "The bank handles that" means you're talking to someone who didn't do it.

### Debt and covenants — [facilities](wf:credit-facility-management), [covenants](wf:covenant-monitoring)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through the last compliance certificate you sent to the banks."
- **Probes:** "Who produced each number — treasury or accounting?" · "Which definitions needed judgment (adjusted EBITDA, net debt)?" · "When did headroom last worry anyone?" · "What happened in the last refinancing, from first thought to signing?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers name the spreadsheet, the accounting hand-off and a definition debate. Avoid asking about upcoming financings at listed companies.

### Fraud and controls — [fraud investigation](wf:fraud-investigation), [policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance)

- **Opener:** "Without naming anyone — tell me about the last suspicious payment request someone caught."
- **Probes:** "How was it spotted?" · "What happened in the next hour?" · "What changed afterwards?" · "What does the process look like when a supplier sends new bank details?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers describe detection by a person noticing something odd, and a process change afterwards. Accept vagueness about specifics — it's a control, not evasion.

### Cash pooling and intercompany funding — [funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary)

- **Opener:** "Tell me about the last time a subsidiary needed money from the group — who noticed first?"
- **Probes:** "How did the request arrive?" · "What did you check before sending money?" · "Loan, capital or pool — who decided?" · "What did tax require?" · "Which entities are outside the pool, and why?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers include the early-warning (or lack of it), a tax constraint and paperwork. "The pool takes care of it" → ask about the entities it doesn't cover.

### Month-end treasury reporting — [workflow](wf:month-end-reporting)

- **Opener:** "Walk me through last month-end — what did treasury have to deliver, to whom, by when?"
- **Probes:** "Which numbers did accounting need from you?" · "What did you reconcile, and what didn't match?" · "Who reads the treasury report, and what questions came back?"
- **Good vs evasive:** real answers name deal lists, revaluations, intercompany balances and a disagreement with accounting. "It's all automated from the TMS" → ask what they checked before sending.

## Adapting to seniority

| Person | Spend the time on | Walk-through prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst / operations | Mechanics, systems, exceptions, time | "Walk me through this morning." |
| Manager | Review, judgment, escalation, the recurring week | "Walk me through the last forecast you reviewed — what did you change?" |
| Head of Treasury | Organisation, decisions reserved, projects, failures | "Walk me through the last decision that reached you — how did it arrive and what did you need?" |
| CFO | What treasury output drives, risk appetite, budget, crises | "Tell me about the last time cash, FX or a bank surprised you." |
| Subsidiary finance | The other side of hand-offs | "Walk me through the last forecast you sent to group." |

See [Preparing by role](/interview/by-role) for what each role can and cannot answer, and [Questions to avoid](/interview/bad-questions) for the questions that sound reasonable but produce noise.
