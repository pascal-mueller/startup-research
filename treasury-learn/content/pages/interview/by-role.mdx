## How to use this page

Find the role of the person you are meeting. Each block tells you what that person **probably** owns and does — "probably" because titles are unreliable across company sizes and countries. Confirm in the first five minutes by asking *"What were the last three things you worked on?"* The blocks map questions to people by **proximity to the last instance of the work**, not by title — [why the mapping looks the way it does](why:doer-reviewer-decider).

Each block has the same parts:

- **Probably owns** — accountability, what lands on their desk.
- **Workflows they actually touch** — linked to the workflow pages. Distinguish doing, reviewing and approving.
- **Questions they can answer well** — where to spend your interview time.
- **What they probably can't answer** — don't waste time here; ask who can.
- **Terms to know first** — so you don't spend their time on definitions. More in [Vocabulary](/interview/vocabulary).
- **"What does this person know?"** — the panel from the role data (best source for / may know / probably won't know).

<Callout type="confusion" title="Two titles, one role id — and one title, several jobs">

This manual uses one role card for **Treasurer / Head of Treasury / Group Treasurer** and one for **Cash Manager / Liquidity Manager**, because the work overlaps heavily. Below they are split where the *interview* differs: a "Treasurer" at a mid-market company is usually hands-on; a Group Treasurer at a multinational manages managers. In US usage, "Treasurer" is often an officer title held by the head of treasury (sometimes also covering corporate finance); in Swiss/German companies "Treasurer" may simply mean "the person in treasury".

</Callout>

## CFO

<RoleLink id="cfo" /> — accountable for all finance; how much treasury they personally do depends almost entirely on company size (see [Treasury and its neighbours](/start/neighbours)).

**Probably owns:** financial strategy, funding decisions and bank relationships at the top, risk appetite (whether and how much to hedge), approval of the treasury policy (with the board), large or unusual payments as second signatory, covenant and lender communication, board reporting. At a startup or small SME ([Kleio](co:kleio)'s Sarah, [Alpine](co:alpine)'s Martin) the CFO also *does* treasury: checks balances, releases payments, calls the bank.

**Workflows they actually touch:**

| Size | Does | Approves / decides | Only informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Startup / small SME | [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) (Excel runway), [payment approval](wf:large-payment-approval), [bank accounts](wf:bank-account-management) | everything | — |
| Mid-market | little hands-on | [credit facilities](wf:credit-facility-management), hedging programme ([FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging)), [large payments](wf:large-payment-approval), [subsidiary funding](wf:funding-subsidiary) above a limit | daily positioning, bank recs |
| Multinational | none | refinancing, rating, M&A funding, policy; chairs the [treasury committee](term:treasury-committee) | most operational work |

**Questions they can answer well:**

- What decisions the forecast, the hedge programme or the liquidity report actually drive — and what they did the last time the numbers were wrong.
- What escalates to them, and why (the threshold is often a number *and* a feeling: "anything that could surprise the board").
- The last refinancing, bank dispute, currency shock or near-liquidity crisis, told as a story.
- Budget: what treasury has asked for, what was approved, what was refused.
- At small companies: everything operational — they are the treasurer.

**What they probably can't answer (above SME size):** which systems the analyst opens, how long the weekly forecast takes, how subsidiary data arrives, how exposures are calculated, which bank files fail. Ask for an introduction to whoever does it.

**Terms to know first:** [liquidity headroom](term:liquidity-headroom), [covenant](term:covenant), [RCF](term:rcf), [net debt](term:net-debt), [EBITDA](term:ebitda), [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), [treasury policy](term:treasury-policy), [credit rating](term:credit-rating) (larger groups).

## Treasurer (hands-on, often the whole treasury function)

<RoleLink id="head-of-treasury">Treasurer</RoleLink> — used here for the senior treasury person at a mid-market company, or a one-to-three-person team at an SME. Often titled "Treasurer", "Head of Treasury" or "Treasury Manager" interchangeably; reports to the CFO.

**Probably owns:** everything treasury — cash visibility, forecasting, bank relationships and accounts, FX hedging within policy, facilities and covenants, investments, treasury systems — with one analyst or none. Payments are often still run by AP / local finance, with the treasurer controlling bank channels and signatories.

**Workflows they actually touch:**

| Workflow | What they do |
|---|---|
| [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning), [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) | reviews the position daily (or builds it, with no analyst); owns the weekly forecast |
| [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging) | executes within policy — at this size they *are* the FX desk |
| [credit facilities](wf:credit-facility-management), [covenants](wf:covenant-monitoring) | runs with accounting; draws and repays |
| [bank account management](wf:bank-account-management), [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) | owns the bank inventory and relationships |
| [funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary), [surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment) | decides and executes |
| [large payment approval](wf:large-payment-approval) | approver (payments are often run by AP / local finance) |

**Questions they can answer well:** both the mechanics *and* the reasons. This is often the richest single interview in the mid-market: they can walk you through the Monday forecast, the hedge they booked last week, the bank that is slow to open an account, and why the CFO rejected a TMS last year.

**What they probably can't answer:** subsidiary-level detail (how Italy builds its receipts line), AP mechanics in each entity, the IT side of integrations; and — being spread thin — metrics they have never had time to measure (forecast accuracy by line, hours per task). Don't press for numbers they don't have; ask for the last instance instead.

**Terms to know first:** [cash position](term:cash-position), [short-term forecast](term:short-term-forecast), [forward](term:forward), [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), [RCF](term:rcf), [covenant](term:covenant), [cash pooling](term:cash-pooling), [EBICS](term:ebics), [TMS](term:tms).

## Head of Treasury / Group Treasurer (manages managers)

<RoleLink id="head-of-treasury" /> — at a multinational ([GlobalChem](co:globalchem)'s Anna, ~25 staff), runs a department with desks for cash & liquidity, financial risk, corporate finance, operations and systems. Reports to the CFO; sits on or runs the treasury committee.

**Probably owns:** treasury strategy, policy and organisation; funding strategy and bank group; rating-agency relationship (often with the CFO); liquidity and risk frameworks (limits, hedging policy); treasury technology roadmap and budget; board/CFO reporting; crisis leadership.

**Workflows they actually touch:** reviews and escalation point for most; decides in [liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning), [credit facilities](wf:credit-facility-management), [policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance), [acquisition integration](wf:acquisition-integration), [currency shock](wf:currency-shock), [liquidity crisis](wf:liquidity-crisis). Does little day-to-day execution.

**Questions they can answer well:** how the function is organised and why; what is centralised vs local; what the team spends time on (from their perspective); major projects (TMS replacement, payment factory, in-house bank) and what went wrong; what they report upward; which decisions they reserve for themselves; how they judge whether a team member's output is good.

**What they probably can't answer:** the current mechanics of any single process — they will describe the designed process, not the workarounds. Treat their description as a map, then ask for introductions: *"Who on your team would be best to walk me through the weekly forecast in detail?"*

**Terms to know first:** [in-house bank](term:in-house-bank), [payment factory](term:payment-factory), [POBO](term:pobo), [netting](term:netting), [counterparty limit](term:counterparty-limit), [credit rating](term:credit-rating), [commercial paper](term:commercial-paper), [front/middle/back office](term:front-middle-back-office), [treasury committee](term:treasury-committee).

## Treasury Manager

<RoleLink id="treasury-manager" /> — the most important interviewee for most founders. In a mid-market group ([Helvetic](co:helvetic)'s Daniel) this is the operational head of treasury with one analyst; in a multinational it is typically a manager running one area (cash, FX, operations) under a head.

**Probably owns:** the recurring treasury machine — daily liquidity decisions, the weekly [13-week forecast](wf:cash-forecasting) review, subsidiary funding, FX hedge execution within policy, bank relationships at working level, facility drawdowns, monthly treasury reporting, being the escalation point for the analyst.

**Workflows they actually touch:** reviews [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) and decides transfers; reviews and adjusts [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting); decides and executes [funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary), [surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment), [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging); approves in [payment processing](wf:payment-processing); prepares [month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) and [covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) drafts for the CFO.

**Questions they can answer well:**

- What the analyst prepares vs what they review — and exactly what they change when they review it.
- What makes them escalate to the Head of Treasury / CFO (amounts, policy breaches, anything irreversible).
- Judgment calls: which subsidiary numbers they override, when they hedge more or less than the model says, when to draw the RCF.
- The recurring week (the Monday roll-forward, submissions due Tuesday noon, the Wednesday review, month-end crunch) and what breaks it.
- Tools: what they have built in Excel and why the TMS/connectivity tool doesn't cover it.

**What they probably can't answer:** board-level strategy and budget rationale (partly), detailed subsidiary processes, IT architecture. In large groups, a treasury manager in one area won't know the others well.

**Terms to know first:** [cash position](term:cash-position), [value date](term:value-date), [cut-off time](term:cut-off-time), [variance analysis](term:variance-analysis), [intercompany loan](term:intercompany-loan), [forward](term:forward), [swap](term:swap), [drawdown](term:drawdown), [counterparty limit](term:counterparty-limit).

## Treasury Analyst

<RoleLink id="treasury-analyst" /> — the person who assembles the data ([Helvetic](co:helvetic)'s Lea). Early career or a few years in; often came from accounting, audit or a bank.

**Probably owns:** the **preparation** of recurring outputs — morning cash position, forecast consolidation, exposure reports, deal confirmations and reconciliations, bank-fee checks, TMS data maintenance, ad-hoc analyses for the manager. Rarely owns decisions.

**Workflows they actually touch:** builds [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) and [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting); prepares [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management) reports; supports [bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation) and [month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting); runs [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis) where it is done at all.

**Questions they can answer well:** step-by-step mechanics — the order in which they open screens, what they export, which macros they run, which entity is always late, how long each step takes, what they re-type, what they check before sending to the manager, what the manager sends back. **Best interviewee for "walk me through this morning".**

**What they probably can't answer:** why the policy says what it says, why a hedge was sized as it was, the economics of a funding decision, budgets and vendor selection (though they may have sat in demos). Don't ask them to speak for the department's priorities.

**Terms to know first:** [bank statement](term:bank-statement), [MT940](term:mt940) / [camt.053](term:camt-053) (the statements), [camt.054](term:camt-054) (item detail behind a booking), [value date](term:value-date), [cash position](term:cash-position), [forecast submission](term:forecast-submission), [variance analysis](term:variance-analysis), [reconciliation](term:reconciliation), [TMS](term:tms).

<RoleKnows id="treasury-analyst" />

<Callout type="field" title="Analysts may speak more freely — and less authoritatively">

Analysts are often generous with detail and time (field note), but they may not know what they are not allowed to share, and they are rarely the budget holder. Be especially careful not to collect anything sensitive, and don't mistake their frustrations for the department's priorities — check with the manager.

</Callout>

## Cash Manager

<RoleLink id="cash-manager" /> — a specialised role mainly in larger groups (e.g. Head of Cash & Liquidity, [GlobalChem](co:globalchem)'s Priya; or a cash manager within a regional treasury centre). In mid-market firms the same work is part of the treasury manager's job.

**Probably owns:** daily group liquidity: positioning across banks and entities, [cash pools](term:cash-pooling) and sweeps, the [in-house bank](term:in-house-bank) accounts, intercompany funding, short-term investments and borrowings, often the group cash forecasting process.

**Workflows they actually touch:** owns [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning), [funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary), [surplus cash investment](wf:surplus-cash-investment); often owns [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) and contributes to [liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning); first responder in a [liquidity crisis](wf:liquidity-crisis).

**Questions they can answer well:** pool mechanics and their exceptions (entities outside the pool, restricted currencies); what the morning looks like at scale (what's automated, what isn't); which subsidiaries' forecasts are unreliable and why; counterparty-limit juggling; the in-house bank's intercompany positions.

**What they probably can't answer:** FX hedging decisions (a separate desk in large groups), debt capital markets, the AP-level mechanics of payments.

**Terms to know first:** [cash concentration](term:cash-concentration), [physical pooling](term:physical-pooling), [notional pooling](term:notional-pooling), [sweeping](term:sweeping), [in-house bank](term:in-house-bank), [trapped cash](term:trapped-cash), [money market fund](term:money-market-fund), [counterparty limit](term:counterparty-limit).

## Liquidity Manager

Same <RoleLink id="cash-manager">role family</RoleLink> as the cash manager; the title tends to signal a **forward-looking** emphasis. Where both titles exist in one group, the cash manager tends to run today (positioning, pooling, execution) and the liquidity manager the coming weeks and months (forecasting, [liquidity headroom](term:liquidity-headroom), stress scenarios, facility usage, minimum-liquidity policy). In many companies one person does both. Confirm with *"What does your week look like — how much of it is about today's cash vs the next three months?"*

**Workflows they actually touch:** owns [liquidity planning](wf:liquidity-planning) and often [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting); contributes to [credit facility management](wf:credit-facility-management) (headroom), [covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) (liquidity covenants), [liquidity crisis](wf:liquidity-crisis) scenarios.

**Questions they can answer well:** how the forecast horizon is structured (13 weeks, 12 months, beyond); how scenarios and stress tests are built and who asks for them; the minimum-liquidity target and where it came from; what happens when headroom falls below it.

**What they probably can't answer:** payment mechanics, bank connectivity detail, FX execution.

**Terms to know first:** [liquidity headroom](term:liquidity-headroom), [short-term forecast](term:short-term-forecast), [medium-term forecast](term:medium-term-forecast), [direct method](term:direct-method) vs [indirect method](term:indirect-method), [cash flow at risk](term:cash-flow-at-risk), [committed facility](term:committed-facility), [cash buffer](term:cash-buffer).

## FX / Financial Risk Manager

<RoleLink id="fx-risk-manager" /> — a dedicated role mainly in larger groups ([GlobalChem](co:globalchem)'s Jonas, covering FX and interest rates). In mid-market firms FX is part of the treasury manager's job; at SMEs the CFO books the occasional forward.

**Probably owns:** exposure identification and collection from subsidiaries, hedge requirement calculation against policy, hedge execution (often on a multi-bank trading platform), [hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting) documentation with accounting, risk reporting, interest-rate hedging.

**Workflows they actually touch:** owns [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management) and [FX hedging](wf:fx-hedging); central in a [currency shock](wf:currency-shock); contributes to [policy compliance](wf:policy-compliance) and [month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting).

**Questions they can answer well:** where exposure data comes from and how much they trust it; how the hedge ratio is set and when they deviate; instrument choice (forwards vs [options](term:option) vs [swaps](term:swap)); how they get quotes and execute; the last big currency move — what they did that day; the friction with accounting at month-end.

**What they probably can't answer:** daily liquidity, payments, bank account administration, how subsidiaries forecast their sales (they receive the numbers).

**Terms to know first:** [transaction exposure](term:transaction-exposure), [translation exposure](term:translation-exposure), [economic exposure](term:economic-exposure), [forward](term:forward), [forward points](term:forward-points), [hedge ratio](term:hedge-ratio), [natural hedge](term:natural-hedge), [hedge accounting](term:hedge-accounting), [functional currency](term:functional-currency), [ISDA](term:isda).

## Treasury Operations (back office)

<RoleLink id="treasury-operations" /> — in large groups a team ([GlobalChem](co:globalchem)'s Marta, in a shared-service centre); in mid-market firms often the analyst plus accounting. The "back office" of the [front/middle/back office](term:front-middle-back-office) split.

**Probably owns:** deal confirmations and settlement, treasury payments and their monitoring, [static data](term:static-data) (bank accounts, counterparties, settlement instructions), bank account administration (mandates, [KYC](term:kyc) paperwork, signatory updates), treasury bank reconciliation, accounting entries for treasury deals, and in some groups the payment factory.

**Workflows they actually touch:** [payment processing](wf:payment-processing), [bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation), [bank account management](wf:bank-account-management), [bank fee analysis](wf:bank-fee-analysis), first responder in [fraud investigation](wf:fraud-investigation), supports [month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting).

**Questions they can answer well:** exception handling — rejected payments, unmatched confirmations, missing statements, returned funds; the real duration of opening an account; what the four-eyes controls look like in practice; what breaks at month-end; the volume of manual matching.

**What they probably can't answer:** why decisions were made (why this hedge, why this bank), forecasting, policy design.

**Terms to know first:** [settlement](term:settlement), [SWIFT](term:swift), [pain.001](term:pain-001) / [pain.002](term:pain-002), [ISO 20022](term:iso-20022), [signatory](term:signatory), [payment mandate](term:payment-mandate), [KYC](term:kyc), [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle), [segregation of duties](term:segregation-of-duties).

## Subsidiary finance leader

<RoleLink id="subsidiary-finance" /> — the local finance manager or subsidiary CFO ([Helvetic](co:helvetic)'s Giulia in Italy, Jens in Germany, Wei in Suzhou; at [Alpine](co:alpine), the managing director plus a bookkeeper). Reports locally to the subsidiary MD and functionally to group finance.

**Probably owns:** local accounting and close, local AP/AR and payments, local bank accounts and relationships (sometimes), the [forecast submission](term:forecast-submission) and exposure reporting to group, local tax and statutory matters.

**Workflows they actually touch:** contributes data to [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting) and [FX exposure management](wf:fx-exposure-management); requests funding in [funding a subsidiary](wf:funding-subsidiary); prepares and often approves local [payment processing](wf:payment-processing); involved in [bank account management](wf:bank-account-management) locally and in [acquisition integration](wf:acquisition-integration) when their entity is acquired.

**Questions they can answer well:** how long group reporting takes and what is re-typed; where their numbers really come from (AR ageing, sales team, gut feel); what they think group does with their forecast; local banking constraints; the experience of asking group for money; what group requests they find pointless.

**What they probably can't answer:** group-level liquidity, hedging decisions, pooling design, why group asks for what it asks.

**Terms to know first:** [forecast submission](term:forecast-submission), [intercompany loan](term:intercompany-loan), [cash pooling](term:cash-pooling), [transfer pricing](term:transfer-pricing), [functional currency](term:functional-currency), [trapped cash](term:trapped-cash) (for restricted-currency countries).

<RoleKnows id="subsidiary-finance" />

<Callout type="interview" title="Interview both ends of every hand-off">

Most treasury workflows cross the line between group treasury and local finance. After a treasury manager says "Italy's forecasts are always late", ask Giulia what the template asks for, when she gets it, what she has to chase from sales, and whether anyone tells her when the forecast was wrong. You will usually hear two different, both accurate, stories. See the [hand-offs](/org/handoffs) page.

</Callout>

## Head of Accounting / Financial Controller

<RoleLink id="head-of-accounting" /> — owns the accounting close, the ledger and the finance team's non-treasury half. At SME size this person often runs the whole payment chain and the cash sheet too ([Alpine](co:alpine)'s Petra runs cash, payments and bank recs), which makes them one of the most likely interviewees at startup/SME size and one of the richest. At mid-market and above they are the counterpart of treasury, not treasury.

**Probably owns:** the close and consolidation, GL, AP/AR, bank reconciliations, payroll, fixed assets, statutory reporting, the audit relationship — and at SME size payments, bank accounts and the daily cash sheet.

**Workflows they actually touch:** at SME size does [payment processing](wf:payment-processing), [bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation) and the [daily cash positioning](wf:daily-cash-positioning) cash sheet, and supports [cash forecasting](wf:cash-forecasting); at larger sizes owns the accounting side of [month-end reporting](wf:month-end-reporting) and the numbers [covenant monitoring](wf:covenant-monitoring) is tested on.

**Questions they can answer well:** the payment chain end to end (invoice → proposal → approval → bank → reconciliation), vendor master data and what really happens when a supplier's bank details change, unmatched bank items and how they get cleared, what treasury asks of accounting and where the friction is, month-end sequencing.

**What they probably can't answer:** hedging decisions, group liquidity strategy, treasury policy design — except at SME size, where they may do all of it.

**Terms to know first:** [payment run](term:payment-run), [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle), [segregation of duties](term:segregation-of-duties), [vendor master data](term:vendor-master-data), [reconciliation](term:reconciliation), [bank statement](term:bank-statement), [direct method](term:direct-method).

## Accounts payable

<RoleLink id="accounts-payable" /> — the person who processes supplier invoices and prepares the payment runs (and, with credit control, chases customer receipts). Often reports to the head of accounting. Ranked among the best interviewees for payments and fraud topics — they see every fake invoice and bank-detail-change attempt first.

**Probably owns:** invoice capture and matching, the ERP payment proposal and payment run, supplier master data including bank-detail changes, payment investigations ("the supplier says they weren't paid").

**Workflows they actually touch:** prepares [payment processing](wf:payment-processing); supports [bank reconciliation](wf:bank-reconciliation) (unmatched supplier items); first responder when a fake invoice or changed bank details surface ([fraud investigation](wf:fraud-investigation)).

**Questions they can answer well:** the weekly payment rhythm and its cut-offs, exception handling (rejected, returned, urgent), what happens when a supplier's bank details change — the real process versus the written one — and how many payments a run contains.

**What they probably can't answer:** treasury policy, funding, FX, anything above the payment chain.

**Terms to know first:** [payment run](term:payment-run), [pain.001](term:pain-001), [vendor master data](term:vendor-master-data), [SEPA](term:sepa), [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle), [cut-off time](term:cut-off-time).
