## How much vocabulary is enough

You don't need to speak like a treasurer. You need to **not interrupt the story** to ask what a word means, and to **recognise when something important was said**. The lists below are the terms that typically come up unprompted in a walk-through of each topic. Hover a term for the definition; click for the full glossary entry with examples.

If a term you don't know comes up, note it and ask at a natural pause — *"You mentioned value date — how does that affect what you did there?"* is fine and often yields more detail. What costs credibility is not knowing the core terms of the topic you asked to talk about.

## Always (every treasury interview)

[cash position](term:cash-position) · [liquidity](term:liquidity) · [value date](term:value-date) · [bank statement](term:bank-statement) · [short-term forecast](term:short-term-forecast) · [treasury policy](term:treasury-policy) · [ERP](term:erp) · [TMS](term:tms) · [four-eyes principle](term:four-eyes-principle) · [segregation of duties](term:segregation-of-duties) · [intercompany loan](term:intercompany-loan) · [FP&A](term:fpa) · [front/middle/back office](term:front-middle-back-office)

Plus the organisational distinctions in [Treasury and its neighbours](/start/neighbours): treasury manages cash by value date; accounting records by posting date; FP&A forecasts profit, treasury forecasts cash. "Controlling" in DACH is not "the controller" in the US.

## Phrases that signal naivety

These are common founder slips. Each one tells an experienced treasurer that you are new — which is fine if you say so, but costly if you pretend otherwise.

| You say | What they hear | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Your cash balance" (meaning the ERP / accounting balance) | You don't know book vs bank balance. | "The bank balance by value date" or "the cash position" — see [cash position](term:cash-position). |
| "Treasury does the books / the budget" | Confusing treasury with accounting / FP&A. | "Where does your work stop and accounting's begin?" |
| "Real-time" as a goal in itself | You haven't asked what decision needs it. Many decisions are made once a day before [cut-off](term:cut-off-time). | "When do you need a number that you don't have yet?" |
| "Predict the cash flow with AI" | Treating forecasting as a pure prediction problem; much of it is collecting information people haven't shared yet. | "Which inputs are late or unreliable, and why?" |
| "Hedging to make money on FX" / "betting on the currency" | Corporate hedging reduces risk; speculation is typically prohibited by policy. | "What's the hedging programme meant to protect?" |
| "Just connect to the bank API" | Underestimates bank connectivity (EBICS, SWIFT, host-to-host, formats, KYC, bank projects). | "How does bank data get into your systems today, and how long did the last bank take to onboard?" |
| "Automate payments end-to-end" | Human approvals in payments are deliberate controls, not inefficiencies. | "Which payment steps are deliberately manual?" |
| "The treasurer" (at a 70-person company) | There probably isn't one — the CFO does it. | "Who looks after cash and the banks here?" |
| "A multinational like you" (to a CHF 650m group) | Mid-market groups do not have in-house banks, payment factories or FX desks — mis-sizing their world. | Check size first: [Treasury by company size](/size). |
| "Liquidity" meaning profitability or solvency | Basic concept confusion. | [liquidity](term:liquidity) = ability to pay on time. |

<Callout type="confusion" title="Words that mean different things to different people">

- **Forecast** — FP&A means P&L forecast; treasury means cash forecast. Ask which.
- **Controller / Controlling** — in the US, head of accounting; in DACH, management accounting and planning.
- **Pooling** — may mean physical sweeping, notional pooling, or informally "we move cash to the parent".
- **Hedge** — to treasury, a derivative (usually a forward); to operations, "we priced in a buffer".
- **Treasury Manager** — the whole treasury function at a mid-market company; one desk at a multinational.
- **Bank reconciliation** — owned by accounting in many companies, by treasury operations in others.

</Callout>
